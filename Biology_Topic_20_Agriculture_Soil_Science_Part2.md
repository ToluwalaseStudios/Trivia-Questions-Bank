Q: An acidic soil can be improved upon by
A) Sedimentation
B) Leaching
C) Flocculation
D) Watering
ANSWER: (see explanation)
EXPLANATION: An acidic soil is specifically improved by liming, which raises soil pH and neutralizes acidity, a process related to flocculation (in the sense that lime application also causes clay particle flocculation, improving soil structure alongside the acidity correction) — of the listed options, this liming-related process is the most relevant improvement method, rather than sedimentation, leaching (which actually contributes to soil acidity), or simple watering.

Q: Fertilizers are lost from the soil through
A) sheet erosion and evaporation
B) leaching, underground seepages and run-off
C) sheet erosion, gully erosion and wind erosion
D) wind erosion and heavy rainfall
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Fertilizer nutrients are most directly lost from the soil through leaching (downward washing by percolating water), underground seepage, and surface run-off, all of which physically remove dissolved nutrients from the root zone.

Q: Soil with the finest particles is called
A) silt
B) clay
C) sand
D) gravel
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Clay particles are, by definition, the smallest (finest) of the standard soil particle size classifications, smaller than silt, sand, or gravel.

Q: Soil fertility can be conserved and renewed by
A) yearly mono cropping
B) crop rotation and cover crops
C) bush burning to remove unwanted debris
D) avoiding artificial manures
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Practicing crop rotation (alternating different crops to balance nutrient use) together with growing cover crops (which protect and can enrich the soil) are genuine, effective methods for conserving and renewing soil fertility — unlike continuous mono-cropping (which depletes specific nutrients) or bush burning (which destroys organic matter).

Q: The water-retention capacity of a soil indicates its
A) fertility
B) capillarity
C) aeration level
D) pH level
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A soil's water-retention capacity is directly and specifically an indicator of its capillarity — the tendency of the soil's pore structure to draw up and hold water against gravity.

Q: In a soil sample, the relative amounts of the different soil particles can best be determined by the process of
A) filtration
B) centrifugation
C) precipitation
D) sedimentation
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, sedimentation (shaking a soil-water mixture and allowing the particles to settle out in layers by size/density) is the standard method for determining the relative proportions of different soil particle types.

Q: Soil micro-organisms are beneficial because of their involvement in
A) photosynthesis
B) translocation
C) cycling of nutrients
D) respiration using soil air
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Soil micro-organisms play a crucial beneficial role by decomposing organic matter and recycling nutrients back into forms usable by plants, a process fundamental to maintaining soil fertility.

Q: A soil consisting of alumina and iron (II) oxide is known as
A) loamy soil
B) clayey soil
C) laterite
D) podzol
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Laterite soil is specifically characterized by its high content of aluminium oxide (alumina) and iron oxide, typically formed through intense weathering and leaching in tropical climates, giving it its distinctive reddish colour.

Q: The addition of lime to clay soil serves to
A) aid water retention
B) close up the texture
C) prevent water-logging
D) improve capillary action
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, lime added to clay soil causes flocculation, aggregating fine clay particles into larger clumps, which improves drainage and specifically helps prevent water-logging (the opposite of increasing water retention or capillary action).

Q: The excessive use of agro-chemicals could lead to the pollution of
A) the lithosphere
B) the atmosphere
C) fresh water
D) space
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Excess agro-chemicals (such as fertilizers and pesticides) commonly wash off from fields via run-off and leaching, ultimately contaminating nearby freshwater sources (rivers, lakes, groundwater), representing a major environmental pollution pathway.

Q: The stunted growth of a leguminous plant suffering from nitrogen deficiency may be corrected by inoculating the soil with
A) Denitrifying bacteria
B) Saprophytic bacteria
C) Rhizobium
D) Nitrosomonas
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Rhizobium is specifically the nitrogen-fixing bacterium that forms a symbiotic relationship with leguminous plant root nodules, converting atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form — inoculating deficient soil with Rhizobium directly addresses a nitrogen deficiency in legumes.

Q: Soil fertility can best be conserved and renewed by the activities of
A) Earthworms
B) Man
C) Rodents
D) Microbes
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Soil microbes (bacteria, fungi, and other micro-organisms) play the most fundamental role in conserving and renewing soil fertility, through their essential activities in decomposing organic matter and recycling nutrients.

Q: The soil type that will be most difficult to plough in a wet season is one that is
A) loamy
B) clayey
C) sandy
D) silty
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Clay soil, due to its fine particles and high water retention, becomes especially heavy, sticky, and waterlogged when wet — making it notably difficult to plough in wet conditions compared to the more workable loamy, sandy, or silty soils.

Q: A farm practice that results in the loss of soil fertility is
A) mixed farming
B) bush fallowing
C) shifting cultivation
D) continuous cropping
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Continuous cropping (repeatedly growing crops on the same land without any rest or nutrient-replenishing rotation) steadily depletes the soil's nutrients, leading to a loss of fertility over time — unlike mixed farming, bush fallowing, or shifting cultivation, which all incorporate natural fertility-restoring practices.

Q: Which of the following is used to test for the presence of lime in a soil sample?
A) H2SO4(aq)
B) NaOH(aq)
C) HCl(aq)
D) HNO3(aq)
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) is specifically preferred for testing lime (calcium carbonate) presence in soil, since it reacts with any lime present to produce visible effervescence (carbon dioxide gas bubbles) — sulphuric acid is less suitable since it can form an insoluble calcium sulphate precipitate that masks the reaction.

Q: The importance of practicing crop rotation in agriculture is to
A) maintain soil fertility
B) improve the nutritional value of crops
C) control soil erosion
D) ensure the growth of crops
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, the core agricultural importance of crop rotation lies in maintaining and conserving soil fertility, by alternating crops with differing nutrient demands.

Q: The soil type that contains nutrients which are not readily available for plants is
A) alluvial
B) clayey
C) sandy
D) loamy
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, clayey soil binds its water and mineral nutrients very tightly to its fine particles, making these nutrients less readily available for plant uptake compared to more open-structured alluvial, sandy, or loamy soils.

Q: The increasing order of the particle size in the following soil types is
A) clay - sand - silt - gravel
B) silt - sand - clay - gravel
C) clay - silt - sand - gravel
D) silt - clay - sand - gravel

Q: A crucible of 5gm weighed 10gm after filling with fresh soil. It is then heated in an oven at 100°C for 1 hour. After cooling in a desiccator, the weight was 8gm. The percentage of water in the soil is
A) 80%
B) 60%
C) 20%
D) 40%
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The fresh soil sample itself weighed (10g − 5g crucible) = 5g. After oven-drying, the soil+crucible weighed 8g, meaning the dried soil weighed (8g − 5g crucible) = 3g. The water lost was (5g − 3g) = 2g, giving a water percentage of (2/5) × 100% = 40%.

Q: In an experiment to determine the percentage of air in a soil sample, the following readings were recorded: Volume of water in a measuring cylinder = 500cm³; Volume of soil added to water = 350cm³; Volume of water and soil after stirring = 800cm³. The percentage of air in the soil sample is
A) 6.25%
B) 10.36%
C) 14.28%
D) 43.28%
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The expected combined volume (if the soil contained no air pockets) would be 500cm³ + 350cm³ = 850cm³; since the actual measured volume after stirring was only 800cm³, this "missing" 50cm³ represents the volume of air originally trapped within the soil particles that escaped upon stirring. As a percentage of the original soil volume: (50/350) × 100% ≈ 14.28%.

Q: The loss of soil through erosion can be reduced by
A) crop rotation
B) manuring
C) irrigation
D) watering
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Crop rotation, particularly when it incorporates cover crops or deep-rooted plants at intervals, helps protect soil structure and reduce erosion, unlike manuring, irrigation, or watering, which do not directly address erosion prevention.

Q: In an experiment to determine the percentage of humus and water in a soil sample, the following results were obtained: Weight of the evaporating basin alone = 80.5g; Weight of basin and soil = 101.5g; Weight after drying the soil in the oven = 99.0g; Weight of basin and roasted soil = 95.5g. [Determine the percentage of humus in the soil.]
ANSWER: (see explanation)
EXPLANATION: The fresh soil sample weighed (101.5g − 80.5g) = 21.0g. After oven-drying (removing water), the soil+basin weighed 99.0g, so water lost was (101.5g − 99.0g) = 2.5g, giving % water = (2.5/21.0) × 100% ≈ 11.9%. After further roasting (removing humus from the already-dried soil), the soil+basin weighed 95.5g, so humus lost was (99.0g − 95.5g) = 3.5g, giving % humus = (3.5/21.0) × 100% ≈ 16.7% — this stepwise drying-then-roasting method is the standard way to separately determine both water and humus content in a single soil sample.

Q: An indigenous method of renewing and maintaining soil fertility is by
A) clearing farms by burning
B) planting one crop type yearly
C) adding inorganic fertilizers yearly
D) crop rotation and shifting cultivation
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Crop rotation and shifting cultivation are both traditional, indigenous agricultural methods long used to renew and maintain soil fertility naturally, unlike bush burning (which destroys organic matter), continuous mono-cropping, or synthetic inorganic fertilizer application (a more modern approach).

Q: The soil with the highest water-retaining capacity is
A) clayey soil
B) Stoney soil
C) sandy soil
D) loamy soil
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As established repeatedly throughout this topic, clayey soil, due to its very fine particle size and resulting high capillarity, has the highest water-retaining capacity among standard soil types.
