# TOPIC 12: SUPPORTING TISSUES, MUSCULOSKELETAL SYSTEM, MOVEMENT
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: One of the functions of the xylem is
A) strengthening the stem
B) manufacturing food
C) reducing loss of water
D) conducting manufactured food
E) storing unused sugar
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Xylem tissue, being made of thickened, often lignified dead cells, gives significant mechanical strength and support to the stem, in addition to its main role of conducting water — it does not manufacture food, conduct manufactured food (that's phloem's job), reduce water loss, or store sugar.

Q: The cilia in paramecium are used for
A) respiring
B) locomotion
C) protection
D) regulating food intake
E) excretion
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Paramecium's numerous cilia beat rhythmically to propel the organism through water, making locomotion their primary function, alongside a secondary role in directing food particles toward the oral groove (not a primary respiratory, protective, or excretory role).

Q: Which of these skeletons is most appropriate to the cockroach?
A) Hydrostatic skeleton
B) Exoskeleton
C) Endoskeleton
D) cartilaginous skeleton
E) Bony skeleton
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The cockroach, an arthropod, possesses a hard, chitinous exoskeleton covering the outside of its body, which provides both support and protection, unlike vertebrate endoskeletons or the fluid-based hydrostatic skeletons of soft-bodied invertebrates.

Q: Which of these is not associated with the movement of toad, reptile or birds?
A) Hopping
B) Bopping
C) Flapping
D) Gliding
E) Pecking
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Pecking is a feeding-related action, not a form of locomotion — hopping (toads), flapping/gliding (birds), and general movement patterns of reptiles are all genuine modes of movement, unlike pecking.

Q: Which of these tissues serves the function of support and water conduction?
A) Parenchyma
B) Collenchyma
C) Sclerenchyma
D) Xylem
E) Phloem
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Xylem uniquely combines both functions — its lignified, hollow dead cells provide mechanical support to the plant while simultaneously conducting water and dissolved minerals upward, a dual role not matched by parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma (support only), or phloem (food conduction only).

Q: Which of the structures listed below is peculiar to the Axis vertebra?
A) Odontoid process
B) Anterior zygapophysis
C) Posterior zygapophysis
D) lateral spine
E) Centrum
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The odontoid process (also called the dens) is a distinctive, forward-projecting peg-like structure unique to the axis (the second cervical vertebra), around which the atlas (first cervical vertebra) rotates — the other structures listed are more general features found across multiple vertebrae types.

Q: The joint between the atlas and axis vertebrae allows for
A) rotary movements only
B) up and down or nodding movements only
C) rotatory and nodding movements

ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The atlas-axis joint is specifically a pivot joint, allowing the head to rotate from side to side (as in shaking the head "no") — nodding movements (up and down) instead occur at the separate joint between the skull and the atlas.

Q: In animals, cilia, flagella, pseudopodia, and chaetae can be used for
A) reproduction
B) fighting enemies

E) location sensation
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Cilia, flagella, pseudopodia, and chaetae are all structures that different organisms use, in their own specific ways, to achieve movement (locomotion) — swimming, crawling, or gripping the substrate to move forward.

Q: The tissue which connects a muscle to a bone is a
A) cartilage
B) Muscle fibre
C) Tendon

ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A tendon is specifically the tough connective tissue that attaches a muscle to a bone, enabling the muscle's contraction to pull and move that bone — distinct from a ligament, which connects bone to bone.

Q: The thoracic vertebrae of a mammal is characterized by the
A) vertebrarterial canal
B) prominent neural arch
C) prominent transverse process
D) prominent neural spine
E) prominent centrum
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Thoracic vertebrae are specifically distinguished by their long, prominent neural spines, which project upward and backward, providing large attachment surfaces for back muscles and ligaments, unlike the vertebrarterial canal (a cervical vertebra feature).

Q: Starting from the skull end, the vertebrae are arranged in the following order:
A) axis, atlas, cervical, thoracic and lumbar
B) atlas, cervical, axis, thoracic and lumbar
C) atlas, axis, thoracic, cervical and lumbar
D) atlas, axis, cervical, thoracic and lumbar
E) atlas, thoracic, cervical, axis and lumbar
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Starting closest to the skull, the correct anatomical sequence of the vertebral column runs: atlas (1st cervical vertebra) → axis (2nd cervical vertebra) → the remaining cervical vertebrae → thoracic vertebrae → lumbar vertebrae, matching option D.

Q: Which of the following types of vertebrae occur in equal numbers in the rabbit, rat and man?
A) Caudal
B) Thoracic
C) Lumbar
D) Cervical
E) Sacral
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: All mammals, regardless of the length or shape of their neck, possess exactly seven cervical vertebrae — a remarkably consistent feature shared equally by rabbits, rats, and man, unlike the variable numbers of thoracic, lumbar, sacral, or caudal (tail) vertebrae between species.

Q: The axial skeleton of a mammal does not include the bones of the
A) skull
B) tail
C) limbs
D) back
E) neck
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The axial skeleton comprises the skull, vertebral column (including neck, back, and tail regions), and rib cage/sternum — the limb bones (and their girdles) belong instead to the separate appendicular skeleton.

Q: The appendicular skeleton is made up of the
A) limbs
B) skull and phalanges
C) phalanges
D) ulna and radius
E) girdles and limbs
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The appendicular skeleton consists specifically of the limb bones together with the girdles (pectoral and pelvic) that attach those limbs to the axial skeleton — making "girdles and limbs" the most complete and accurate description among the options.

Q: Two main distinguishing features of the cervical vertebra are the presence of
A) short neural spine and vertebrarterial canal
B) prezygapophysis and cervical ribs
C) large centrum and cervical ribs
D) vertebrarterial canal and large centrum
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Cervical vertebrae are specifically distinguished by having a short neural spine along with the vertebrarterial canal (a small opening in the transverse process through which the vertebral artery passes) — a combination of features unique to this region of the vertebral column.

Q: Sclerenchyma cells are lignified to
A) strengthen and support the plant
B) transport synthesized food
C) conduct water and salt
D) protect the plant from injury
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The deposition of lignin in sclerenchyma cell walls makes them tough and rigid specifically to provide mechanical strength and support to the plant, rather than serving a transport or conduction role (those are functions of xylem and phloem instead).

Q: Parenchyma cells serve as supporting tissue when they
A) contain chloroplasts
B) have crystals
C) become flaccid
D) become turgid
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: When parenchyma cells absorb water and become turgid (firm, swollen with internal pressure against their cell walls), they collectively provide a form of structural support to soft plant tissues — flaccid (water-deficient) parenchyma cells provide no such support.

Q: During the bending of the arm, the biceps muscle

Q: Which of the following is not a function of the mammalian skeleton?
A) Protection
B) Respiration
C) Transportation
D) Support
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: While the ribs do assist the mechanics of breathing, "respiration" itself (gas exchange) is not a direct function performed by the skeleton — protection (of vital organs), transportation (via limb movement), and support (holding up the body) are the skeleton's genuine core functions.

Q: The organism that has a hydrostatic skeleton is
A) Tilapia
B) Hydra
C) Mosquito larva
D) Earthworm
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The earthworm relies on a hydrostatic skeleton — fluid-filled body compartments under muscular control, which provide support and enable movement by peristaltic contractions — unlike Tilapia (bony endoskeleton), Hydra (no true skeleton needed given its small size/simplicity), or mosquito larvae (which may have other support structures).

Q: The chitin in the exoskeleton of many arthropods is strengthened by
A) calcium compounds
B) organic salts
C) lipids
D) proteins
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In many arthropods, the chitin of the exoskeleton is further hardened and strengthened through the deposition of calcium compounds (such as calcium carbonate), a process called calcification, particularly notable in crustaceans.
