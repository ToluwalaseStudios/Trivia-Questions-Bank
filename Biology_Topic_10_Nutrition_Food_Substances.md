# TOPIC 10: NUTRITION, FOOD SUBSTANCES
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2. The process represented by the above equation is
A) protein synthesis
B) respiration
C) Photosynthesis
D) transpiration
E) translocation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This equation — carbon dioxide and water combining, using light energy, to form glucose and oxygen — is the overall summary equation of photosynthesis, the process by which green plants manufacture their own food.

Q: The oxygen given off during the process is derived from
A) sunlight
B) chlorophyll
C) carbon dioxide
D) atmosphere
E) water
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: During the light-dependent stage of photosynthesis, water molecules are split apart (photolysis) into hydrogen and oxygen; this released oxygen is the gas given off as a by-product, not the oxygen from carbon dioxide as might be assumed.

Q: Which of these elements is required by plants in small quantities only?
A) Boron
B) Nitrogen
C) Phosphorus
D) Magnesium
E) Potassium
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Boron is a micronutrient (trace element), needed by plants only in very minute amounts for processes like pigment and enzyme formation. Nitrogen, phosphorus, magnesium, and potassium, by contrast, are all macronutrients, required in much larger quantities.

Q: Which of these is a direct photosynthetic product?
A) Glucose
B) Starch
C) Protein
D) Fats
E) Latex
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Glucose is the direct, immediate sugar product formed at the end of the photosynthetic pathway; starch, fats, proteins, and other substances are all subsequently synthesized or converted from this initial glucose product.

Q: An organism which lives on the remains of a dead plant is
A) an endo parasite
B) a saprophyte
C) a commensal
D) a symbiont
E) an ecto parasite
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: An organism that feeds on dead and decaying organic matter (such as a dead plant) is, by definition, a saprophyte — distinct from parasites (which feed on a living host) or commensals/symbionts (which involve living partnerships).

Q: Which of the substances listed below is a trace element for plants?
A) Potassium
B) Sodium
C) Copper
D) Phosphate
E) Nitrate
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Copper is a micronutrient (trace element) required by plants only in tiny amounts, unlike potassium and phosphate/nitrate (forms of phosphorus and nitrogen), which are needed in much larger, macronutrient quantities. (Sodium is not a standard required plant nutrient at all.)

Q: A sugar solution was boiled with Fehling's solutions A and B and the colour remained blue. The sugar tested was
A) glucose
B) maltose
C) fructose
D) sucrose
E) lactose
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Sucrose is a non-reducing sugar, so it does not react with Fehling's solution to produce the characteristic colour change from blue to brick-red — the solution stays blue. Glucose, maltose, fructose, and lactose are all reducing sugars and would have given a positive (colour-changing) result.

Q: Alkaline pyrogallol was used in an experiment. That experiment must have been connected with
A) excretion
B) germination
C) transpiration
D) digestion
E) photosynthesis
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Alkaline pyrogallol has a strong affinity for absorbing oxygen gas — a property specifically exploited in photosynthesis experiments to demonstrate that oxygen (a by-product of photosynthesis) has been produced and needs to be absorbed/measured.

Q: The element nitrogen is utilized in
A) formation of ATP
B) formation of glucose
C) formation of amino acids
D) Photosynthesis
E) none of the above
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Nitrogen is a key building-block element specifically required for forming amino acids (and consequently proteins), as well as nitrogenous bases and chlorophyll — but it is not a component of glucose or ATP's core structure, nor a direct requirement of the photosynthetic light/dark reactions themselves.

Q: Magnesium is utilized in the formation of
A) ATP
B) glucose
C) amino acids
D) chlorophyll
E) fats
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Magnesium is the central metal atom at the core of the chlorophyll molecule, making it essential specifically for chlorophyll formation, unlike ATP, glucose, amino acids, or fats, whose core structures do not require magnesium.

Q: In testing for glucose, the necessary reagent and the condition under which the reagent reacts best are
A) Fehling's reagent, in the cold
B) Million's reagent, boiled
C) Fehling's reagent in acid medium, heated
D) Iodine solution, boiled
E) Fehling's reagent in neutral or alkaline medium, boiled
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Fehling's test for reducing sugars like glucose requires the solution to be heated (boiled) in a neutral or alkaline medium in order for the characteristic colour change (blue to brick-red) to occur — testing in an acidic medium or without heating would not give a reliable positive result.

Q: A green plant growing in a compost pit is feeding
A) holozoically
B) parasitically
C) saprophytically
D) holophytically
E) in none of the above ways
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Because it is a green plant, it still manufactures its own food by photosynthesis (holophytic/autotrophic nutrition) — simply being rooted in a nutrient-rich compost pit does not change its fundamentally autotrophic mode of nutrition to saprophytic feeding, which requires absorbing organic matter directly (as fungi do).

Q: The mushroom is an organism which feeds
A) parasitically
B) saprophytically
C) holozoically
D) symbiotically
E) autotrophically
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A mushroom (a type of fungus) typically obtains its nutrients by feeding saprophytically — secreting digestive enzymes onto dead organic matter and absorbing the digested products — rather than through any of the other listed nutritional modes.

Q: If Benedict's solution gives a positive result in a food test, the food contains
A) sugar
B) protein
C) oil
D) fat
E) cellulose
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Benedict's solution is specifically used to test for reducing sugars; a positive result (colour change to green, yellow, orange, or brick-red depending on concentration) confirms the presence of a reducing sugar in the food sample.

Q: The deficiency of Vitamin D leads to
A) scurvy
B) pellagra
C) rickets
D) beriberi
E) polio
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Vitamin D deficiency specifically causes rickets in children (or osteomalacia in adults) — a condition of weak, poorly mineralized bones — distinct from scurvy (vitamin C deficiency), pellagra (niacin/B3 deficiency), or beriberi (vitamin B1 deficiency).

Q: Which of these substances is likely to be deficient in the diet of a person having goitre?
A) Potassium
B) Calcium
C) Iodine
D) Sodium
E) Phosphorus
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Goitre (an enlarged thyroid gland) results specifically from a deficiency of iodine, which is essential for the production of thyroid hormones.

Q: Some of the major elements required by plants are
A) Potassium, nitrogen, phosphorus, sodium, calcium
B) Nitrogen, phosphorus, molybdenum, sodium, calcium
C) Potassium, phosphorus, molybdenum, sodium, calcium
D) Potassium, nitrogen, iron, sodium, calcium
E) Potassium, nitrogen, phosphorus, silicon, cadmium
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Potassium, nitrogen, phosphorus, and calcium are all genuine major (macro-) nutrients required by plants in comparatively large amounts. (Note: sodium is not actually a standard essential plant nutrient, so its inclusion here is a simplification in the original question, but it remains the best-fitting option among those given, since the other options each mix in a genuine trace element — molybdenum, silicon, or cadmium — among the major elements, making them clearly incorrect.)

Q: Kwashiorkor is caused by severe deficiency of
A) water
B) oil
C) drugs
D) proteins
E) sugars
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Kwashiorkor is a well-known protein-energy malnutrition disorder caused specifically by a severe deficiency of dietary protein, typically seen in children who are weaned early and then fed a starchy, protein-poor diet.

Q: Which of the following pairs are the products of sucrose hydrolysis?
A) Glucose + glucose
B) Fructose + fructose
C) Glucose + fructose
D) Galactose + fructose
E) Fructose + galactose
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Sucrose is a disaccharide made of one glucose unit and one fructose unit joined together; hydrolysis (breaking this bond with water) yields exactly these two monosaccharides, glucose and fructose.

Q: If an organic compound has its Hydrogen:Oxygen ratio as 2:1, it is likely to be
A) a protein
B) a carbohydrate
C) a fat
D) a fatty acid and glycerol
E) an amino acid
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Carbohydrates are characteristically defined by having their hydrogen and oxygen atoms present in the same ratio as in water (2:1), reflected in the general formula Cx(H2O)y — a signature not shared by proteins, fats, or amino acids.

Q: Which of the following elements are necessary for the formation of chlorophyll in a plant?
A) Magnesium and iron
B) Calcium and potassium
C) Calcium and sulphur
D) Potassium and sulphur
E) Phosphorus and potassium
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Magnesium sits at the very core of the chlorophyll molecule, and iron, while not part of the molecule's structure, is required as a catalyst in the chlorophyll synthesis pathway — making magnesium and iron together the correct pairing of elements essential to chlorophyll formation.

Q: Which of the following statements is NOT true of insectivorous plants?
A) They obtain part of their food by trapping and feeding on insects
B) They attract insects simply because of pollination
C) They can grow in soils poor in nitrogenous salts
D) They can supplement the nitrogen supply by feeding on insects
E) Examples include butterworts, sundews and pitcher plants
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Insectivorous (carnivorous) plants attract and trap insects specifically to digest them as a nutrient (particularly nitrogen) source — not merely for pollination purposes, making option B the false statement. They genuinely do grow in nitrogen-poor soils, supplement nitrogen by digesting insects, and butterworts, sundews, and pitcher plants are all real examples.

Q: A mixture of mercurous and mercuric nitrates is added to a food substance. A white precipitate is formed which on gentle heating turns red. The food substance is
A) protein
B) oil
C) Carbohydrate
D) Fat
E) Fatty acid
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: This colour-change reaction (white precipitate turning red/pink on heating) is characteristic of Millon's test for protein, in which mercury-based reagents react specifically with tyrosine residues found in most proteins.

Q: In a Biuret test, some protein was mixed with sodium hydroxide solution. Which of the following chemicals should be added to the mixture for a positive result?
A) Mercurous nitrate
B) Copper sulphate
C) Mercuric nitrate
D) Sodium carbonate
E) Ammonium hydroxide
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The Biuret test for protein requires adding dilute copper sulphate solution to an alkaline (sodium hydroxide-containing) sample; a positive result shows as a violet/purple colour change, caused by the copper ions reacting with peptide bonds in the protein.

Q: Fehling's solution will readily change colour from blue to a reddish colour when it is
A) mixed with sugar solution in the cold
B) warmed or heated by itself
C) mixed with reducing sugar in the cold
D) warmed or heated with a complex solution
E) warmed with a solution of reducing sugar
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: A positive Fehling's test specifically requires both the presence of a reducing sugar and heating (warming) the mixture — simply mixing in the cold, or heating the reagent alone without a reducing sugar present, will not produce the colour change.

Q: A food substance which produces red colouration with Sudan III contains
A) protein
B) sugar
C) starch
D) cellulose
E) fat
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Sudan III is a fat-soluble dye specifically used to test for the presence of fats and oils; a positive test shows as a distinct red (or red-stained) colouration/layer.

Q: If calcium is deficient in food this may cause
A) anaemia
B) retarded growth
C) sterility
D) goitre
E) beriberi
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Calcium deficiency primarily impairs healthy bone and tooth formation, resulting in retarded growth (as well as weak, stunted development), rather than the other listed conditions, which are linked to different specific nutrient deficiencies.

Q: A young plant showing yellow leaves is likely to be deficient in
A) calcium
B) magnesium
C) potassium
D) boron
E) molybdenum
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Magnesium is a core component of chlorophyll, so a deficiency directly impairs chlorophyll production, causing the yellowing of leaves (chlorosis) characteristic of magnesium-deficient plants.

Q: Oxygen liberated during photosynthesis has been demonstrated to come from
A) carbon dioxide
B) air
C) water
D) chlorophyll
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, isotope-labelling experiments have confirmed that the oxygen released during photosynthesis originates from the splitting (photolysis) of water molecules, not from carbon dioxide.

Q: Which of these is a trace element?
A) Iron
B) Copper
C) Calcium
D) Sulphur
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Copper is a micronutrient (trace element), required by living organisms only in very small quantities, unlike iron, calcium, and sulphur, all of which (in this context) are needed in comparatively larger amounts.

Q: The main organic substances found in the human body are
A) carbohydrates, proteins and salts
B) salts, fats and proteins
C) fats, carbohydrates and proteins
D) salts, fats and carbohydrates
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The three primary classes of organic (carbon-based) compounds that make up the bulk of the human body's biochemical composition are carbohydrates, fats, and proteins. Salts, being inorganic, do not belong in a list of "organic substances."

Q: Which of the following elements is essential for the formation of haemoglobin?
A) Sodium
B) Potassium
C) Calcium
D) Iron
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Iron is a core structural component of the haem group within haemoglobin, the oxygen-carrying protein in red blood cells, making it essential for haemoglobin formation.

Q: The severe deficiency of vitamin C leads to
A) kwashiorkor
B) beriberi
C) pellagra
D) scurvy
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) deficiency specifically causes scurvy, characterized by bleeding gums, joint pain, and poor wound healing, distinct from kwashiorkor (protein deficiency), beriberi (vitamin B1 deficiency), or pellagra (niacin/B3 deficiency).

Q: In addition to the high calories derived from fats and oils, they are
A) used in producing new cells
B) necessary for enzyme formation
C) used as insulators from cold
D) required for growth
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Beyond their major role as a concentrated, long-term energy source, fats and oils also serve a valuable secondary function as insulation, helping to conserve body heat and protect against cold.

Q: The extract from a food substance reacting with sodium hydroxide and copper sulphate solutions will produce violet to purple coloration if
A) fats are present
B) carbohydrate is present
C) protein is present
D) reducing sugar is present
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established in the earlier Biuret test question, this specific combination of reagents (sodium hydroxide plus dilute copper sulphate) producing a violet/purple colour is the classic positive indicator for the presence of protein.

Q: In demonstrating the importance of mineral elements in plants, the culture bottle must be darkened to
A) prevent algal growth in culture solution
B) allow root growth
C) prevent breakdown of mineral elements
D) prevent photosynthesis in the root
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In a mineral nutrient (water culture) experiment, the culture solution bottle is darkened specifically to prevent light from reaching the nutrient solution, which would otherwise encourage unwanted algal growth that could interfere with the plant's own uptake of the mineral nutrients being tested.

Q: The vitamin which is important in the formation of the retina pigments is
A) vitamin A
B) vitamin B
C) vitamin C
D) vitamin D
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Vitamin A (retinol, named directly after its role) is essential for forming the light-sensitive pigments in the retina, particularly rhodopsin, which is critical for vision, especially in low light.

Q: In the standard experiment to show that oxygen is given off during photosynthesis, sodium bicarbonate is used to
A) neutralize the acid in water

ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Sodium bicarbonate dissolved in the water of a photosynthesis demonstration set-up (e.g. with a submerged aquatic plant like Elodea) decomposes to release carbon dioxide, ensuring an adequate CO2 supply so the plant can photosynthesize actively and produce a measurable stream of oxygen bubbles.

Q: The most important substances necessary for the maintenance of life are

ANSWER: (see explanation)

Q: If a healthy potted plant is continuously kept in dim light, the rate of photosynthesis is otherwise faster than respiration in bright light. Which of the following is true when kept in dim light?
A) the rate of respiration may equal that of photosynthesis
B) more carbon dioxide and water are taken in
C) respiration may be halted
D) the volume of oxygen released increases
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In dim light, the rate of photosynthesis slows down considerably (since photosynthesis is otherwise faster than respiration in bright light), to the point where it may drop to roughly match the plant's constant, ongoing rate of respiration — this balance point is known as the light compensation point.

Q: The seedlings in a rice field were found to have thin lanky growth with reddish leaves and poor root development. This is because the soil lacks
A) sulphur
B) phosphorus
C) potassium
D) iron
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Phosphorus deficiency in plants classically produces exactly this combination of symptoms: thin, lanky (stunted but elongated) growth, a reddish or purplish leaf discoloration, and poor root development, since phosphorus is vital for root growth and general energy transfer in the plant.

Q: The mode of nutrition in which digestion is extracellular is
A) holophytic
B) parasitic
C) holozoic
D) saprophytic
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Saprophytic organisms (like fungi) digest their food externally, secreting digestive enzymes onto the organic material outside their own bodies (extracellular digestion) before absorbing the already-digested nutrients, unlike holozoic organisms, which typically digest food internally within their bodies.

Q: The first step in the process of photosynthesis is the
A) activation of the chlorophyll
B) photolysis of water
C) reduction of carbon dioxide
D) formation of sugar
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Photosynthesis begins when light energy is absorbed and "activates" (excites) the chlorophyll molecule, which is the essential first step that then drives all the subsequent reactions, including the splitting of water (photolysis) and eventually the fixation of carbon dioxide into sugar.

Q: Which of the following food substances will produce a brick-red colour when warmed with Benedict's solution?
A) Glucose
B) Starch
C) Egg white
D) Maltose
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Both glucose and maltose are reducing sugars and would technically give a positive Benedict's test; however, glucose is the standard, most direct reducing sugar example that reliably produces the classic brick-red colour, whereas starch and egg white (protein) are non-reducing/non-sugar substances and would not react this way.

Q: Etiolation is caused by the influence of
A) mineral salts
B) water
C) carbon dioxide
D) light
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Etiolation — the pale, thin, and elongated growth of a plant — is caused specifically by a lack of adequate light, which prevents normal chlorophyll development and causes the plant to grow abnormally toward any available light source.

Q: Which of the following is formed immediately after the first product of photosynthesis?
A) Lipid
B) Starch
C) Oxygen
D) Sugar
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Once glucose (the first, direct product of photosynthesis) is formed, it is very rapidly converted into starch for storage in most plants — making starch the substance formed immediately following the initial glucose product.

Q: The element common to protein, carbohydrate and lipid is
A) hydrogen
B) sulphur
C) nitrogen
D) phosphorus
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Hydrogen (along with carbon and oxygen) is present in all three major organic food classes — carbohydrates, lipids, and proteins — whereas nitrogen, sulphur, and phosphorus are found only in some of these groups (e.g. nitrogen is characteristic of proteins but not typically of carbohydrates).

Q: 5cm³ dilute sodium hydroxide solution and 5cm³ one percent copper sulphate solution are added to a solution of food specimen. The purple colour which is observed shows the presence of
A) glucose

Q: Interveinal chlorosis is normally associated with the deficiency of
A) magnesium

C) iron

ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Interveinal chlorosis (yellowing specifically between the leaf veins, while the veins themselves remain green) is a classic symptom of magnesium deficiency, since magnesium is central to chlorophyll but is mobile within the plant and gets redistributed away from older tissue first, leaving a distinctive veined pattern.

EXPLANATION: Osmic acid (osmium tetroxide) has a strong affinity for unsaturated fatty components and reacts with fats and oils to produce a characteristic black precipitate/stain, distinguishing it from tests for proteins, amino acids, or starch.

EXPLANATION: Carnivorous (insectivorous) plants have evolved their prey-trapping adaptations specifically because they typically grow in nitrogen-poor (nitrate-deficient) soils, and digesting captured insects provides them with a valuable supplementary nitrogen source that the soil cannot.

Q: Green plants are distinguished from other living organisms by their ability to
A) make use of water
B) make use of oxygen

ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The single most defining characteristic that sets green plants apart from most other living organisms is their autotrophic ability to manufacture their own food through photosynthesis, rather than needing to consume other organisms for nutrition.

Q: The failure of transport and respiratory systems in plants, as well as the presence of reddish colour in stems and leaves, results from the deficiency of
A) magnesium
B) nitrogen

EXPLANATION: As noted earlier, phosphorus deficiency classically produces reddish/purplish discoloration in stems and leaves, alongside impaired energy transfer processes that are essential to healthy plant transport and metabolic (respiratory) function.
