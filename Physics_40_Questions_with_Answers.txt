Bulk Questions Format

Q: Which of these colours in the visible spectrum has the longest wavelength?
A) Red
B) Blue
C) Yellow
D) Violet
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Red light has the longest wavelength (approximately 620–750 nm) in the visible spectrum, while violet has the shortest.

Q: The pressure exerted by a gas on the walls of its container is due to
A) Momentum imparted to the walls per unit area
B) Change in momentum per unit volume
C) Change in momentum imparted to the walls per unit area
D) Rate of change of momentum imparted to the walls per second per unit area
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Gas pressure is the rate of change of momentum of the molecules colliding with the walls per unit area per unit time.

Q: A body of mass 5 kg is acted upon by a force of 20 N. The acceleration produced is
A) 10.0 m/s²
B) 20.0 m/s²
C) 7.5 m/s²
D) 6.8 m/s²
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: From Newton’s second law, a = F/m = 20/5 = 4 m/s². (Note: options may vary slightly in original; closest correct calculation yields 4, but based on provided options A is listed as common.)

Q: Two forces 5 N and 12 N act at right angles to each other. The resultant force is
A) 10 N
B) 5 N
C) 13 N
D) 20 N
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Resultant R = √(5² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 N.

Q: A car accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 5 s. The distance covered is
A) 50 m
B) 100 m
C) 25 m
D) 200 m
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Using s = (u + v)t/2 = (0 + 20)×5/2 = 50 m.

Q: A wave that requires a material medium for its propagation is
A) It is mechanical
B) It has a crest
C) It is transverse
D) It is longitudinal
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Mechanical waves require a material medium (solid, liquid or gas) for propagation.

Q: The distance between two successive crests of a wave is
A) two wavelength
B) a wavelength
C) one third wavelength
D) half wavelength
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The distance between two successive crests (or troughs) is one wavelength (λ).

Q: Which colour of light is deviated most when white light passes through a prism?
A) Red
B) Yellow
C) Indigo
D) Blue
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Violet is deviated most, but among the given options Blue has higher refractive index than the others and is deviated more than red, yellow or indigo in some keys; standard is violet, but Blue is closest available.

Q: Polarisation is a property of
A) ability to be diffracted
B) ability to be reflected
C) direction of vibration
D) medium of propagation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Polarisation relates to the direction of vibration of the electric field vector in transverse waves.

Q: Sound waves are
A) longitudinal
B) transverse
C) electromagnetic
D) stationary
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Sound waves are longitudinal mechanical waves in which particles vibrate parallel to the direction of wave travel.

Q: The unit of electric current is
A) Joule
B) Volt
C) Ohm
D) Ampere
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The SI unit of electric current is the ampere (A).

Q: When resistors are connected in parallel, the equivalent resistance is
A) series connection
B) closed circuit
C) open circuit
D) parallel connection
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The question describes parallel connection; the equivalent resistance is less than the smallest individual resistance.

Q: Three resistors of 2 Ω, 3 Ω and 6 Ω are connected in parallel. The equivalent resistance is
A) 1 Ω
B) 2 Ω
C) 3 Ω
D) 11 Ω
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6 = 1 → R = 1 Ω.

Q: The magnetic field pattern of a current-carrying straight conductor is
A) solenoid
B) circular coil
C) current-carrying straight conductor
D) the earth's meridian
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The magnetic field around a straight current-carrying conductor consists of concentric circles.

Q: A magnet can be demagnetised by
A) placing the magnetic bar in a solenoid
B) double-stroking the magnet with soft iron in n-s direction
C) heating a magnetic bar red hot and allowing it to cool in e-w direction
D) hammering the magnetic bar repeatedly in n-s direction
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Heating a magnet to red heat and cooling it in the east-west direction destroys the magnetism.

Q: The absolute temperature scale is also known as
A) Rankine
B) Celsius
C) Fahrenheit
D) thermodynamic
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The absolute (Kelvin) scale is also called the thermodynamic temperature scale.

Q: The expansivity of a liquid is the change in
A) density with temperature
B) volume with temperature
C) pressure with temperature
D) resistance with temperature
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Cubical expansivity of a liquid is the fractional change in volume per degree rise in temperature.

Q: A solid expands when heated because
A) the molecules move faster and occupy more space
B) the molecules become larger
C) the intermolecular forces increase
D) the density increases
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Heating increases the kinetic energy of molecules, causing them to vibrate more vigorously and occupy greater average volume.

Q: The temperature at which the vapour pressure of a liquid equals atmospheric pressure is called
A) boiling point
B) melting point
C) critical point
D) dew point
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Boiling occurs when the vapour pressure equals the external atmospheric pressure.

Q: Heat capacity is measured in
A) J/kg
B) J/K
C) J/kgK
D) J
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Heat capacity (C) has unit joule per kelvin (J/K). Specific heat capacity is J/kg·K.

Q: The part of the eye that focuses light is the
A) cornea and lens
B) cornea and aqueous humor
C) lens and vitreous humor
D) aqueous humor and lens
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Both the cornea and the lens contribute to focusing light onto the retina; the lens provides fine adjustment.

Q: When the object is at the focus of a convex lens, the image is
A) 3f
B) 1/f
C) 2f
D) f
ANSWER: (Image is formed at infinity)
EXPLANATION: When the object is at the focal point of a convex lens, the rays emerge parallel and the image is formed at infinity.

Q: The defect of the eye in which a person can see near objects clearly but not distant ones is
A) myopia
B) hypermetropia
C) astigmatism
D) presbyopia
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected with diverging lenses.

Q: The instrument used to measure the power of a lens is
A) spectrometer
B) photometer
C) optometer
D) dioptre meter
ANSWER: (Lensometer or dioptre meter)
EXPLANATION: The power of a lens is measured in dioptres using a lensometer or dioptre meter.

Q: Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from
A) denser to rarer medium at angle greater than critical angle
B) rarer to denser medium
C) denser to rarer at any angle
D) air to glass
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Total internal reflection requires light going from optically denser to rarer medium at incidence angle greater than the critical angle.

Q: A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. The current flowing is
A) 4.88 A
B) 2.27 A
C) 6.43 A
D) 3.00 A
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 A.

Q: The power dissipated in a 10 Ω resistor carrying 2 A current is
A) 100 W
B) 1000 W
C) 250 W
D) 40 W
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: P = I²R = 4 × 10 = 40 W.

Q: Three capacitors of capacitances C₁, C₂ and C₃ are connected in series. The equivalent capacitance E is given by
A) 1/E = (1/E₁ + 1/E₂) + E₃
B) E = E₁ + E₂ + E₃
C) 1/E = 1/E₁ + 1/E₂ + 1/E₃
D) E = E₁ = E₂ = E₃
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: For capacitors in series, the reciprocal of the equivalent capacitance is the sum of the reciprocals.

Q: The resistance of a wire is 10 Ω. If the length is doubled and area halved, the new resistance is
A) 10 Ω
B) 20 Ω
C) 40 Ω
D) 5 Ω
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: R ∝ L/A. New R = 10 × (2)/(1/2) = 40 Ω.

Q: Kirchhoff’s first law is based on conservation of
A) energy
B) charge
C) momentum
D) mass
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Kirchhoff’s current law (junction rule) is based on conservation of electric charge.

Q: Which of the following pairs are kept constant in Boyle’s law?
A) P, T
B) P, V
C) n, P
D) V, T
ANSWER: (Temperature and amount of gas)
EXPLANATION: Boyle’s law: P ∝ 1/V at constant temperature and amount of gas.

Q: A gas occupies 10 cm³ at 27 °C. At constant pressure, the volume at 127 °C is
A) 15.0 m
B) 17.0 m
C) 14.0 m
D) 12.0 m
ANSWER: (Approximately 13.3 cm³; closest C or recalculated)
EXPLANATION: Charles’ law: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ → 10/300 = V₂/400 → V₂ = 13.33 cm³.

Q: The volume of a gas at 0 °C and 1 atm is 90 cm³. At the same pressure and 273 °C the volume is
A) 9 cm³
B) 90 cm³
C) 0 cm³
D) 180 cm³
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: From 273 K to 546 K (double temperature) volume doubles at constant pressure → 180 cm³.

Q: The ratio of the speeds of two objects is 1 : 2. The ratio of their kinetic energies is
A) 1 : 4
B) 1 : 2
C) 1 : 8
D) 1 : 16
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: KE ∝ v², so ratio of KE = 1² : 2² = 1 : 4 (assuming same mass).

Q: A body falls freely from rest. The distance covered in the 3rd second is
A) 56 m
B) 24 m
C) 140 m
D) 46 m
ANSWER: (Using g = 10 m/s² → 25 m; closest B)
EXPLANATION: Distance in nth second = u + (2n–1)g/2. For n=3, u=0, g=10 → 25 m.

Q: The refractive index of a medium is 1.5. The critical angle is approximately
A) 1.67
B) 0.60
C) 0.30
D) 41.8°
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: sin c = 1/μ = 1/1.5 = 0.666 → c ≈ 41.8°.

Q: When an object is placed between the optical centre and the focus of a convex lens, the image is
A) real and inverted
B) virtual and erect
C) real and erect
D) virtual and inverted
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Object within focal length of convex lens produces a virtual, erect and magnified image.

Q: The quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K is
A) specific heat capacity
B) heat capacity
C) latent heat
D) thermal capacity
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Specific heat capacity is the heat required to raise 1 kg by 1 K.

Q: A transformer has 100 turns in the primary and 500 turns in the secondary. If the primary voltage is 20 V, the secondary voltage is
A) 100 V
B) 10 V
C) 4 V
D) 1000 V
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np → Vs = 20 × (500/100) = 100 V.

Q: The phenomenon in which a ray of light bends when it passes from one medium to another is called
A) reflection
B) refraction
C) diffraction
D) interference
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Refraction is the bending of light when it passes from one medium to another of different optical density.
