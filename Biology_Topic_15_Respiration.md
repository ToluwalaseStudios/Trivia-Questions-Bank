# TOPIC 15: RESPIRATION
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: In anaerobic respiration, glucose is converted to one of the following:
A) Carbon dioxide and water
B) Carbon dioxide only
C) Carbon dioxide and alcohol
D) Alcohol and water
E) Water and acetic acid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Anaerobic respiration in organisms like yeast (alcoholic fermentation) breaks down glucose into carbon dioxide and ethanol (alcohol), releasing a comparatively small amount of energy — unlike aerobic respiration, which fully breaks glucose down into carbon dioxide and water while releasing much more energy.

Q: The lime water in container C turned milky because
A) Carbon dioxide was produced by plant B during respiration and entered C through the filter pump
B) carbon dioxide reacts with lime water to produce milkiness
C) oxygen reacts with lime water to produce milkiness
D) soda lime did not remove carbon dioxide from air
E) the delivery tubes were not well connected
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Lime water (calcium hydroxide solution) is a standard test reagent that turns milky specifically in the presence of carbon dioxide; in this experimental set-up, the carbon dioxide produced by the respiring plant in container B was carried through to container C, causing the characteristic milky colour change — confirming respiration was occurring.

Q: Oxygen from the atmosphere reaches the mammalian bloodstream through one of the paths indicated below (a = mouth, b = trachea, c = bronchiole, d = nostril, e = bronchus, f = alveoli). Indicate the correct path.
A) a→b→e→f→d
B) b→d→f→e→c
C) d→b→e→c→f
D) b→a→f→e→c
E) f→e→c→d→c
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The correct anatomical route for inhaled air is: nostril (d) → trachea (b) → bronchus (e) → bronchiole (c) → alveoli (f), where oxygen finally diffuses into the bloodstream — matching option C's sequence.

Q: What is the name of the respiratory organ of the crab?
A) Tubules
B) Trachea
C) Spiracles
D) Lungs
E) Gills
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The crab, being an aquatic crustacean, breathes using gills, which extract dissolved oxygen from water — unlike insects (trachea/spiracles) or terrestrial vertebrates (lungs).

Q: In a bakery, yeast is added to flour to make bread rise. This is possible because yeast produces
A) alcohol
B) oxygen
C) carbondioxide
D) energy
E) ethanol
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As yeast respires anaerobically within the dough, it produces carbon dioxide gas as a by-product; these gas bubbles become trapped within the dough, causing it to rise before baking.

Q: The glottis is the opening which leads to the
A) oesophagus
B) larynx
C) nostrils
D) pharynx
E) mouth
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The glottis is specifically the opening (guarded by the vocal cords) that leads into the larynx (voice box) and onward to the trachea, forming the entrance to the respiratory airway, distinct from the oesophagus, which leads to the digestive tract.

Q: A grasshopper respires by means of its
A) lung books
B) gills
C) lungs
D) antennae
E) tracheal tubes
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Insects such as grasshoppers breathe using an internal network of air-filled tracheal tubes, which deliver oxygen directly to tissues, entering through external openings called spiracles — quite different from lung books (spiders), gills (aquatic animals), or true lungs (vertebrates).

Q: When freshly tapped palm wine was kept overnight, it was found to be more alcoholic. This effect must have been due to
A) the addition of saccharin
B) the addition of sugar
C) the addition of whisky or local gin
D) some complex hydrolytic reactions
E) anaerobic respiration of an organism
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The increased alcohol content overnight results from natural yeast organisms present in the palm wine continuing to anaerobically respire (ferment) the sugars in the sap, producing additional ethanol as a by-product over time.

Q: Which of the following statements is TRUE of inhalation of air by man?
A) The ribs are not raised
B) Diaphragm is raised
C) Intercostal muscles relax
D) Pressure of the thoracic cavity increases
E) Size of thoracic cavity increases
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: During inhalation, the diaphragm flattens (lowers, not raises) and the external intercostal muscles contract (not relax), raising the ribs — these combined actions increase the overall size (volume) of the thoracic cavity, which is the accurate description among the options given.

Q: Anaerobic respiration in yeast produces
A) carbondioxide and ethanol
B) carbondioxide and water
C) carbondioxide and glucose
D) carbondioxide and oxygen
E) ethanol and water
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, anaerobic respiration (alcoholic fermentation) in yeast specifically breaks down glucose into carbon dioxide and ethanol (alcohol).

Q: Heat produced in tissue respiration in plants is
A) a chemical form of energy
B) the only form of energy
C) the main form of energy
D) a useful form of energy
E) a waste form of energy
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The heat generated as a by-product of cellular respiration is typically not harnessed for any useful biochemical work by the plant — it simply dissipates, making it, in this context, effectively a waste form of energy release.

Q: Which of the following events does NOT occur during anaerobic respiration of glucose?
A) Muscle cells produce lactic acid
B) Carbon dioxide is produced
C) Milk produces lactic acid
D) Energy is not produced
E) Germinating seeds produce alcohol
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Anaerobic respiration does still release a (smaller) amount of energy, even though far less than aerobic respiration — claiming that "energy is not produced" at all is therefore the false statement, since muscle lactic acid fermentation, bacterial milk souring, carbon dioxide production, and alcohol production in germinating seeds are all genuine anaerobic respiration processes that do release some usable energy.

Q: Identify which of the following are characteristics of the vertebrate respiratory surface: (1) Moist (2) Vascularized (3) Semipermeable (4) Freely permeable (5) Dry
A) 1, 2, 3
B) 1, 2, 5
C) 2, 3, 5
D) 2, 4, 5
E) 1, 3, 5
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: An effective respiratory surface must be moist (to allow gases to dissolve for diffusion), well vascularized (richly supplied with blood to carry gases away/toward), and semipermeable (selectively allowing only respiratory gases through) — combining features 1, 2, and 3, unlike a dry or non-selectively "freely permeable" surface, which would not function effectively.

Q: Which of the following statements is NOT correct with respect to inhalation in mammals?
A) intercostal muscles contract
B) diaphragm is raised
C) ribs are raised
D) pressure of the thoracic cavity decreases
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: During inhalation, the diaphragm actually contracts and flattens (moving downward, not "raised"), which is essential for increasing thoracic volume — the intercostal muscles do contract, the ribs are indeed raised, and thoracic pressure does decrease as volume increases, making the diaphragm statement the incorrect one.

Q: The equation that can be used to summarize the process of anaerobic breakdown of sugar is
A) C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2
B) 6CO2 → 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
C) C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + energy
D) C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2 + energy
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The complete and accurate summary equation for anaerobic respiration (alcoholic fermentation) must include the release of (a smaller amount of) energy alongside the ethanol and carbon dioxide products, matching option D — option A is missing the energy term, while option C actually represents aerobic respiration instead.

Q: Fatigue of leg muscles may occur after riding many kilometers on a bicycle because of
A) insufficient glucose
B) excess carbondioxide
C) excess protein
D) insufficient oxygen
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: During prolonged, intense exercise, the muscles' oxygen supply can become insufficient to meet the high demand of continued aerobic respiration, forcing the muscles to switch to anaerobic respiration, which produces lactic acid — the resulting build-up of lactic acid is what directly causes the sensation of muscle fatigue and cramping.

Q: Lung books are used for respiration in
A) spiders
B) insects
C) millipedes
D) snails
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Lung books — stacked, book-page-like folds of tissue that increase surface area for gas exchange — are a distinctive respiratory structure specifically found in spiders (and some other arachnids), not in insects (tracheal system), millipedes, or snails.

Q: In the mammalian respiratory system, exchange of gases occurs in the
A) lungs
B) bronchi
C) bronchioles
D) alveoli
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The alveoli are the tiny, thin-walled, richly vascularized air sacs at the very end of the bronchiole branches, specifically where actual gas exchange (oxygen and carbon dioxide diffusion) takes place — the bronchi and bronchioles are simply conducting airways, not exchange surfaces.

Q: In living cells, insufficient oxygen may cause a breakdown of glucose into
A) fatty acids
B) lactic acids
C) glycogen
D) carbon dioxide
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: When oxygen supply is insufficient for normal aerobic respiration, animal cells (such as muscle cells) switch to anaerobic respiration, breaking down glucose into lactic acid instead, rather than fully oxidizing it to carbon dioxide and water.

Q: Which of the following can bring about the greatest increase in the rate of transpiration?
A) Increased humidity
B) Reduced temperature
C) Reduced wind speed
D) Reduced humidity
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Reduced humidity increases the water vapour concentration gradient between the leaf's internal air spaces and the surrounding atmosphere, driving water vapour out faster and thereby increasing the transpiration rate — increased humidity, reduced temperature, and reduced wind speed would each instead slow transpiration down.

Q: Gaseous exchange in Aves (birds) occurs in the
A) tracheoles
B) bronchi
C) air sacs
D) trachea
ANSWER: (see explanation)
EXPLANATION: In birds, actual gas exchange takes place within the specialized parabronchi of the lungs themselves, not in the air sacs, which instead function primarily as bellows-like reservoirs to keep air flowing continuously and unidirectionally through the lungs (nor in the trachea or bronchi, which are simply conducting airways).

Q: In the absence of oxygen, the pyruvic acid produced during glycolysis is converted to CO2 and
A) water
B) glycerol
C) ethanol
D) citric acid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Under anaerobic conditions (as in yeast), pyruvic acid from glycolysis is converted into ethanol and carbon dioxide, in the process known as alcoholic fermentation.

Q: Exhaled air differs from inhaled air in that it

EXPLANATION: Exhaled air picks up moisture from the moist respiratory surfaces on its way out of the body, giving it noticeably more water vapour than inhaled air, in addition to being warmer and containing more carbon dioxide (and less oxygen) than inhaled air.
