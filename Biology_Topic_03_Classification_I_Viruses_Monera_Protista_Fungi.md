# TOPIC 3: CLASSIFICATION I — VIRUSES, MONERA, PROTISTA, FUNGI
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: Euglena may be classified as a plant because it
A) has chloroplast
B) has a gullet
C) lives in a pond
D) possesses a flagellum
E) has a pellicle
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The possession of chloroplasts (allowing Euglena to photosynthesize like a plant) is its key plant-like feature. Its gullet, pellicle, and flagellum are actually animal-like features (since it also lacks a cellulose cell wall), and living in a pond is simply a habitat, not a classification criterion.

Q: Rhizopus (or Mucor) obtains its food from dead organic matter extracellularly. It is said to be
A) a facultative parasite
B) a saprophyte
C) an obligate parasite
D) a commensal
E) an autotroph
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: An organism that feeds on dead and decaying organic matter by secreting digestive enzymes onto it and absorbing the digested products (extracellular digestion) is called a saprophyte. Parasites feed on living hosts, commensals live alongside another organism without harming or feeding directly off its tissue in this way, and autotrophs manufacture their own food.

Q: Which one of the following options is not true for Mucor or Rhizopus?
A) grows on moist dead organic matter
B) is a plant
C) has cellulose cell wall
D) reproduces asexually by producing spores
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Fungal cell walls (including those of Mucor/Rhizopus) are made of chitin, not cellulose, so option C is the false statement being tested here. (It is worth noting that, by modern classification, fungi are no longer classified as plants at all — they form their own separate kingdom — but at the time this question was set, "is a plant" was still treated as broadly acceptable, so chitin-versus-cellulose is the specific error intended.)

Q: Which of the following statements is not true of Paramecium?
A) it is slipper-shaped
B) the anterior end is blunt
C) the gullet is lined with cilia
D) it has an anal pore
E) its food vacuole is not stationary
ANSWER: No single option is false — all five statements are, in fact, biologically accurate descriptions of Paramecium
EXPLANATION: Paramecium truly is slipper-shaped (A); it moves blunt-end first, so that end is the anterior (B); its gullet is lined with cilia that create a feeding water current (C); it does have an anal pore (cytoproct) through which undigested waste is expelled (D); and its food vacuole does move along a set path through the cell rather than staying fixed in one place (E). Since every statement given is actually true, this particular historical question has no valid "not true" option among its choices.

Q: One of the following statements is not true of viruses.
A) They are micro-organisms
B) They are smaller than bacteria
C) They can be seen with an ordinary light microscope
D) They cause tobacco disease
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Viruses are far too small to be seen with an ordinary light microscope — they can only be observed using an electron microscope, which has much higher magnifying and resolving power. Viruses are indeed microscopic, smaller than bacteria, and can cause plant diseases such as tobacco mosaic disease.

Q: In Mucor or Rhizopus, carbohydrate is absorbed in the form of
A) starch
B) sucrose
C) glucose
D) glycogen
E) arabinose
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Like other fungi, Mucor digests complex carbohydrates extracellularly into the simplest absorbable sugar, glucose, before taking it up into its hyphae. Once inside, this glucose is then converted and stored in a different form (see the next question).

Q: In Rhizopus, carbohydrate is stored in the form of
A) glucose
B) paramylum
C) glycogen
D) starch
E) oil
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: After absorbing glucose, fungi such as Rhizopus convert and store their carbohydrate reserves as glycogen — the same storage carbohydrate used by animal cells — rather than as starch (used by plants) or paramylum (used by Euglena).

Q: In which of the following groups of animals are flagella and cilia found?
A) Flatworms
B) Annelids
C) Coelenterates
D) Protozoa
E) Nematodes
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The phylum Protozoa contains members that move by different means — for example, Amoeba by pseudopodia, Paramecium by cilia, and Euglena/Chlamydomonas by flagella — so both flagella and cilia are found within this one group, in different species.

Q: Which of the following is NOT true of Mucor?
A) it contains chlorophyll
B) it grows saprophytically
C) it bears spores in a sporangium
D) it consists of hyphae
E) it reproduces by conjugation
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Mucor is a fungus, and fungi lack chlorophyll entirely (they cannot photosynthesize and must obtain food from organic sources). It does grow saprophytically, has a hyphal body, produces spores in a sporangium, and can reproduce sexually by conjugation.

Q: Viruses are regarded as non-living because they
A) can neither reproduce asexually nor sexually
B) cannot survive in their respective environments
C) do not possess characteristics that can be transmitted from one generation to the next
D) can neither respire nor excrete
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Respiration and excretion are basic metabolic activities carried out independently by every true living cell. Viruses have no cellular machinery of their own to carry out these processes — they rely entirely on a host cell's machinery even to replicate — which is a major reason they are often classified as non-living outside a host.

Q: Which of the following features of Euglena is found only in animals?
A) Paramylum granules
B) Flagellum
C) Pellicle
D) Pyrenoid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A pellicle (a flexible protein-based outer covering, used instead of a rigid cell wall) is a distinctly animal-like feature of Euglena. Paramylum granules and pyrenoids are associated with its plant-like carbohydrate/starch storage, and flagella are found in both plant-like and animal-like organisms, so they are not exclusively animal features.

Q: The hypha of Rhizopus is said to be coenocytic because it
A) does not contain chlorophyll
B) has no cross walls
C) is vacuolated
D) stores oil globules
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Coenocytic" (or aseptate) describes a hypha that is one continuous tube of cytoplasm containing many nuclei, without internal cross-walls (septa) dividing it into separate cells. This lack of cross walls is exactly what the term coenocytic refers to.

Q: Spirogyra, Euglena and Chlamydomonas share many characteristics EXCEPT
A) nutrition
B) reproduction
C) mobility
D) irritability
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Spirogyra is a non-motile filamentous alga — it cannot actively move from place to place — whereas both Euglena and Chlamydomonas are motile, propelling themselves using flagella. All three, however, share similarities in their nutrition (photosynthetic), modes of reproduction, and general irritability (response to stimuli).

Q: What is the function of trichocyst in Paramecium?
A) Movement
B) Defence
C) Excretion
D) Reproduction
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Trichocysts are small organelles just beneath the cell surface of Paramecium that can rapidly discharge thread-like filaments to the outside. This discharge is used mainly for defence against predators (and sometimes to help anchor the organism), not for movement (which is the job of cilia), excretion (contractile vacuole), or reproduction (which involves the nuclei).

Q: When a virus is placed in a non-living medium, it
A) becomes dehydrated
B) forms spores
C) forms flagella
D) becomes crystallized
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Outside a living host, a virus can become inactive and form a crystal-like structure, remaining in this non-living crystalline state until it once again enters a suitable host cell, at which point it "reactivates" and resumes its living, infective characteristics.

Q: A multinucleate body without internal cell boundaries is characteristic of
A) bryophytes
B) fungi
C) algae
D) gymnosperms
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This describes the coenocytic hyphae of many fungi (such as Rhizopus/Mucor) — a single continuous mass of cytoplasm containing many nuclei but no internal walls separating them into distinct cells, unlike bryophytes, algae, or gymnosperms, whose bodies are made of separate, walled cells.

Q: Viruses are considered to be living organisms because they
A) possess transmittable characters
B) move from one place to another
C) respond to stimulation
D) ingest food materials
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Every virus contains a core of genetic material — either DNA or RNA, never both — that carries coded hereditary information capable of being copied and passed on (transmitted) to new virus particles once inside a host cell. This capacity for heredity is the main reason they are sometimes considered to have a "living" characteristic, even though they lack most other features of life such as independent movement, response, or feeding.

Q: Which of the following characteristics is common to Amoeba and Paramecium?
A) Oral groove
B) Trichocyst
C) Contractile vacuole
D) Cilia
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Both Amoeba and Paramecium are freshwater protozoans that constantly take in excess water by osmosis, so both possess a contractile vacuole to pump this excess water back out (osmoregulation). Oral grooves, trichocysts, and cilia, however, are specific to Paramecium and are not found in Amoeba.

Q: Which of the following are non-green plants?
A) Euglena
B) Fungi
C) Spirogyra
D) Angiosperms
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Fungi completely lack chlorophyll and are therefore never green; they cannot photosynthesize at all. Euglena and Spirogyra both contain chlorophyll (green pigment) for photosynthesis, and angiosperms are flowering green plants.

Q: One of the features which adapts Paramecium to its environment is the possession of
A) a regular shape
B) two nuclei
C) cilia
D) a pellicle
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Cilia allow Paramecium to swim actively — moving toward food sources and away from danger or unfavourable conditions — which is a key adaptation for a free-living organism in its watery environment.

Q: In which of the following organisms does a single cell perform all the functions of movement, nutrition, growth, excretion and photosynthesis?
A) Paramecium
B) Euglena
C) Amoeba
D) Spirogyra
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Euglena is unicellular, motile (by flagellum), and photosynthetic (it contains chlorophyll), so a single Euglena cell alone carries out movement, feeding, growth, excretion, and photosynthesis. Paramecium and Amoeba cannot photosynthesize, and Spirogyra, though photosynthetic, is technically a multicellular (filamentous) organism, not a single free cell.

Q: The spores of mucor are dispersed by
A) water
B) wind
C) insects
D) explosive mechanism
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mucor produces very light, dry spores inside a sporangium; once released, these spores are carried away by air currents (wind) and germinate into new fungal colonies wherever they land on a suitable substrate.

Q: Members of the phylum Protozoa use the contractile vacuole
A) to remove excess food
B) for movement
C) for digestion
D) to remove excess water
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Freshwater protozoans constantly absorb water by osmosis because their surroundings are more dilute than their cytoplasm; the contractile vacuole collects this excess water and periodically expels it from the cell, preventing the cell from bursting.

Q: Which of the following is TRUE of the process of conjugation in Paramecium?
A) Micro-nucleus disintegrates
B) Each ex-conjugant divides only once
C) Macronucleus undergoes division
D) Each micronucleus divides twice
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: During conjugation, two Paramecium cells pair up at their oral grooves and exchange micronuclear genetic material. Each micronucleus involved actually divides twice (through a meiotic-type process) to produce the necessary haploid nuclei for exchange, after which each ex-conjugant itself divides twice more, eventually giving rise to four daughter cells per original conjugant (eight cells in total from the pair).

Q: The smallest living organisms which share the characteristics of both living and non-living matter are
A) bacteria
B) fungi
C) viruses
D) protozoa
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Viruses are the smallest biological entities known, and they display a mix of living traits (they carry genetic material capable of heredity) and non-living traits (no independent metabolism, growth, or reproduction outside a host cell) — which is why they are often described as sitting at the boundary between living and non-living matter.

Q: Which of the following does a virus have in common with animal cells?
A) Nucleus
B) DNA
C) Glycogen
D) Cytoplasm
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Both viruses and animal cells contain nucleic acid as their genetic material — a virus contains either DNA or RNA, and animal cells contain DNA. However, viruses have no true nucleus, no cytoplasm, and do not store glycogen, unlike animal cells.

Q: Viruses differ from all forms of life because they
A) have a thick cell wall
B) feed on waste products of other organisms
C) cause infectious diseases
D) require other living cells to multiply
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The defining biological limitation of a virus is that it cannot reproduce (multiply) on its own — it must invade a living host cell and hijack that cell's machinery in order to make copies of itself. Not all viruses cause disease, they do not have a "thick cell wall," and they certainly do not feed on waste products.

Q: The procaryotic cell type is characterized by
A) a complex cytoplasm in which different regions are poorly defined
B) localization of different regions of the cell into tissues
C) a collection of organelles and macromolecular complexes
D) simple cytoplasm with well-defined regions
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Prokaryotic cells (like bacteria and blue-green algae/cyanobacteria) lack membrane-bound organelles, so their cytoplasm, while chemically complex and full of various molecular machinery, has no clearly compartmentalized regions the way a eukaryotic cell's organelles do. (Prokaryotic cells apply only to bacteria and cyanobacteria; all other organisms are eukaryotic.)

Q: The most recently evolved structure in animals is the
A) hair
B) cilium
C) scale
D) feather
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Hair is a defining feature unique to mammals, which are considered the most recently evolved and most advanced class of vertebrates, sitting at the top of the evolutionary scale among the options given (scales belong to older groups like fish/reptiles, feathers to birds, and cilia are an ancient, primitive cellular structure).

Q: The order in which organic evolution has progressed in plants is
A) Thallophyta, Schizophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta and Spermatophyta
B) Schizophyta, Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta and Spermatophyta
C) Pteridophyta, Spermatophyta, Thallophyta, Schizophyta and Bryophyta
D) Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Spermatophyta, Thallophyta and Schizophyta
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Plant evolution progressed from the simplest prokaryotic organisms upward: Schizophyta (bacteria and blue-green algae) → Thallophyta (algae) → Bryophyta (mosses/liverworts) → Pteridophyta (ferns) → Spermatophyta (seed-bearing plants), representing increasing structural complexity over time.

Q: Which of the following groups is the most advanced?
A) Pteridophytes
B) Bryophytes
C) Thallophytes
D) Gymnosperms
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Gymnosperms are seed-bearing plants and represent a more advanced evolutionary stage than thallophytes (algae), bryophytes (mosses), or pteridophytes (ferns), none of which produce true seeds.

Q: The animals that move by means of flagella include
A) Chlamydomonas and Euglena
B) Planaria and Amoeba
C) Amoeba and Hydra
D) Paramecium and Planaria
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Both Chlamydomonas and Euglena are flagellates — single-celled organisms that swim using one or more whip-like flagella. Amoeba moves by pseudopodia, Paramecium by cilia, and Planaria and Hydra move using muscular/ciliary means rather than flagella.

Q: The umbrella-shaped fruiting body of a fully developed mushroom is the
A) Pileus
B) Mycelium
C) Basidium
D) Stipe
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The pileus is the umbrella-shaped cap that sits on top of the mushroom's stalk (stipe); together, tightly packed hyphae form this cap and stalk structure. The mycelium is the underground/underlying network of hyphae, and the basidium is the microscopic spore-producing structure found on the gills beneath the cap, not the visible "umbrella" itself.

Q: The similarity among organisms belonging to the same group will be least within each
A) family
B) order
C) kingdom
D) species
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: In taxonomic classification, kingdom is the broadest, most inclusive category, so members within the same kingdom can be extremely varied (e.g. a mushroom and a human are both in different kingdoms, but a fungus and a mould could be quite different within the same kingdom Fungi). Species, in contrast, is the narrowest classification, so members of the same species are the most alike.

Q: The cell component that is present in a prokaryotic cell is the
A) chloroplast
B) ribosome
C) mitochondrion
D) nuclear envelope
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Prokaryotic cells (bacteria and cyanobacteria) lack membrane-bound organelles such as chloroplasts, mitochondria, and a true nuclear envelope, but they do contain ribosomes (specifically the smaller 70S type), which are essential for protein synthesis in every living cell.

Q: A plant-like feature in Euglena is the
A) pellicle
B) pigment spot
C) large spot
D) gullet
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The pigment spot (eyespot/stigma) in Euglena is associated with its chloroplast and helps the organism detect and orient itself toward light for photosynthesis — a plant-like function. Pellicle and gullet, by contrast, are animal-like features.

Q: The most common characteristic of the fungal hyphae is the possession of
A) cell-like compartments with one nucleus each
B) cell walls that are both rigid and chitinous
C) a multicellular mycelium in the substrate
D) cell-like partitions formed by cross-walls
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Fungal hyphal walls are consistently rigid and made of chitin (not cellulose), regardless of whether the hyphae are divided by cross-walls (septate) or not (aseptate/coenocytic) — making this the most universally common feature among fungal hyphae.

Q: The hyphal wall of fungi is rigid owing to the presence of
A) cell wall
B) lignin
C) cellulose
D) chitin
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Chitin, a tough nitrogen-containing polysaccharide (the same substance found in insect exoskeletons), gives fungal hyphal walls their rigidity — not cellulose (found in plant cell walls) or lignin (a plant cell wall-strengthening compound).

Q: The chromosomes of members of the kingdom Monera are within the
A) nucleoplasm
B) Cytoplasm
C) nucleus
D) nucleolus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Members of the kingdom Monera (bacteria and blue-green algae) are prokaryotic and have no true, membrane-bound nucleus. Their single circular chromosome lies free within the cytoplasm, in a region sometimes loosely called the nucleoid, rather than being enclosed by a nuclear membrane.

Q: In terms of the number of individuals, which of the following taxa is most inclusive?
A) Order
B) Family
C) Class
D) Species
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Following the taxonomic hierarchy (Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species), class ranks higher than order, family, and species among the options given, meaning it contains the largest number of different individuals/organisms overall.

Q: A blue-green alga is not a protophyte because
A) it is aquatic
B) its cells are prokaryotic
C) it cannot move
D) it is not a green plant
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Protophytes (plant-like protists, e.g. true algae) are eukaryotic organisms. Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) are actually prokaryotic, like bacteria, which is why they are classified under the kingdom Monera rather than as protophytes, despite being photosynthetic and superficially algae-like.

Q: Which of the following groups of cells is devoid of true nuclei?
A) Algae
B) Monera
C) Fungi
D) Viruses
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Members of the kingdom Monera (bacteria and cyanobacteria) are prokaryotic, meaning their genetic material is not enclosed within a true, membrane-bound nucleus. Algae and fungi are eukaryotic and do have true nuclei, and viruses are not made of cells at all, so this question is best answered with reference to actual cellular organisms.

Q: Which of the following is the lowest category of classification?
A) Class
B) Species
C) Family
D) Genus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Species is the most specific, narrowest, and lowest-ranking unit in the standard taxonomic hierarchy, sitting below genus, family, order, and class.

Q: The fungi are a distinct group of eukaryotes mainly because they have
A) spores
B) no chlorophyll
C) many fruiting bodies
D) sexual and asexual reproduction
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: While fungi do lack chlorophyll, this alone doesn't distinguish them (animal cells also lack chlorophyll), and spores plus sexual/asexual reproduction are shared with other groups like bryophytes and protozoans. What is genuinely distinctive about fungi is their characteristic fruiting bodies built from a mass of branching hyphae (mycelium) — a structural feature unique to this kingdom.

Q: The organ which is sensitive to light in Euglena is
A) flagellum
B) chloroplast
C) eyespot
D) gullet
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The eyespot (stigma) is a light-sensitive pigmented structure near the base of the flagellum in Euglena, allowing the organism to detect light direction and swim toward well-lit areas for effective photosynthesis.

Q: Which of the following is the simplest living organism?
A) Virus
B) Amoeba
C) Chlamydomonas
D) Paramecium
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: If a virus is considered to be "alive" for the purposes of this question, then it is structurally by far the simplest of the options, consisting of little more than genetic material wrapped in a protein coat, with no cell structure or organelles at all — far simpler than any of the true unicellular organisms listed. (It should be noted, however, that many biologists dispute whether viruses should be classed as "living" at all, precisely because of this lack of independent cellular machinery.)

Q: Fungi are referred to as heterotrophs because they
A) lack chlorophyll
B) have mycelium
C) lack roots
D) are filamentous
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Without chlorophyll, fungi cannot photosynthesize and manufacture their own food the way green plants (autotrophs) do; instead, they must obtain organic nutrients from other sources (dead matter, hosts, or partners), which is the defining feature of a heterotroph.
