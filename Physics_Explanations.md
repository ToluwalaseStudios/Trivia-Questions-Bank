**Q1. Increase in area of heated metal sheet**
**Answer: B, 5.76 × 10⁻⁵ m²** | ✔
Area expansivity β = 2α = 2 × 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ K⁻¹. A₀ = 0.20² = 0.04 m². Δθ = 75 − 15 = 60 K. ΔA = βA₀Δθ = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.04 × 60 = **5.76 × 10⁻⁵ m²**.

**Q2. Incorrect statements about free fall**
**Answer: D, iii and v** | ✔
- i. PE decreases: true (height falls).
- ii. KE increases linearly with distance fallen: true, since KE gained = mgh.
- iii. Mechanical energy increases: **false**, it is conserved (PE lost = KE gained).
- iv. KE at half-way = half of initial energy: true; at half-way, half the PE has become KE, so KE = ½ × initial energy.
- v. Work is done against gravity: **false**, gravity does the work on the falling body.
So the incorrect ones are iii and v.

**Q3. Scalar quantity**
**Answer: C, Electric potential** | ✔
Electric potential is work done per unit charge and has magnitude only. Electric field intensity and gravitational field are vectors (they have direction). Strictly, magnetic flux (Φ = BA cosθ) is also a scalar in physics, but electric potential is the keyed answer.

**Q4. Forces on a block at rest on an inclined plane (along the vertical)**
**Answer: C, At equilibrium** | You picked A ✘
The block is at rest, so the net force is zero. Along the vertical, the weight mg (down) is balanced by the combined upward effect of the normal reaction and friction, so the block is in equilibrium. mg sinθ acts along the plane (balanced by friction), and mg cosθ acts perpendicular to the plane (balanced by the normal reaction). Neither is "the vertical force".

**Q5. Methylated spirit feels cold**
**Answer: B, Extracts its latent heat of vaporisation from the body** | ✔
Methylated spirit evaporates quickly. Evaporation needs latent heat, which the spirit takes from the skin, so the skin loses heat and feels cold. Option D has the heat going the wrong way.

**Q6. Spring balance vs chemical balance (statements that are true)**
**Answer: C, III & IV** | ✔
The stem says "not true ... except", so pick the true statements.
- I false: the spring balance measures weight (a force), not mass.
- II false: the chemical balance compares masses, so it measures mass rather than weight.
- III true: spring balance follows Hooke's law; chemical balance follows the principle of moments.
- IV true: a spring balance reading changes with g, while a chemical balance compares masses at the same place, so g cancels.

**Q7. Projectile at 60°: ratio of range to maximum height**
**Answer: D, 2:1** | You picked C ✘
R = u² sin2θ / g and H = u² sin²θ / 2g. R/H = 2 sin2θ / sin²θ = 4 cotθ = 4 cot 60° = 4/√3 ≈ 2.31. Among the options, 2:1 is the closest. 4:1 occurs when θ = 45°, not 60°.

**Q8. Plank pivoted at O, weight 2P added to P**
**Answer: B, moving P nearer to O** | You picked C ✘
(The diagram was not in the file; the reasoning works whatever the distances are.) Initially P × p = Q × q about O. After adding 2P, the load on that side is 3P. To balance Q × q = P × p, the 3P load must sit at p/3 from O, so it must be moved **nearer to O**.
- A (moving Q nearer) reduces Q's moment and worsens the imbalance.
- C (adding Q to Q) gives 2Q × q = 2Pp, which still cannot balance 3Pp.
- D (moving P further) increases the imbalance.

**Q9. Charging an insulated conductor by induction**
**Answer: A** | You picked B ✘
The physically correct procedure is: bring the charged body near (I), touch the conductor with a finger to earth it (III), remove the finger (IV), then remove the charged body (II). That is I, III, IV, II. The order printed in option A (I, II, III, IV) differs from this, so it looks like a transcription slip in the question bank. A is the keyed option. Option B cannot be right: it repeats II and leaves out III (earthing).

**Q10. Ideal gas, pressure doubled and temperature halved**
**Answer: D, 0.5 dm³** | You picked B ✘
P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂. P₁ = 1.5 atm, V₁ = 2.0 dm³, T₁ = 400 K; P₂ = 3.0 atm, T₂ = 200 K.
V₂ = (P₁V₁T₂) / (T₁P₂) = (1.5 × 2.0 × 200) / (400 × 3.0) = 600 / 1200 = **0.5 dm³**.
(Doubling P halves V, and halving T halves V again, so V falls to ¼ of 2.0.)

**Q11. Escape velocity depends on**
**Answer: C, The radius of the Earth** | ✔
v = √(2GM/R) = √(2gR). It depends on the mass and radius of the planet (or g and R), not on the direction of launch, the shape of the object or the object's mass.

**Q12. Wire cutting through ice block that stays solid**
**Answer: D, Regelation** | ✔
Pressure under the wire lowers the melting point of ice, so it melts under the wire. The water flows above the wire, where pressure is normal, and refreezes. This melting under pressure and refreezing when pressure is removed is regelation.

**Q13. Substances that sublime**
**Answer: C, II, III and IV** | ✔
Benzoic acid, naphthalene and iodine change directly from solid to vapour on heating. Butanoic acid is a liquid at room temperature that boils; it does not sublime.

**Q14. Heat extracted cooling 200 g steam at 100 °C to water at 30 °C**
**Answer: A, 5.108 × 10⁵ J** | ✔
Step 1, condensing steam: Q₁ = mL = 0.2 kg × 2.26 × 10⁶ = 4.52 × 10⁵ J.
Step 2, cooling water 100 → 30 °C: Q₂ = mcΔθ = 200 × 4.2 × 70 = 58,800 J = 0.588 × 10⁵ J.
Total = 4.52 × 10⁵ + 0.588 × 10⁵ = **5.108 × 10⁵ J**.

**Q15. New gauge pressure of tyre**
**Answer: B, 278.27** | You picked D ✘
Gas laws use absolute pressure and kelvin. T₁ = 30 + 273 = 303 K; T₂ = 85 + 273 = 358 K. The numbers in the options are on a kPa scale with atmospheric pressure taken as 101.
P₁(abs) = 220 + 101 = 321. At constant volume, P₂ = P₁T₂/T₁ = 321 × 358 / 303 = 379.27 (this is the **absolute** pressure; it is option C, a trap). New gauge pressure = 379.27 − 101 = **278.27**. Option D (101) is just atmospheric pressure.

**Q16. Temperature rise of substance falling 20 m (c = 450)**
**Answer: D, 4/9 °C** | ✔
Loss in PE = heat gained: mgh = mcΔθ, so Δθ = gh / c = (10 × 20) / 450 = 200/450 = **4/9 °C**.

**Q17. Two equal capacitors: series ÷ parallel**
**Answer: C, 1/4** | You picked B ✘
Series: C_s = C × C / (C + C) = C/2. Parallel: C_p = C + C = 2C. Ratio C_s : C_p = (C/2) ÷ 2C = **1/4**.

**Q18. Minimum time to reach speed V with power P**
**Answer: C, MV²/2P** | ✔
Work done by the engine = KE gained = ½MV². Power = work ÷ time, so t = ½MV² / P = **MV²/2P**.

**Q19. What raises the boiling point of water**
**Answer: C, I** | You picked A ✘
- I: dissolving salt raises the boiling point (boiling-point elevation). True.
- II: higher altitude means lower pressure, so the boiling point **falls**. False.
- IV: a faster heat supply only boils the water faster; it does not change the boiling temperature. False.
- III: heating in a sealed flask does raise the pressure, which would raise the boiling point (the principle of a pressure cooker). The keyed answer counts only I.

**Q20. Initial temperature of hot water**
**Answer: C, 50 °C** | ✔
Let the hot water have mass m and temperature θ; the cold water is 3m at 10 °C; the final temperature is 20 °C. Heat lost = heat gained: mc(θ − 20) = 3mc(20 − 10). So θ − 20 = 30 and **θ = 50 °C**.

**Q21. Momentum of particles with the same speed**
**Answer: C, I & III only** | ✔
p = mv, and the speed is the same, so momentum depends on mass.
- I true: the alpha particle (about 4 u) is the heaviest.
- II false: the least momentum belongs to the electron/beta particle (mass about 1/1840 u), not the proton.
- III true: a beta particle is a fast electron, so it has the same mass and momentum as an electron.

**Q22. Increasing rate of oscillation of a pendulum**
**Answer: B, Decreasing the length** | ✔
T = 2π√(L/g), so frequency f = (1/2π)√(g/L). A shorter length gives a shorter period and a higher frequency. The mass has no effect.

**Q23. Radius in terms of v and ω**
**Answer: C, r = vω⁻¹** | ✔
v = ωr, so r = v/ω = vω⁻¹.

**Q24. Simplest electrostatic generator**
**Answer: B, Electrophorus** | ✔
An electrophorus (insulating plate and metal disc with an insulated handle) produces many charges by induction from one initial charging, so it is the simplest electrostatic generator. A lightning conductor protects buildings, a proof plane transfers charge for testing, and a gold-leaf electroscope detects charge.

**Q25. Heat conducted through iron plate per second**
**Answer: A, 184.4** | ✔
Q/t = kAΔθ / L = (59 × 0.5 × (140 − 15)) / 0.02 = (59 × 0.5 × 125) / 0.02 = 3687.5 / 0.02 = 184,375 W. This is **184.4 kW**, so the figure 184.4 in option A is the match. The unit printed with it is W; the magnitude is what identifies the option.

**Q26. Density of cylinder**
**Answer: D, 973 kg m⁻³** | ✔
V = πr²h = 3.14 × (0.3)² × 0.2 = 3.14 × 0.09 × 0.2 = 0.05652 m³. Density = m/V = 55 / 0.05652 = **973 kg m⁻³**.

**Q27. Efficiency of transformer**
**Answer: C, 78%** | You picked A ✘
Output power = 3 lamps × 40 W = 120 W. Input power = V × I = 220 × 0.7 = 154 W. Efficiency = (120 / 154) × 100 = 77.9%, which is **78%**.

**Q28. Work done by centripetal force**
**Answer: B, Zero** | ✔
Centripetal force acts towards the centre, perpendicular to the velocity (displacement). W = Fd cos90° = 0.

**Q29. Time to ascend 44 m at 200 J/s**
**Answer: C, 165 s** | ✔
Work = mgh = 75 × 10 × 44 = 33,000 J. Time = work ÷ power = 33,000 / 200 = **165 s**.

**Q30. Detecting and measuring radiation**
**Answer: D, Radiometer & Thermopile** | You picked B ✘
A radiometer's vanes turn when radiation falls on them, and the rate of rotation indicates intensity, so it detects and compares radiation. A thermopile (thermocouples in series) produces an e.m.f. proportional to the radiation it absorbs, so it detects and measures it. A Leslie cube is a source of radiation used to compare emitters, not a detector. Both of the first two qualify.

**Q31. Same unit as Young modulus**
**Answer: C, I, III, IV & V** | ✔
Young modulus = stress ÷ strain, and strain has no unit, so its unit is N m⁻² (Pa), the same as stress (I), pressure (III), bulk modulus (IV) and modulus of elasticity (V, the general name for Young, bulk and shear moduli). Strain (II) is dimensionless. Option E leaves out V, which also has the pascal as its unit.

**Q32. Fluid property for choosing a lubricant**
**Answer: A, Viscosity** | ✔
Viscosity is a fluid's resistance to flow. A lubricant must have suitable viscosity to form a film that reduces friction between moving parts.

**Q33. Speed of wave from the diagram**
**Answer: D, 10.0 m s⁻¹** | You picked A ✘
(The diagram was not in the file, so the figures cannot be re-read here.) The method is v = fλ, or v = λ/T. Read the wavelength λ as the distance between two successive crests (or troughs) on the diagram, and take the frequency from the period (f = 1/T). The keyed result is 10.0 m s⁻¹.

**Q34. Two pendulums of equal length, masses 5 g and 20 g**
**Answer: B, Ta = Tb** | ✔
T = 2π√(L/g) is independent of mass. Same length and same place give the same period.

**Q35. Electric potential energy between two protons**
**Answer: D, q²/4πε₀r** | You picked E ✘
For two point charges, U = q₁q₂ / 4πε₀r. For two protons, U = q² / 4πε₀r (r to the first power). Option E (with r²) is the Coulomb **force** expression, not energy.

**Q36. Node to adjacent antinode distance**
**Answer: D, 0.5 cm** | ✔
λ = v/f = 20 cm/s ÷ 10 Hz = 2 cm. Adjacent node-antinode distance = λ/4 = 2/4 = **0.5 cm**.

**Q37. Magnetic force on electron moving parallel to the field**
**Answer: D, 0 N** | ✔
F = qvB sinθ. The electron is projected **parallel** to the field, so θ = 0° and sin0° = 0, giving F = 0. (The 60° in the first sentence is a distractor. At 60° the force would be 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 5 × 10⁷ × 10 × sin60° = 6.9 × 10⁻¹¹ N, option A.)

**Q38. Total energy of ball just before hitting the ground**
**Answer: C, 15 J** | You picked A ✘
Take the ground as zero level. Energy at launch = KE + PE = ½(0.1)(10)² + (0.1)(10)(10) = 5 + 10 = 15 J. With no air resistance, mechanical energy is conserved, so just before impact the total energy is **15 J** (all of it kinetic). 5 J is only the initial KE.

**Q39. Gas compressed to one-fifth of its volume at constant T**
**Answer: D, 5P₀** | ✔
Boyle's law: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. P₀V₀ = P₂(V₀/5), so P₂ = **5P₀**.

**Q40. Force on catapult (m = 0.5 kg, e = 0.2 m, v = 40 m/s)**
**Answer: C, 4.0 × 10³ N** | You picked A ✘
KE of stone = ½mv² = ½ × 0.5 × 40² = 400 J. Elastic energy stored = ½Fe, because the force grows from 0 to F as the catapult is stretched. So ½ × F × 0.2 = 400, giving F = 800 / 0.2 = **4000 N = 4.0 × 10³ N**. (Using Fe = 400 gives 2000 N, which is the trap in option B.)
