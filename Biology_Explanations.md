**Q1. Evolutionary link between higher algae and pteridophytes**
**Answer: A, Bryophytes** | You picked D ✘
Bryophytes (mosses and liverworts) are the first land plants. They keep algal features (a simple thalloid body, water needed for fertilisation, a dominant gametophyte) but also show land-plant features such as multicellular sex organs and an embryo. They lead on to pteridophytes, which have true vascular tissue. Fungi are a separate kingdom with no chlorophyll, so they cannot be a link in plant evolution. Spermatophytes are seed plants that come later, and schizophytes are bacteria.

**Q2. Disease passed from a carrier mother to a son**
**Answer: B, Haemophilia** | ✔
Haemophilia is X-linked recessive. A carrier mother (XᴴXʰ) passes Xʰ to a son with a 50% chance, and he has no second X to mask it. Night blindness (vitamin A), beriberi (vitamin B₁) and kwashiorkor (protein) are deficiency diseases and are not inherited.

**Q3. Evolutionary link between higher algae and pteridophytes**
**Answer: D, Bryophytes** | ✔
This is the same question as Q1. Bryophytes link the algae and pteridophytes (see Q1).

**Q4. Blood group that cannot belong to the father**
**Answer: D, AB** | ✔
A child of group O has genotype ii, so each parent must give an *i* allele. The mother (group A) must be IAi. The father must carry *i*: A (IAi), B (IBi) and O (ii) all can. Group AB (IAIB) has no *i* allele, so he cannot be the father.

**Q5. Term not associated with fingerprint classification**
**Answer: B, Contour** | ✔
Fingerprint patterns are classified as arch, loop, whorl (and composite). "Contour" is not a fingerprint pattern.

**Q6. Vector of trypanosome**
**Answer: D, Tsetse fly** | You picked A ✘
Trypanosoma causes sleeping sickness and is transmitted by the tsetse fly (*Glossina*). The black fly (*Simulium*) transmits onchocerciasis (river blindness). *Aedes* transmits yellow fever and dengue. The housefly carries germs mechanically (cholera, typhoid).

**Q7. A space containing a group of interactive organisms**
**Answer: A, Community** | You picked D ✘
A community is all the different populations of organisms living and interacting in an area. A population is one species only. A habitat is the place where an organism lives, and an ecosystem is a community plus its non-living environment. The question names only the interacting organisms, so the answer is community.

**Q8. World biome found in Nigeria**
**Answer: D, Derived savanna** | ✔
Nigeria has mangrove/swamp forest, tropical rainforest, derived savanna, Guinea savanna, Sudan savanna and Sahel savanna. Tundra is a polar biome, Afro-alpine is a high-mountain vegetation of East Africa, and true desert is not a Nigerian biome.

**Q9. Never a secondary host of tapeworm**
**Answer: C, Man** | You picked D ✘
Man is the primary (definitive) host, where the adult tapeworm lives in the intestine. The secondary (intermediate) hosts are the pig (*Taenia solium*), cow (*Taenia saginata*) and fish (*Diphyllobothrium*). In the standard teaching, man is never the secondary host.

**Q10. How a samara differs from a cypsela**
**Answer: A, Extended pericarp** | ✔
In a samara the pericarp extends into a thin wing for wind dispersal (for example *Khaya*). A cypsela is a dry, one-seeded, indehiscent fruit from an inferior ovary, often with a hairy pappus (as in *Tridax* and sunflower). The hairy outgrowth (D) belongs to the cypsela, so it is not what the samara has.

**Q11. Coconut and oil palm fruits**
**Answer: B, Drupe** | You picked D ✘
A drupe has a fleshy or fibrous mesocarp, a hard stony endocarp around a single seed, and a thin exocarp. Coconut and oil palm both fit. A nut is a dry, hard, indehiscent fruit with no fleshy layer.

**Q12. Why a leaf is boiled in alcohol in the starch test**
**Answer: D, Decolorize the leaf** | ✔
Alcohol dissolves chlorophyll, removing the green colour so the blue-black colour of the iodine-starch reaction can be seen. (Boiling in water first kills the cells and softens the leaf.)

**Q13. Organism on bare rock with algae and fungus features**
**Answer: C, Lichen** | ✔
A lichen is a symbiosis between an alga (makes food by photosynthesis) and a fungus (holds water, gives support). It is a pioneer on bare rock.

**Q14. Not likely found in cells of ripe tomato**
**Answer: B, Chlorophyll** | You picked A ✘
As a tomato ripens, chlorophyll breaks down and chloroplasts change to chromoplasts, which hold the red pigment lycopene. Plastids (chromoplasts), cellulose cell walls and mitochondria are all still present.

**Q15. Artificial stimulation of plant growth**
**Answer: A, Gibberellin** | You picked C ✘
Gibberellins promote stem elongation, bolting and germination, and can overcome dwarfism, so they are the one commonly applied to stimulate growth. Ethylene mainly promotes fruit ripening and leaf fall and tends to restrain elongation.

**Q16. Plant waste used to turn hide into leather**
**Answer: C, Tannin** | ✔
Tannins (from barks such as acacia and mangrove) bind to proteins in the hide. This stops decay and makes it tough and flexible, which is tanning.

**Q17. Purple colour with NaOH and a drop of CuSO₄**
**Answer: C, Protein** | ✔
This is the biuret test. Peptide bonds form a purple/violet complex with Cu²⁺ in alkali.

**Q18. Evidence that a tooth is living**
**Answer: A, Pulp cavity** | You picked B ✘
The pulp cavity contains blood vessels and nerves, which supply the tooth with food and oxygen and give it sensation. Cement fixes the root in the jaw, enamel is the hard dead covering, and the gum is the tissue around the tooth.

**Q19. Prevention of excessive water loss from leaves**
**Answer: D, Cuticle** | You picked B ✘
The cuticle is a waxy waterproof layer on the epidermis that reduces evaporation. The mesophyll carries out photosynthesis, the vascular bundles conduct, the blade is the whole lamina and parenchyma is a general tissue.

**Q20. Tissue directly surrounding the stele in the root**
**Answer: B, Endodermis** | ✔
The endodermis is the innermost layer of the cortex and forms a ring around the stele. Its Casparian strip controls water entry into the stele. The pericycle lies inside the stele.

**Q21. Nutrition of sundew and bladderwort**
**Answer: C, Holozoic** | ✔
These are insectivorous plants. They trap insects and digest them with enzymes to obtain nitrogen compounds, which is animal-like (holozoic) feeding. They also photosynthesise, but the trapping and digestion of prey is what defines their mode.

**Q22. What strengthens chitin in arthropod exoskeletons**
**Answer: B, Proteins** | You picked C ✘
The cuticle is made of chitin embedded in a protein matrix. Cross-linking (tanning) of the protein hardens it. Calcium salts add hardness in crustaceans, but for arthropods in general the strengthening component is protein.

**Q23. Companion cell is found beside**
**Answer: C, Sieve tube** | You picked A ✘
Each companion cell lies next to a sieve-tube element in the phloem. It has a nucleus and many mitochondria and supports the sieve tube, which has none.

**Q24. Wide pith in the centre**
**Answer: A, Dicot stems and monocot roots** | You picked D ✘
Dicot stems have a ring of vascular bundles around a large central pith. Monocot roots have a ring of vascular tissue around a large central pith. Dicot roots have xylem in the centre with no pith. Monocot stems have scattered bundles in ground tissue and no distinct pith.

**Q25. Atlas vertebra distinguishing feature**
**Answer: C, Complete absence of neural spine** | ✔
The atlas (first cervical vertebra) is a ring with no centrum and no neural spine, and it carries facets for the skull. Every vertebra has a neural canal and a neural arch, and the odontoid process belongs to the axis (second cervical vertebra).

**Q26. Hormone that can cause anxiety**
**Answer: D, Epinephrine** | ✔
Epinephrine (adrenaline) triggers the fight-or-flight response: faster heartbeat, sweating, trembling and anxiety.

**Q27. Oily substance in mammalian skin**
**Answer: B, Sebaceous gland** | ✔
Sebaceous glands, usually attached to hair follicles, secrete sebum, which oils and waterproofs hair and skin. Sweat glands secrete sweat.

**Q28. End product of glycolysis**
**Answer: B, Pyruvic acid** | ✔
Glycolysis (in the cytoplasm) breaks glucose (6C) into two pyruvic acid molecules (3C each), with a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH.

**Q29. Part of the eye supplying food and oxygen**
**Answer: C, Choroid** | ✔
The choroid is the dark, highly vascular layer between the sclera and the retina. Its blood vessels nourish the eye, and its pigment absorbs stray light.

**Q30. Gland on top of the kidneys**
**Answer: B, Adrenal gland** | You picked C ✘
The adrenal (suprarenal) glands sit on top of each kidney. The pituitary is at the base of the brain, and the thyroid and parathyroids are in the neck.

**Q31. Muscles attached to bones by**
**Answer: A, Tendon** | ✔
Tendons are tough inelastic fibres joining muscle to bone. Ligaments join bone to bone.

**Q32. Locomotion in Euglena**
**Answer: A, Whipping action of its flagellum** | ✔
Euglena has one long flagellum that lashes like a whip to pull the cell through water. It has no cilia or pseudopodia.

**Q33. Stage when chromosomes line up at the equator**
**Answer: C, Metaphase** | You picked B ✘
At metaphase the chromosomes line up on the equatorial plate, each attached by its centromere to spindle fibres. At prophase they condense and the nuclear envelope breaks down. At anaphase chromatids move to the poles. At telophase new nuclear envelopes form.

**Q34. Continuous variation in humans**
**Answer: C, Height** | ✔
Continuous variation shows a full range of values between extremes, and height is controlled by many genes and the environment. Sex, Rh factor and blood group fall into distinct classes (discontinuous).

**Q35. Donor to any patient**
**Answer: D, Group O** | ✔
Group O red cells carry no A or B antigens, so they do not clump in any recipient. This is the universal donor group.

**Q36. Nerves that carry impulses to the brain**
**Answer: A, Afferent nerves** | You picked C ✘
Afferent (sensory) nerves carry impulses from receptors to the brain and spinal cord. Efferent (motor) nerves carry them from the CNS to effectors. Relay (intermediate) neurons connect sensory to motor neurons inside the CNS.

**Q37. Pineapple fruit**
**Answer: D, Multiple, succulent and indehiscent** | ✔
A pineapple develops from a whole inflorescence of many flowers fused together, so it is multiple (composite). It is fleshy (succulent) and does not split open (indehiscent). An aggregate fruit comes from one flower with many separate carpels.

**Q38. Part above the soil in epigeal germination**
**Answer: B, Cotyledon** | You picked C ✘
In epigeal germination the hypocotyl elongates, arches and pulls the cotyledons above the soil (for example in bean and castor oil seed). The cotyledons are the part brought above the surface. In hypogeal germination the epicotyl grows and the cotyledons stay below ground.

**Q39. Site of fertilisation in humans**
**Answer: B, Upper part of the oviduct** | You picked D ✘
The ovum is picked up by the fimbriae and fertilised in the upper oviduct (the ampulla). The zygote then travels down to the uterus and implants.

**Q40. Excretory product in amphibians**
**Answer: C, Ammonia** | ✔
Amphibians in their aquatic stages (tadpoles) excrete mostly ammonia, which is very toxic but easily diluted and lost in water through the moist skin. Adult frogs living on land convert more of it to urea, which is why some texts list urea. The keyed answer here is ammonia.
