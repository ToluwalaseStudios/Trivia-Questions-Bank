# TOPIC 2: THE CELL AND ITS ENVIRONMENT
(Diffusion, Osmosis, Plasmolysis, Turgidity — JAMB Past Questions)

Q: A Spirogyra cell was placed in solution X. After 1 minute the cell had been plasmolysed. Solution X was
A) tap water
B) a very strong sugar solution
C) a weak sugar solution
D) a very weak salt solution
E) distilled water
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Plasmolysis (shrinking of the cell contents away from the cell wall) happens when a plant cell loses water to its surroundings by osmosis. This only occurs when the cell is placed in a strongly hypertonic solution — that is, a solution with a much higher solute concentration (much lower water potential) than the cell sap, such as a very strong sugar solution. Tap water, weak solutions, and distilled water are all more dilute than cell sap and would cause water to move into the cell instead.

Q: The movement of molecules from a region of higher concentration to one of lower concentration is
A) diffusion
B) transpiration
C) osmosis
D) translocation
E) plasmolysis
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: This is the standard definition of diffusion — the net movement of any type of molecule (not just water) from where it is more concentrated to where it is less concentrated, down a concentration gradient. Osmosis is a special case of diffusion that applies specifically to water across a semi-permeable membrane; the other terms describe unrelated processes (water loss from leaves, movement of food in phloem, and cell shrinkage).

Q: A fresh kolanut weighing 1g was put into a salt solution. After 3 hours, the kolanut weighed 1.2g. This simple experiment demonstrates that
A) the salt solution is more concentrated than the cell sap of the kolanut
B) the cell sap of the kolanut is more concentrated than the salt solution
C) the salt solution has the same concentration as the cell sap of the kolanut
D) kolanut cells can multiply in water
E) the kolanut lost some water molecules to the salt solution
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Since the kolanut gained mass (from 1g to 1.2g), it must have absorbed water from the salt solution by osmosis. Water moves into a cell only when the surrounding solution is more dilute (has a higher water potential) than the cell's own contents — meaning the kolanut's cell sap was more concentrated (more negative water potential) than the salt solution around it.

Q: The filaments of Spirogyra were put into equal volumes of sugar solutions X and Y. After 5 minutes, filaments from solution X had their cell contents rounded up, while those from Y were unaffected. The experiment shows that
A) solution X was stronger than Y
B) solution Y was stronger than X
C) Spirogyra used up the sugar in solution Y
D) solution X is good for stimulating sexual reproduction in Spirogyra
E) solution Y was less contaminated than X
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Cell contents "rounding up" and pulling away from the cell wall is a classic sign of plasmolysis, which is caused by a strongly hypertonic (concentrated) external solution drawing water out of the cell. Since only the filaments in solution X plasmolysed, solution X must have been more concentrated ("stronger") than solution Y, which was too dilute to cause any visible water loss.

Q: If an isolated living cell is left in distilled water for two hours, it is likely to
A) lose some of its water to the surrounding water
B) lose all of its water to the surrounding water
C) reproduce by binary fission
D) become more turgid
E) die due to excess water
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Distilled water is extremely hypotonic (has no solutes, so it has a very high water potential) compared to the cell's contents, so water will move into the cell by osmosis. If the cell in question is a plant cell, its rigid cellulose cell wall resists the resulting internal pressure, so instead of bursting, the cell simply becomes firm and swollen — that is, it becomes turgid.

Q: If an isolated living cell is left in distilled water for two hours, it is likely to
A) lose some of its water to the surrounding water
B) lose all of its water to the surrounding water
C) reproduce by binary fission
D) become more turgid
E) die due to excess water
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: This is the same basic scenario as the previous question, but here the answer assumes a cell without a rigid wall (for example, an animal cell or a naked protozoan). Without a cell wall to resist the build-up of internal pressure caused by continuous water entry, the cell keeps swelling until its membrane ruptures, killing the cell — this is why the two nearly identical past questions have different "correct" answers depending on whether a wall is present (compare with the previous question, where turgidity was possible because of the cell wall).

Q: Which of the following will NOT allow osmosis to take place?
A) pig's bladder
B) cellophane
C) parchment paper
D) transparent polythene
E) cow's bladder
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Osmosis can only occur across a semi-permeable (partially permeable) membrane — one that lets small molecules like water pass through but not larger solute molecules. Pig's bladder, cellophane, parchment paper, and cow's bladder are all naturally semi-permeable. Transparent polythene, however, is completely impervious — it does not allow any molecules through at all — so no osmosis can take place across it.

Q: When a Spirogyra cell is immersed in a salt solution more concentrated than its cell sap, it
A) remains unchanged
B) takes up water and bursts
C) absorbs a little water
D) loses water and shrivels
E) becomes turgid
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A salt solution that is more concentrated than the cell sap is hypertonic to the cell, meaning it has a lower water potential. Water will therefore move out of the Spirogyra cell into the salt solution by osmosis, causing the cell to lose water and shrivel (plasmolyse).

Q: Osmosis can be defined as the movement of
A) molecules from a solution of high concentration to low concentration
B) molecules from a solution of low concentration to high concentration
C) water from a solution of high concentration to low concentration
D) water across a semi-permeable membrane from a solution of low (solute) concentration to high (solute) concentration
E) water across a semi-permeable membrane from a solution of high (solute) concentration to low (solute) concentration
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Osmosis is specifically the movement of water (not solute molecules in general) across a semi-permeable membrane, and it moves from a region where the solute concentration is low (and water potential is high) toward a region where the solute concentration is high (and water potential is low). This precise combination of conditions — water only, semi-permeable membrane, and direction relative to solute concentration — is exactly what option D describes.

Q: Mineral salts can be absorbed into the roots by
A) osmosis only
B) osmosis and diffusion
C) diffusion and active transport
D) diffusion only
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Mineral salts (being solutes, not water) cannot be taken up by osmosis, which applies only to water. Instead, roots absorb mineral salts partly by diffusion (when soil solution concentration is higher outside than inside the root hair) and, importantly, by active transport, which uses metabolic energy (ATP from respiration) to move ions into the root even against their concentration gradient.

Q: The process by which water moves from X to Y through the semi-permeable membrane is called
A) diffusion
B) osmosis
C) active diffusion
D) osmoregulation
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Any movement of water (specifically) across a semi-permeable membrane, from a region of higher water potential to one of lower water potential, is by definition osmosis — regardless of which particular set-up or diagram it is shown in.

Q: The process by which a red blood cell placed in distilled water absorbs water until it bursts and releases its contents into the surroundings is known as
A) osmosis
B) plasmolysis
C) turgidity
D) haemolysis
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A red blood cell has no cell wall, so when placed in distilled water it takes in water by osmosis (a hypotonic environment) and keeps swelling until its membrane ruptures. This specific bursting of red blood cells due to excessive water uptake has its own special name: haemolysis. (Osmosis is the general water-movement process involved, but "haemolysis" is the more precise term for what happens to the cell itself.)

Q: Salts and water are absorbed in the roots and transported to the leaves by
A) diffusion through the xylem tissues
B) osmosis through the phloem tissues
C) diffusion through the phloem tissues
D) osmosis through the xylem tissues
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Water enters root hair cells by osmosis and is then transported upward through the xylem tissue (in a continuous transpiration stream) to the leaves, carrying dissolved mineral salts along with it by mass flow. The phloem, in contrast, transports manufactured food (like sugars), not water absorbed from the soil.

Q: When a marine fish was taken from the ocean and put in a tank of fresh water, it died after a short period because
A) the tank was too small compared to the large ocean
B) the body cells of the fish swelled and burst as a result of the hypotonic fresh water
C) the body cells of the fish shrank as their sap was hypertonic to the fresh water
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A marine fish's body fluids are normally in balance with the salty (concentrated) seawater around it. Fresh water is hypotonic in comparison, so once the fish is transferred into it, water rushes into the fish's body cells by osmosis faster than it can be excreted, causing the cells to swell and eventually burst — leading to the fish's death.

Q: Which of the following results is to be expected if the set-up is left for several hours?
A) decrease in the size of the yam cup
B) movement of the salt solution into the surrounding water
C) decrease in the volume of the surrounding water
D) movement of water from the salt solution into the surrounding dish
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Because the salt solution inside the yam cup is more concentrated (hypertonic) than the water in the surrounding dish, water moves by osmosis out of the dish and into the yam cup, through the semi-permeable yam tissue. Over several hours this causes a noticeable decrease in the volume of water remaining in the outer dish, while the salt solution level inside the yam cup rises.

Q: What will happen when two equal-sized pieces of pawpaw, labelled X and Y, are dropped into equal quantities of concentrated salt solution and distilled water respectively?
A) pawpaw X will become turgid
B) both will increase in size
C) pawpaw Y will become turgid
D) both will decrease in size
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Pawpaw X, sitting in a concentrated (hypertonic) salt solution, will lose water by osmosis and become flaccid/shrunken, not turgid. Pawpaw Y, sitting in distilled water (strongly hypotonic), will absorb water by osmosis; because it is plant tissue with a cell wall, it becomes firm and swollen — that is, turgid — rather than bursting.

Q: A marine protozoan is unlikely to have a contractile vacuole mainly because its cytoplasm is
A) isotonic to sea water
B) hypotonic to sea water
C) hypertonic to sea water
D) impervious to sea water
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A contractile vacuole exists to pump out excess water that continually enters a cell by osmosis. If a marine protozoan's cytoplasm is isotonic with (has the same solute concentration as) the surrounding seawater, there is no net osmotic gain of water, so the organism has no need to actively expel excess water and can manage without a contractile vacuole.

Q: Which of the following can cause shrinkage of living cells?
A) deionized water
B) hypertonic solution
C) hypotonic solution
D) isotonic solution
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A hypertonic solution has a higher solute concentration (lower water potential) than the cell's contents, so it draws water out of the cell by osmosis (ex-osmosis), causing the cell to shrink. Deionized/distilled water and hypotonic solutions instead cause cells to gain water and swell, while isotonic solutions cause no net water movement at all.
