Q: Which of these is not a characteristic of wind pollinated flowers?
A) Smaller quantities of sticky pollen produced
B) Feathery styles of stigmas pendulously hang outside the flower
C) Small, inconspicuous scentless flowers
D) Large anthers which are loosely attached to filaments
E) Light pollen grains produced in large quantities from anthers
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Wind-pollinated flowers actually produce very large quantities of light, smooth, powdery pollen (since wind dispersal is inefficient and needs abundant pollen to succeed), not "smaller quantities" — this makes option A false. Feathery stigmas, small scentless flowers, loosely attached anthers, and abundant light pollen are all genuine wind-pollination features.

Q: A germinating seed requires oxygen for which of the following essential processes?
A) converting carbohydrate into glucose
B) transporting energy from one part of the plant to another
C) production of energy by oxidizing essential carbohydrates
D) hydrolysis of proteins
E) the formation of water molecules within the germinating seed
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Oxygen is required specifically for aerobic respiration, which oxidizes stored carbohydrate reserves to release the energy a germinating seed needs to fuel its growth.

Q: The region of cell division in a root is A. root cap B. endodermis C. xylem D. piliferous layer E. meristem
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The meristematic region (just behind the protective root cap) contains actively dividing cells responsible for root growth — the other structures listed are more mature, differentiated tissues.

Q: Which of the statements below is NOT characteristic of wind pollinated flowers?
A) Stigmas are usually large and feathery
B) Nectary is usually absent
C) The pollen grains have rough spiny surfaces
D) The flowers are not scented
E) The pollen is light and smooth
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Wind-pollinated pollen is characteristically light and smooth (an aerodynamic adaptation for easy wind dispersal), not rough and spiny — spiny/sticky pollen surfaces are instead an insect-pollination adaptation, for clinging to pollinators' bodies.

Q: The plumule is best described as
A) part of the cotyledon
B) part of the endosperm
C) a miniature shoot
D) structure near the micropyle
E) structure opposite the hilum
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The plumule is the embryonic structure within a seed that will develop into the young shoot system, making "a miniature shoot" its most accurate description.

B) caryopsis

E) follicle
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A samara is specifically defined as a dry, single-seeded, indehiscent fruit with a wing-like extension of the pericarp (as seen in Dipterocarpus), distinguishing it from the other listed fruit types.

Q: In which of the following tissues do the cells have the ability to divide?
A) Meristem
B) Sclerenchyma
C) Phloem
D) Collenchyma
E) Pith
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Meristematic tissue is specifically composed of undifferentiated, actively dividing cells capable of cell division (mitosis) to generate new plant growth — sclerenchyma, phloem, collenchyma, and pith are all more mature, differentiated tissues.

Q: The maize grain is regarded as a fruit and not a seed because
A) it is covered by a sheath of leaves
B) the testa and fruit wall fuse after fertilization
C) it has both endosperm and cotyledon
D) it has coleorhiza and coleoptile
E) the pericarp and seed coat are separate
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In a maize grain, the seed coat (testa) and the fruit wall (pericarp) become tightly fused together into a single layer after fertilization — this fusion of "seed" and "fruit" tissue is precisely why the maize grain (a caryopsis) is technically classified as a fruit rather than simply a naked seed.

Q: A true fruit is formed from

Q: Water is required for seed germination to take place because it
A) activates the enzymes
B) softens the testa
C) liberates energy for growth
D) permits radicle growth
E) allows oxygen to diffuse into the seed
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: While water does contribute to several germination processes (softening the seed coat, enabling diffusion, aiding growth), its most fundamental and primary role is activating the dormant enzymes within the seed, which then drive the mobilization of stored food reserves needed to fuel germination.

Q: For pollination and fruit formation, the essential part(s) of the flower should be the

C) pistil (gynoecium)

ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The pistil (gynoecium, the female reproductive structure comprising stigma, style, and ovary) is the essential part required for pollination to lead to fertilization and subsequent fruit formation — the corolla and receptacle are non-essential (accessory) floral parts.

Q: If the anthers of a flower mature before the stigma, the condition is termed
A) epigyny

D) protandry
E) perigyny
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Protandry specifically describes the condition where the male parts (anthers) of a flower mature and release pollen before the female stigma becomes receptive — a mechanism that helps promote cross-pollination by avoiding self-fertilization timing.
