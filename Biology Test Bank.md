# BIOLOGY QUESTIONS & EXPLANATIONS

Q1.
Question: The evolutionary link between higher algae and pteridophytes is provided by
A. Bryophytes
B. Spermatophytes
C. Schizophytes
D. Fungi
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Bryophytes (mosses and liverworts) represent the evolutionary transition from aquatic algae to terrestrial vascular plants (pteridophytes). They share features with algae (thalloid plant body, dependence on water for fertilization) and exhibit land-plant adaptations (multicellular jacketed sex organs and embryo retention).

Q2.
Question: Which of the following conditions is a sex-linked genetic disorder passed from a carrier mother to her son?
A. Night blindness
B. Haemophilia
C. Beriberi
D. Kwashiorkor
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Haemophilia is an X-linked recessive bleeding disorder. A heterozygous carrier mother ($X^H X^h$) passes the recessive mutated allele ($X^h$) to her son with a $50\%$ probability. Because males are hemizygous ($X^h Y$), the trait is expressed. Beriberi, kwashiorkor, and night blindness are nutritional deficiencies.

Q3.
Question: Which plant group forms the primary producer foundation of marine food webs?
A. Angiosperms
B. Phytoplankton
C. Bryophytes
D. Pteridophytes
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Phytoplankton (diatoms and dinoflagellates) are microscopic photosynthetic organisms floating in aquatic ecosystems. They act as the primary producers that synthesize organic compounds forming the base of marine food webs.

Q4.
Question: A mother with blood group A gives birth to a child with blood group O. Which of the following blood groups cannot belong to the biological father?
A. Group A
B. Group B
C. Group O
D. Group AB
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: A child with blood group O has genotype $ii$ and must inherit an $i$ allele from both parents. A mother of group A must have genotype $I^A i$. The father must possess at least one $i$ allele. A father of group AB has genotype $I^A I^B$ and carries no $i$ allele; thus, he cannot father a child of group O.

Q5.
Question: Which pattern term is not used in dermatoglyphic fingerprint classification?
A. Whorl
B. Contour
C. Arch
D. Loop
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Standard fingerprint classification recognizes three main pattern types: arches, loops, and whorls (plus composite patterns). "Contour" is a topographic term and not a fingerprint classification category.

Q6.
Question: The biological vector of *Trypanosoma gambiense* is the
A. black fly
B. Aedes mosquito
C. housefly
D. tsetse fly
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: *Trypanosoma gambiense*, the protozoan parasite responsible for African sleeping sickness (trypanosomiasis), is transmitted to humans through the bite of infected tsetse flies (*Glossina* species).

Q7.
Question: An assemblage of populations of different species interacting within a defined physical area is called a
A. community
B. habitat
C. ecosystem
D. population
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: A biological community consists of all interacting populations of different species living together in a specific habitat. An ecosystem includes both the biotic community and its abiotic environment.

Q8.
Question: Which terrestrial biome covers vast areas of central and southern Nigeria?
A. Afro-alpine
B. Tundra
C. Desert
D. Derived savanna
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Derived savanna is a major ecological zone in southern and central Nigeria created through deforestation, farming, and burning of original tropical rainforest margins.

Q9.
Question: Which of the following organisms is the definitive host, rather than an intermediate host, of *Taenia saginata*?
A. Cow
B. Pig
C. Human
D. Fish
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Humans serve as the definitive (primary) host for *Taenia saginata* (beef tapeworm), harbouring adult tapeworms in the small intestine. Cattle serve as intermediate hosts housing encysted larvae (*Cysticercus bovis*).

Q10.
Question: A samara fruit differs structurally from a cypsela fruit by having an
A. extended pericarp modified into a wing
B. excessively hardened pericarp
C. inner pericarp fused with the seed coat
D. apical pappus of hairy outgrowths
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: A samara is a dry indehiscent fruit with a pericarp extended into a fibrous membrane or wing for wind dispersal (e.g. *Khaya*). A cypsela (e.g. *Tridax*) features an apical pappus formed from a modified calyx.

Q11.
Question: Coconut and oil palm fruits are botanically classified as
A. capsules
B. drupes
C. berries
D. nuts
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Drupes are simple fleshy fruits with a thin exocarp, a fibrous or fleshy mesocarp, and a hard woody endocarp enclosing a single seed. Both coconut and oil palm match this structure.

Q12.
Question: In testing a green leaf for starch, the leaf is boiled in ethanol to
A. kill leaf cells
B. soften cell walls
C. increase membrane permeability
D. extract chlorophyll pigments
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Boiling in ethanol dissolves and extracts chlorophyll pigments, decolourizing the leaf so that the blue-black colour change of the iodine reaction can be clearly observed.

Q13.
Question: A pioneer organism colonizing bare rock surfaces consisting of a symbiotic association of algae and fungi is a
A. epiphyte
B. mycorrhiza
C. lichen
D. fern
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Lichens represent a mutualistic symbiotic association between a photosynthetic partner (alga or cyanobacterium) and a fungus. They secrete organic acids that weather bare rock surfaces during primary succession.

Q14.
Question: Which cellular component breaks down and disappears in the pericarp cells of a fully ripe red tomato?
A. Plastids
B. Chlorophyll
C. Cellulose cell wall
D. Mitochondria
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: During fruit ripening, chloroplasts transform into chromoplasts. Chlorophyll decomposes completely while carotenoid pigments (such as lycopene) accumulate, giving ripe tomatoes their red colour.

Q15.
Question: Which plant hormone is applied to genetically dwarf plants to stimulate stem elongation?
A. Gibberellin
B. Auxin
C. Ethylene
D. Cytokinin
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Gibberellins promote internodal stem elongation, overcome genetic dwarfism in plants like dwarf peas, and break seed dormancy.

Q16.
Question: A plant secondary metabolite used commercially in tanning animal hides into leather is
A. gum
B. alkaloid
C. tannin
D. resin
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Tannins are polyphenolic plant compounds extracted from bark (e.g. acacia, mangrove) that cross-link collagen proteins in raw animal hides, preventing decay and converting hides into leather.

Q17.
Question: A food sample treated with sodium hydroxide solution followed by copper(II) sulphate solution turns purple. This confirms the presence of
A. carbohydrates
B. lipids
C. proteins
D. reducing sugars
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The biuret test detects peptide bonds in proteins. Alkaline copper(II) sulphate reacts with peptide bonds to form a characteristic violet-purple coordination complex.

Q18.
Question: Direct evidence that a mammalian tooth is a living organ is provided by the presence of blood vessels and nerves within the
A. pulp cavity
B. cementum
C. enamel
D. periodontal ligament
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: The central pulp cavity contains living vascular connective tissue, nerve fibres, and blood vessels that supply nutrients and oxygen to odontoblasts and mediate sensation. Enamel is non-living mineralized tissue.

Q19.
Question: Excessive transpiration loss from leaf surfaces is restricted by the
A. leaf lamina
B. mesophyll
C. vascular bundle
D. cuticle
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: The cuticle is a hydrophobic, waxy layer secreted by epidermal cells covering leaves and stems, serving as a physical barrier to cuticular water loss.

Q20.
Question: In a dicotyledonous root, the layer of cells immediately surrounding the vascular stele is the
A. parenchyma
B. endodermis
C. pericycle
D. cortex
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The endodermis forms the innermost boundary layer of the root cortex directly surrounding the central stele. Its radial walls feature waterproof Casparian strips regulating solute transport into the vascular bundle.

Q21.
Question: Carnivorous plants like sundew (*Drosera*) and bladderwort (*Utricularia*) exhibit a nutritional mechanism categorized as
A. autotrophic
B. saprophytic
C. holozoic ingestion of animal prey
D. chemosynthetic
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Although carnivorous plants photosynthesize (autotrophic for carbon), they trap, ingest, and enzymatically digest insects and small animals (holozoic feeding) to absorb essential nitrogen and phosphorus compounds.

Q22.
Question: The structural toughness of chitin in arthropod exoskeletons is reinforced primarily by cross-linked
A. lipids
B. proteins
C. nucleic acids
D. organic salts
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The arthropod cuticle consists of microfibrils of chitin embedded in a protein matrix. Sclerotization (tanning) cross-links these structural proteins, producing a rigid exoskeleton.

Q23.
Question: In dicotyledonous stems, each companion cell is physically adjacent to a
A. endodermal cell
B. xylem vessel element
C. sieve-tube element
D. pericyclic fibre
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Companion cells develop alongside sieve-tube elements in phloem tissue, connected via plasmodesmata. They maintain metabolic functions for mature sieve elements lacking nuclei.

Q24.
Question: A wide central parenchymatous pith is a characteristic feature of cross-sections of
A. dicot stems and monocot roots
B. dicot stems and monocot stems
C. dicot roots and monocot roots
D. dicot roots and monocot stems
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Dicot stems possess a ring of vascular bundles surrounding a large central pith. Monocot roots feature a polyarch vascular ring surrounding a distinct central pith. Dicot roots have central star-shaped xylem without a pith, and monocot stems have scattered bundles in ground tissue.

Q25.
Question: The atlas vertebra is distinguished from other cervical vertebrae by the
A. presence of an odontoid process
B. presence of a neural canal
C. complete absence of a neural spine
D. presence of a neural arch
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The first cervical vertebra (atlas) lacks a true centrum and neural spine, existing as a ring of bone with large articular facets that articulate with occipital condyles of the skull. The odontoid process belongs to the axis (second cervical vertebra).

Q26.
Question: The hormone secreted during emergency situations that triggers acute anxiety and elevated heart rate is
A. thyroxine
B. progesterone
C. insulin
D. epinephrine
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Epinephrine (adrenaline), secreted by the adrenal medulla during stress or danger, activates the sympathetic nervous system, inducing fight-or-flight responses including tachycardia and anxiety.

Q27.
Question: Sebum, an oily substance that lubricates mammalian hair and skin, is produced by
A. Malpighian glands
B. sebaceous glands
C. sweat glands
D. subcutaneous fat layer
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Sebaceous glands are exocrine glands located in the dermis associated with hair follicles. They secrete sebum, an oily lipid mixture that waterproofs skin and prevents hair desiccation.

Q28.
Question: The three-carbon organic acid produced as the end product of glycolysis is
A. aspartic acid
B. pyruvic acid
C. citric acid
D. ethanoic acid
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Glycolysis breaks down one mole of 6-carbon glucose in the cytoplasm into two moles of 3-carbon pyruvic acid (pyruvate), generating net 2 ATP and 2 NADH molecules.

Q29.
Question: The vascular layer of the mammalian eye that supplies nutrients and oxygen to the retina is the
A. cornea
B. sclera
C. choroid
D. ciliary body
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: The choroid is a dark, heavily pigmented and highly vascularized layer situated between the sclera and retina, supplying oxygen and metabolic nutrients to outer retinal layers.

Q30.
Question: Which endocrine gland is situated directly on the superior pole of each kidney?
A. Thyroid gland
B. Adrenal gland
C. Pituitary gland
D. Parathyroid gland
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: The adrenal (suprarenal) glands lie attached to the upper anterior border of each kidney, comprising an outer cortex and inner medulla.

Q31.
Question: Skeletal muscles are anchored to bones by tough, inelastic connective tissue bands called
A. tendons
B. ligaments
C. cartilage
D. synovial membranes
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Tendons are dense bundles of collagenous connective tissue that attach skeletal muscle to bone. Ligaments connect bone to bone at joints.

Q32.
Question: *Euglena gracilis* achieves forward locomotion primarily through the
A. whipping motion of its flagellum
B. synchronized beating of cilia
C. extension of pseudopodia
D. movement of myonemes alone
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: *Euglena* moves through aquatic environments via the undulatory whipping action of a long anterior flagellum that pulls the cell forward.

Q33.
Question: During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes align along the equatorial plane of the cell?
A. Anaphase
B. Prophase
C. Metaphase
D. Telophase
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: At metaphase, condensed chromosomes align along the equatorial plane (metaphase plate), with spindle fibers attached to centromere kinetochores.

Q34.
Question: Which of the following human phenotypic traits demonstrates continuous variation?
A. Sex
B. Rhesus blood factor
C. Height
D. ABO blood group
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Height is a polygenic trait influenced by multiple genetic loci and environmental factors, exhibiting a continuous spectrum of phenotypes. Sex and blood groups display discontinuous variation.

Q35.
Question: An individual whose red blood cells carry neither A nor B surface antigens belongs to blood group
A. Group A
B. Group B
C. Group AB
D. Group O
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Group O blood contains red cells devoid of A and B agglutinogens, allowing safe donation to recipients of all ABO groups (universal donor).

36.
Question: Sensory nerve fibres that transmit electrical impulses from sensory receptors toward the central nervous system are described as
A. afferent nerves
B. motor nerves
C. relay neurons
D. efferent nerves
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Afferent (sensory) neurons transmit impulses from peripheral sensory receptors to the brain and spinal cord. Efferent (motor) neurons conduct impulses away from the CNS to effector organs.

Q37.
Question: The pineapple fruit (*Ananas comosus*) is botanically categorized as a
A. succulent aggregate indehiscent fruit
B. dehiscent aggregate fruit
C. dehiscent multiple fruit
D. succulent multiple indehiscent fruit
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Pineapple is a multiple (composite/syncarp) fruit formed by the fusion of an entire inflorescence of flowers around a fleshy central axis. It is fleshy (succulent) and does not split open at maturity (indehiscent).

Q38.
Question: During epigeal germination, which seed structure is carried above the soil surface?
A. Coleoptile
B. Cotyledon
C. Epicotyl
D. Radicle
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: In epigeal germination (e.g. cowpea), rapid elongation of the hypocotyl arches upward and pulls the cotyledons above the soil level.

Q39.
Question: Fertilization of the human ovum by a sperm cell normally occurs in the
A. lower body of the uterus
B. upper portion of the fallopian tube (oviduct)
C. lower segment of the oviduct
D. cervix
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Human fertilization normally occurs in the ampulla, situated in the upper portion of the fallopian tube (oviduct), shortly after ovulation.

Q40.
Question: What is the principal nitrogenous waste product excreted by adult terrestrial amphibians?
A. Uric acid
B. Ammonia
C. Urea
D. Amino acids
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Aquatic amphibian larvae (tadpoles) excrete toxic ammonia. During metamorphosis, terrestrial adult frogs and toads convert nitrogenous waste to less toxic urea for excretion by kidneys.
