Q: People suffering from myopia
A) can see near objects clearly
B) can see far away objects clearly
C) cannot see any objects clearly
D) are colour blind
E) are able to see better in the dark
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Myopia (short-sightedness) results from the eyeball being too long or the lens focusing images just in front of the retina, meaning near objects are seen clearly but distant objects appear blurred, since their focal point falls short of the retina.

Q: In a phototropic experiment, young seedlings in a box were subjected to light from one direction. The seedlings continued to grow erect. Which of the following statements is correct?
A) Only the tips of the seedlings received light
B) The light was not strong enough
C) The seedlings continued young
D) the tips of the seedlings may have been covered
E) The box containing the seedlings should have been placed on a laboratory bench
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Since phototropic bending specifically requires light to be detected at the growing tip (where auxin distribution is affected), seedlings continuing to grow straight up despite one-directional lighting suggests their tips were somehow covered/shielded from detecting the directional light source.

Q: When the ciliary muscle of the eye contracts, the eye lens
A) bulges
B) contracts
C) rotates
D) flattens
E) is rounded
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Contraction of the ciliary muscle slackens the suspensory ligaments holding the lens, allowing the lens's natural elasticity to make it bulge (become thicker and more rounded) — this increases its focusing power for viewing near objects.

Q: The part of the mammalian brain responsible for maintaining balance is
A) medulla oblongata

D) cerebrum

ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The cerebellum is specifically responsible for coordinating balance, posture, and fine motor control, integrating sensory input from the muscles, joints, eyes, and ears.

Q: Which part of the human brain is concerned with reflexes controlling the rate of heartbeat and breathing?

Q: The following are connected with the movement of a reflex action: (1) Central nervous system (2) Muscle (3) Skin (4) Sensory nerve (5) Motor nerve. Which of the following sequences indicates a correct path?
A) 1-2-3-4-5
B) 3-4-1-2-5
C) 3-4-1-2-5

ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: A correct reflex arc pathway runs: skin (receptor, 3) → sensory nerve (4) → central nervous system (1) → motor nerve (5) → muscle (effector, 2) — matching the sequence 3-4-1-5-2.

Q: The centre which controls respiratory activities in the mammalian brain is the
A) cerebrum

Q: The tips of some rice seedlings were cut off while some were left intact. Both were covered with a container which had only one small hole to allow light through. After twenty-four hours it was observed that
A) the cut tips bent away from light
B) the cut tips bent towards source of light
C) the intact tips bent away from the light
D) the intact tips bent towards light
E) both tips bent away from light
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Only seedlings with their tips intact can detect light and produce the uneven auxin distribution needed to bend toward it (positive phototropism) — the cut-tip seedlings, lacking the light-sensing region, showed no such directional bending response.

Q: Which of these instruments is used to demonstrate response of roots and shoots to gravity?
A) klinostat
B) Manometer
C) Potometer
D) Photometer
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A klinostat is a rotating device specifically used to demonstrate/study a plant's response to gravity (geotropism) by continuously and evenly rotating a plant to counteract the normal, one-directional pull of gravity on its growth.

Q: Accommodation of the human eye is best described as
A) ability to see distant objects
B) movement of upper and lower eyelids
C) focusing of near and distant objects
D) changing of lens shape due to varying light intensities
E) constant adjustment of the iris
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Accommodation is precisely defined as the eye's ability to adjust its lens shape (via the ciliary muscle) to bring both near and distant objects into sharp focus on the retina as needed.

Q: Growing yam tendrils climb for support. This growth response is

E) chemotropism
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Haptotropism (thigmotropism) is specifically the growth response to touch/contact, exactly describing how a climbing tendril curls and grips around a support structure it touches.

Q: The part of the mammalian eye that strongly bends light rays is the
A) cornea and the lens
B) cornea and aqueous humour
C) cornea and vitreous humour
D) lens and aqueous humor
E) lens and vitreous humor
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The cornea provides the majority of the eye's overall light-bending (refractive) power, with the lens providing the finer, adjustable focusing adjustments — together, the cornea and lens are the two structures most responsible for strongly bending incoming light rays to focus on the retina.

Q: External ears of mammals consist of
A) pinna and tympanum
B) pinna only
C) auditory meatus only
D) auditory meatus and pinna
E) auditory meatus and pinnaum
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The external ear of a mammal is correctly composed of both the pinna (the visible external flap) and the auditory (acoustic) meatus (the ear canal leading inward) — the tympanum (eardrum) itself actually marks the boundary with the middle ear, not part of the external ear structure itself.

Q: Which of these parts of the mammalian ear is directly concerned with the sense of balance?
A) Cochlea
B) Eustachian tube
C) Ear-drum
D) Pinna
E) Semicircular canals
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The semicircular canals, located in the inner ear, are specifically responsible for detecting rotational movement and maintaining the sense of balance, distinct from the cochlea (hearing), eustachian tube (pressure equalization), eardrum (sound transmission), or pinna (sound collection).

Q: The movement of part of a plant in response to an external stimulus of no particular direction is
A) taxism
B) tropism
C) haptotropic movement
D) nastic movement
E) phototropism
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Nastic movement is specifically defined as a plant's response to a stimulus that has no fixed direction (a non-directional response), unlike tropisms (directional growth responses) or taxis (whole-organism directional movement).

Q: In a positive phototropic response of a coleoptile, the region of greatest curvature is brought about by the
A) movement of auxins away from the region of curvature
B) even distribution of auxins in all parts of the coleoptile
C) inhibition of growth by auxins in the region of smaller curvature
D) concentration of auxins in the region of curvature
E) absence of auxins in the coleoptile
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: When light strikes a coleoptile from one side, auxin migrates toward and accumulates on the shaded side, stimulating that side's cells to elongate faster — this localized concentration of auxin causes the greatest curvature to occur specifically at that shaded region.

Q: The function of the ossicles (malleus, incus and stapes) in the mammalian ear is the

C) support of the inner ear
D) maintenance of balance during motion
E) secretion of oil
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The three tiny ossicle bones work together to mechanically transmit and amplify sound vibrations from the eardrum across the middle ear to the oval window of the inner ear.

Q: Taxism differs from tropism because
A) the whole organism is affected
B) it is a directional movement
C) it is a response to multi-directional stimuli
D) part of the organism is affected
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Taxis specifically involves movement of the whole organism (like a free-moving animal or unicellular organism) in response to a directional stimulus, whereas tropism involves only a directional growth response of part of a (typically rooted, immobile) organism, such as a plant.

Q: The main function of the choroid is
A) protection of the eye ball
B) transmission of light
C) supply of nutrients to tissues of the eye
D) converging light
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The choroid layer, being richly supplied with blood vessels (highly vascularized), primarily functions to supply nutrients and oxygen to the tissues of the eye, particularly the retina.

Q: What part of the central nervous system is concerned with answering an examination question?
A) Cerebrum
B) Cerebellum
C) Medulla oblongata
D) Spinal cord
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The cerebrum is the seat of higher cognitive functions such as intelligence, memory, reasoning, and thinking — all the mental processes required to answer an examination question, distinguishing it from the cerebellum (balance/coordination), medulla (involuntary functions), or spinal cord (reflexes/signal relay).

Q: The region that controls most of the unconscious processes of a mammalian body is the
A) cerebellum
B) cerebrum
C) spinal cord
D) medulla oblongata
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The medulla oblongata is specifically responsible for controlling most of the body's essential unconscious (involuntary) processes, such as heartbeat, breathing, and digestion-related reflexes.
