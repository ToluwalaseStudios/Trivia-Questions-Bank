# PHYSICS QUESTIONS & EXPLANATIONS

Q1.
Question: A thin square sheet of metal of side $0.20\ \text{m}$ is heated from $15\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $75\ ^\circ\text{C}$. If the linear expansivity of the metal is $1.2 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{K}^{-1}$, find the increase in its area.
A. $6.05 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^2$
B. $5.76 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2$
C. $5.05 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2$
D. $3.76 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2$
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Area expansivity $\beta = 2\alpha = 2 \times 1.2 \times 10^{-5} = 2.4 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{K}^{-1}$. Initial area $A_0 = (0.20)^2 = 0.04\ \text{m}^2$. Temperature change $\Delta\theta = 75 - 15 = 60\ \text{K}$. Increase in area $\Delta A = \beta A_0 \Delta\theta = 2.4 \times 10^{-5} \times 0.04 \times 60 = 5.76 \times 10^{-5}\ \text{m}^2$.

Q2.
Question: Which of the following statements is/are incorrect for a body released from rest that falls freely under gravity?
i. Its potential energy decreases.
ii. Its kinetic energy increases linearly with the distance fallen.
iii. Its total mechanical energy increases.
iv. Its kinetic energy at the halfway point is equal to half of its initial potential energy.
v. Work is done against gravity.
A. i, ii and iv
B. i, iii and v
C. ii and iv
D. iii and v
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Statement iii is incorrect because total mechanical energy is conserved in free fall (potential energy lost equals kinetic energy gained). Statement v is incorrect because gravity does positive work on the falling body; work is not done against gravity. Statements i, ii, and iv are correct physical statements.

Q3.
Question: Which of the following physical quantities is a scalar quantity?
A. Electric field intensity
B. Magnetic flux density
C. Electric potential
D. Gravitational field strength
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Electric potential is defined as work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to a point, possessing magnitude only (scalar). Electric field intensity, magnetic flux density, and gravitational field strength all have both magnitude and direction (vectors).

Q4.
Question: A block of mass $m$ remains at rest on an inclined plane making an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. Which statement describes the equilibrium of forces acting on the block?
A. The net component of force along the plane is $mg\sin\theta$.
B. The net force acting on the block in any direction is zero.
C. The normal reaction force equals $mg\tan\theta$.
D. The vertical force component acting down the plane is $mg\cos\theta$.
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Since the block is at rest, it is in static equilibrium, meaning the net vector force acting on it in all directions is zero. The downward weight $mg$ is balanced by the vector sum of the normal reaction $R = mg\cos\theta$ and static friction $f_s = mg\sin\theta$.

Q5.
Question: When drops of methylated spirit are applied to human skin, a cold sensation is felt because the spirit
A. easily evaporates without heat transfer
B. extracts its latent heat of vaporization from the body
C. evaporates only at its boiling point
D. releases its latent heat of vaporization into the body
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Evaporating liquid requires thermal energy (latent heat of vaporization) to transform into gas. Methylated spirit absorbs this heat directly from the skin, lowering skin temperature and causing a cooling sensation.

Q6.
Question: Which of the following statements correctly compare a spring balance and a chemical balance?
I. Both are used to measure the mass of an object directly.
II. Either of them may be used to measure weight directly.
III. The spring balance works on Hooke's law, whereas the chemical balance works on the principle of moments.
IV. A change in local gravity alters the reading of a spring balance but does not affect the balance point of a chemical balance.
A. I and IV
B. II and III
C. III and IV
D. I, II and III
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Statement III is correct because spring balances measure force by spring extension (Hooke's law), while beam/chemical balances compare unknown mass to known masses using balanced moments. Statement IV is correct because gravity affects the weight pull on a spring balance, but cancels out on both sides of a chemical beam balance. Statements I and II are false.

Q7.
Question: A projectile is launched with an initial velocity $u$ at an angle of $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. What is the ratio of its horizontal range $R$ to its maximum height $H$?
A. $1:4$
B. $4\sqrt{3}:1$
C. $1:2$
D. $2:1$
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: $R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$ and $H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$. The ratio $\frac{R}{H} = \frac{2 \sin 2\theta}{\sin^2\theta} = 4 \cot\theta$. For $\theta = 30^\circ$, $\cot 30^\circ = \sqrt{3}$. Thus $\frac{R}{H} = 4\sqrt{3}:1$.

Q8.
Question: A uniform plank of length $4.0\ \text{m}$ is pivoted at its mid-point $O$. A weight $P$ is placed at a distance of $1.5\ \text{m}$ to the left of $O$, balancing a weight $Q$ placed at a distance $q$ to the right of $O$. If an additional weight $2P$ is added directly to $P$ at the same position, by what distance must the combined load $3P$ be moved to restore equilibrium?
A. $0.5\ \text{m}$ towards $O$
B. $1.0\ \text{m}$ towards $O$
C. $0.5\ \text{m}$ away from $O$
D. $1.0\ \text{m}$ away from $O$
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Initial balance: $P \times 1.5 = Q \times q \implies Qq = 1.5P$. When the left load becomes $3P$, let its new distance from $O$ be $x$. For equilibrium, $3P \cdot x = Qq = 1.5P \implies x = \frac{1.5P}{3P} = 0.5\ \text{m}$. The load was originally at $1.5\ \text{m}$, so it must be moved $1.5 - 0.5 = 1.0\ \text{m}$ closer to pivot $O$.

Q9.
Question: What is the correct sequence of steps for charging an insulated metal sphere negatively by induction using a positively charged rod?
I. Bring the positively charged rod near the insulated sphere.
II. Remove the positively charged rod.
III. Touch the sphere with a finger to earth it.
IV. Remove the finger from the sphere.
A. I, III, IV, II
B. I, II, III, IV
C. III, I, IV, II
D. I, IV, III, II
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Electrostatic induction requires: (I) bringing the charged rod near to separate charges on the sphere, (III) earthing the sphere while the rod is present so electrons flow from earth to neutralize positive charge, (IV) breaking the earth connection to trap the net negative charge, and (II) removing the inducing rod so charge distributes evenly.

Q10.
Question: An ideal gas occupies a volume of $2.0\ \text{dm}^3$ at a pressure of $1.5\ \text{atm}$ and a temperature of $400\ \text{K}$. What is its volume when the pressure is doubled and the temperature is halved?
A. $4.0\ \text{dm}^3$
B. $2.0\ \text{dm}^3$
C. $1.0\ \text{dm}^3$
D. $0.5\ \text{dm}^3$
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Using the general gas law $\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$: $P_1 = 1.5\ \text{atm}$, $V_1 = 2.0\ \text{dm}^3$, $T_1 = 400\ \text{K}$. $P_2 = 2 \times 1.5 = 3.0\ \text{atm}$, $T_2 = 400 / 2 = 200\ \text{K}$.
$$V_2 = \frac{P_1 V_1 T_2}{T_1 P_2} = \frac{1.5 \times 2.0 \times 200}{400 \times 3.0} = \frac{600}{1200} = 0.5\ \text{dm}^3$$

Q11.
Question: The escape velocity of a body from the surface of Earth depends on the
A. direction of launch
B. mass of the body
C. radius of the Earth
D. shape of the body
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Escape velocity is given by $v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR}$, where $M$ is Earth's mass and $R$ is Earth's radius. It depends on planet mass and radius, independent of the mass or launch direction of the projected body.

Q12.
Question: A weighted thin wire cuts through a block of ice supported at both ends, but the ice remains intact behind the wire. This phenomenon is known as
A. fusion
B. condensation
C. sublimation
D. regelation
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Increased pressure under the thin wire lowers the melting point of ice, causing it to melt into water beneath the wire. As the wire passes, pressure drops back to normal and the water refreezes above the wire. This pressure-melting and refreezing process is called regelation.

Q13.
Question: Which of the following substances sublime upon heating?
I. Butanoic acid
II. Benzoic acid
III. Naphthalene
IV. Iodine
A. I and II only
B. II and IV only
C. II, III and IV only
D. I, II, III and IV

Q14.
Question: Calculate the total heat extracted when $200\ \text{g}$ of steam at $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$ is condensed and cooled to water at $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$. [Specific heat capacity of water $= 4.2\ \text{J g}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$; specific latent heat of vaporization $= 2.26 \times 10^6\ \text{J kg}^{-1}$]
A. $5.108 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$
B. $5.180 \times 10^6\ \text{J}$
C. $0.5108 \times 10^7\ \text{J}$
D. $5.108 \times 10^4\ \text{J}$
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Mass $m = 0.20\ \text{kg} = 200\ \text{g}$.
Heat released during condensation: $Q_1 = m L_v = 0.20 \times 2.26 \times 10^6 = 4.52 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$.
Heat released during cooling ($100\ ^\circ\text{C} \rightarrow 30\ ^\circ\text{C}$): $Q_2 = mc\Delta\theta = 200 \times 4.2 \times (100 - 30) = 58,800\ \text{J} = 0.588 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$.
Total heat extracted $= Q_1 + Q_2 = 4.52 \times 10^5 + 0.588 \times 10^5 = 5.108 \times 10^5\ \text{J}$.

Q15.
Question: An automobile tyre is filled to a gauge pressure of $220\ \text{kPa}$ at $30\ ^\circ\text{C}$. After driving on hot roads, the tyre temperature rises to $85\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Assuming air volume stays constant and atmospheric pressure is $101\ \text{kPa}$, calculate the new gauge pressure.
A. $480.3\ \text{kPa}$
B. $278.3\ \text{kPa}$
C. $379.3\ \text{kPa}$
D. $101.0\ \text{kPa}$
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Absolute initial pressure $P_{1,\text{abs}} = 220 + 101 = 321\ \text{kPa}$. Initial temperature $T_1 = 30 + 273.15 = 303.15\ \text{K}$; final temperature $T_2 = 85 + 273.15 = 358.15\ \text{K}$.
By Pressure Law: $P_{2,\text{abs}} = P_{1,\text{abs}} \times \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 321 \times \frac{358.15}{303.15} = 379.27\ \text{kPa}$.
New gauge pressure $= P_{2,\text{abs}} - P_{\text{atm}} = 379.27 - 101 = 278.27\ \text{kPa} \approx 278.3\ \text{kPa}$.

Q16.
Question: A substance of specific heat capacity $450\ \text{J kg}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$ falls from rest through a height of $20\ \text{m}$. Assuming all lost gravitational potential energy converts into internal heat energy within the substance, calculate its temperature rise. [$g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$]
A. $\frac{9}{4}\ ^\circ\text{C}$
B. $\frac{9}{2}\ ^\circ\text{C}$
C. $\frac{2}{9}\ ^\circ\text{C}$
D. $\frac{4}{9}\ ^\circ\text{C}$
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: Potential energy lost $= mgh$. Heat gained $= mc\Delta\theta$. Equating both: $mgh = mc\Delta\theta \implies g h = c \Delta\theta$.
$$\Delta\theta = \frac{gh}{c} = \frac{10 \times 20}{450} = \frac{200}{450} = \frac{4}{9}\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

Q17.
Question: Two identical capacitors of capacitance $C$ are connected first in series and then in parallel. What is the ratio of their effective series capacitance to their effective parallel capacitance?
A. 1:1
B. 2:1
C. 1:4
D. 1:2
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Series equivalent $C_s = \frac{C \cdot C}{C + C} = \frac{C}{2}$. Parallel equivalent $C_p = C + C = 2C$.
Ratio $\frac{C_s}{C_p} = \frac{C/2}{2C} = \frac{1}{4}$.

Q18.
Question: A car of mass $M$ accelerates uniformly from rest driven by an engine delivering constant power $P$. What is the minimum time required to attain speed $V$?
A. $\frac{MV}{P}$
B. $\frac{MV}{2P}$
C. $\frac{MV^2}{2P}$
D. $\frac{MV^2}{P}$
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Work done by engine $=$ kinetic energy gained $= \frac{1}{2} M V^2$. Power $P = \frac{\text{Work}}{\text{Time}} \implies t = \frac{\text{Work}}{P} = \frac{\frac{1}{2} M V^2}{P} = \frac{M V^2}{2P}$.

Q19.
Question: Which of the following procedures will increase the boiling point of pure water?
I. Dissolving common salt in it
II. Taking it to a higher altitude
III. Heating it inside a sealed, pressure-resistant container
A. I and III only
B. I only
C. I and II only
D. III only

Explanation: Non-volatile solutes like salt lower vapor pressure, elevating boiling point (I). Heating in a sealed container increases vapor pressure above the liquid, raising boiling point (III). Higher altitude lowers atmospheric pressure, decreasing boiling point (II is false).

Q20.
Question: A sample of hot water is added to three times its mass of water at $10\ ^\circ\text{C}$. If the final steady mixture temperature is $20\ ^\circ\text{C}$, what was the initial temperature of the hot water?
A. $100\ ^\circ\text{C}$
B. $80\ ^\circ\text{C}$
C. $50\ ^\circ\text{C}$
D. $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Let hot water mass be $m$ at temperature $\theta$. Cold water mass is $3m$ at $10\ ^\circ\text{C}$.
Heat lost by hot water $=$ Heat gained by cold water:
$$m c (\theta - 20) = 3m c (20 - 10)$$
$$\theta - 20 = 3(10) = 30 \implies \theta = 50\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

Q21.
Question: An alpha particle, a proton, and a beta particle move at the same linear speed in a uniform magnetic field. Which of the following statements is/are correct regarding their linear momenta?
I. The linear momentum of the alpha particle is the greatest.
II. The linear momentum of the proton is less than that of the beta particle.
III. The linear momentum of the beta particle is equal to that of an electron with the same speed.
A. I only
B. I and II only
C. I and III only
D. I, II and III
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Linear momentum $p = mv$. Since speed $v$ is constant:
I is true because an alpha particle has the largest mass ($\approx 4\ \text{u}$).
II is false because a proton ($\approx 1\ \text{u}$) is much more massive than a beta particle/electron ($\approx \frac{1}{1840}\ \text{u}$).
III is true because a beta particle is a high-speed electron, having identical mass.

Q22.
Question: Which modification will increase the frequency of oscillation of a simple pendulum?
A. Increasing the length of the pendulum
B. Decreasing the length of the pendulum
C. Increasing the bob mass
D. Decreasing the bob mass
Correct Answer: B
Explanation: Frequency $f = \frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}$. Frequency is inversely proportional to $\sqrt{L}$. Decreasing pendulum length $L$ increases frequency $f$. Mass has no effect on period or frequency.

Q25.
Question: An iron plate $0.02\ \text{m}$ thick has a cross-sectional area of $0.5\ \text{m}^2$. The temperatures on opposite faces are $15\ ^\circ\text{C}$ and $140\ ^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the heat transferred per second through the plate. [Thermal conductivity of iron $= 59\ \text{W m}^{-1}\ \text{K}^{-1}$]
A. $184.4\ \text{kW}$
B. $368.8\ \text{kW}$
C. $18.4\ \text{kW}$
D. $36.9\ \text{kW}$
Correct Answer: A
Explanation: Rate of heat flow $\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{k A \Delta\theta}{L}$.
$$\frac{Q}{t} = \frac{59 \times 0.5 \times (140 - 15)}{0.02} = \frac{59 \times 0.5 \times 125}{0.02} = \frac{3687.5}{0.02} = 184,375\ \text{W} = 184.375\ \text{kW} \approx 184.4\ \text{kW}$$

Q26.
Question: A solid cylinder has base radius $0.3\ \text{m}$ and height $0.2\ \text{m}$. If its mass is $55\ \text{kg}$, calculate its density. [$\pi = 3.14$]
A. $194\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$
B. $291\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$
C. $673\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$
D. $973\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$

Density $\rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{55}{0.05652} \approx 973.1\ \text{kg m}^{-3} \approx 973\ \text{kg m}^{-3}$.

Q27.
Question: A transformer steps down mains supply from $220\ \text{V}$ to $15\ \text{V}$. When operating three $15\ \text{V}, 40\ \text{W}$ lamps connected in parallel, the current drawn from the mains is $0.7\ \text{A}$. Calculate transformer efficiency.
A. $26\%$
B. $80\%$
C. $78\%$
D. $76\%$
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Output power $P_{\text{out}} = 3 \times 40\ \text{W} = 120\ \text{W}$. Input power $P_{\text{in}} = V_p I_p = 220 \times 0.7 = 154\ \text{W}$.
$$\text{Efficiency} = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}} \times 100\% = \frac{120}{154} \times 100\% \approx 77.92\% \approx 78\%$$

Q29.
Question: A man expends energy at a rate of $200\ \text{W}$ climbing a vertical height of $44\ \text{m}$. If his mass is $75\ \text{kg}$, determine the time taken. [$g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$]

Q30.

Q35.

Q38.
Question: A ball of mass $0.1\ \text{kg}$ is thrown vertically upwards with a speed of $10\ \text{m s}^{-1}$ from the top of a tower $10\ \text{m}$ high. Neglecting air resistance, its total mechanical energy just before hitting the ground is [$g = 10\ \text{m s}^{-2}$]
A. $5\ \text{J}$
B. $10\ \text{J}$
C. $15\ \text{J}$
D. $20\ \text{J}$
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Initial total mechanical energy $E = KE + PE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + mgh = \frac{1}{2}(0.1)(10)^2 + (0.1)(10)(10) = 5 + 10 = 15\ \text{J}$. In the absence of air resistance, mechanical energy is conserved throughout flight.

Q39.
Question: A gas at initial pressure $P_0$ and volume $V_0$ is compressed isothermally to one-fifth of its original volume. What is its new pressure?
A. $\frac{P_0}{5}$
B. $\frac{4P_0}{5}$
C. $P_0$
D. $5P_0$
Correct Answer: D
Explanation: By Boyle's Law ($T = \text{constant}$): $P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2 \implies P_0 V_0 = P_2 \left(\frac{V_0}{5}\right) \implies P_2 = 5 P_0$.

Q40.
Question: A catapult holds a stone of mass $0.5\ \text{kg}$ and is stretched by $0.2\ \text{m}$ under maximum force $F$. If the stone leaves the catapult with velocity $40\ \text{m s}^{-1}$, calculate $F$.
A. $4.0 \times 10^2\ \text{N}$
B. $2.0 \times 10^3\ \text{N}$
C. $4.0 \times 10^3\ \text{N}$
D. $4.0 \times 10^4\ \text{N}$
Correct Answer: C
Explanation: Kinetic energy of stone $= \frac{1}{2} m v^2 = \frac{1}{2}(0.5)(40)^2 = 400\ \text{J}$. Elastic potential energy stored $= \frac{1}{2} F e$. Equating energies: $\frac{1}{2} F (0.2) = 400 \implies 0.1 F = 400 \implies F = 4000\ \text{N} = 4.0 \times 10^3\ \text{N}$.
