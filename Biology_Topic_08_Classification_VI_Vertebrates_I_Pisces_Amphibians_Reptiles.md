Q: The feature that distinguishes the toad or frog from a fish is the absence of
A) scales
B) lungs
C) paired appendages
D) a tail
E) skin glands
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Adult toads and frogs have soft, moist, scaleless skin, unlike fish, which are covered in protective scales — this is the clearest distinguishing feature among the options (frogs do have lungs as adults, and both groups have paired appendages/limbs in some form).

Q: Which of these is not associated with the tadpole stages of the toad or frog?
A) V-shaped gland
B) Operculum
C) External gills
D) Jelly
E) Shell
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Amphibian eggs (and the tadpoles that hatch from them) are surrounded by a soft jelly coating, not a hard shell — a shell is instead characteristic of reptile and bird eggs. The V-shaped cement gland, external gills (later covered by an operculum), and jelly coating are all genuinely part of early tadpole development.

Q: Some of the features of an animal are scales, teeth, nares and backbone. The animal is likely to be a
A) toad
B) bird
C) lizard
D) rat
E) bat
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A lizard has scaly skin, teeth, nostrils (nares), and a backbone, matching all the given features — toads lack scales, birds lack teeth, and rats/bats (mammals) lack the typical reptilian scaly covering.

Q: Which of the following animals is cold-blooded?
A) cat
B) Lizard
C) Whale
D) Bird
E) Horse
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The lizard is a reptile and therefore ectothermic (cold-blooded), meaning its body temperature varies with its surroundings. Cats, whales, birds, and horses are all warm-blooded (endothermic) animals that maintain a constant internal body temperature.

Q: The main function of the swim-bladder is for
A) swimming
B) detecting sound
C) buoyancy
D) breathing
E) reproduction
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The swim bladder is a gas-filled sac found in bony fishes that allows them to control their buoyancy, adjusting how much they float or sink in the water without expending extra muscular energy. (In some fish, such as lungfish, it can additionally assist with breathing, but its main, universal role is buoyancy control.)

Q: When an operculate fish breathes, it takes in water through the
A) mouth and passes it out through the same way
B) mouth and passes it out through the operculum
C) operculum and passes it out through the mouth
D) operculum and passes it out through the nostrils
E) nostrils and passes it out through the operculum
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A fish with an operculum (gill cover) draws water in through its mouth, passes it over the gills for gas exchange, and then expels it out from under the operculum — a one-way flow that keeps oxygenated water constantly moving across the gill surfaces.

Q: Which of the following structures is NOT found in the female Agama lizard?
A) Pre-natal pads
B) Ear drum
C) Gular fold
D) Nasal scale
E) Nuchal crest
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: "Pre-natal pads" (nuptial/copulatory pads) are not a feature found in female Agama lizards at all — features like the ear drum, gular fold (throat flap), nasal scale, and nuchal crest are present in the Agama lizard's general anatomy, though some (like a prominent crest or red coloring) tend to be more developed in males.

Q: The male toad differs from the female by having
A) vocal sacs
B) shorter hind limbs
C) longer forelimbs
D) bulging eyes
E) nictating membrane
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Male toads possess vocal sacs, inflatable pouches used to amplify their mating calls — a feature females lack. Vocal sacs are directly tied to the male's role in courtship calling.

Q: The dorsal and anal fins of fish are used for
A) upward movements
B) controlling rolling movements
C) downward movements
D) steering
E) buoyancy
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The dorsal (top) and anal (underside) fins act like keels, helping to stabilize the fish and prevent it from rolling side to side as it swims, rather than directly controlling upward/downward movement (which is more the role of paired fins) or steering (caudal fin).

Q: In fish, the sense organs which detect movements in the water are located within the
A) gills
B) operculum
C) nostrils
D) median fins
E) lateral line
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The lateral line is a specialized sensory system running along the sides of a fish's body, containing cells sensitive to water vibrations and pressure changes, allowing the fish to detect movement and disturbances in the surrounding water.

Q: At what stage in the life history of a toad is its mode of breathing similar to that of a fish?
A) External gill stage
B) Tadpole stage
C) Adult stage
D) Internal gill stage
E) Larval stage
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: During the internal gill stage of tadpole development, the toad breathes using gills enclosed within an operculum — structurally and functionally very similar to how a true fish breathes, before the tadpole eventually develops lungs as it matures into an adult.

Q: Fishes are cold-blooded because their body temperature is
A) constantly low
B) constantly high
C) dependent on that of their surroundings
D) regulated at will
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Being cold-blooded (poikilothermic/ectothermic) specifically means an animal's internal body temperature fluctuates with, and is dependent on, the temperature of its external environment — it is not necessarily "constantly low," since a fish's temperature could actually be quite warm if its surrounding water is warm.

Q: The fins making up the limbs of the bony fish are
A) caudal and ventral
B) ventral and pelvic
C) pelvic and pectoral
D) pectoral and dorsal
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The paired pectoral and pelvic fins of a bony fish are considered analogous to the fore and hind limbs of other vertebrates, serving comparable roles in steering, balance, and braking, unlike the unpaired dorsal, anal, and caudal fins.

Q: The long and coiled intestine of a young tadpole is an adaptation to its
A) herbivorous diet
B) carnivorous diet
C) aquatic habitat
D) insectivorous habit
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Young tadpoles are primarily herbivorous, feeding on algae and plant material, which is comparatively harder to digest and lower in nutrients than animal protein — a long, coiled intestine provides the extra surface area and time needed for thorough digestion and absorption of this plant-based diet.

Q: The major function of the swim-bladder in fishes is
A) breathing
B) buoyancy
C) swimming
D) diving
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, the swim bladder's main and most universal function across bony fishes is buoyancy control, allowing the fish to remain suspended at a particular depth without constant muscular effort.

Q: Which of the following animals has homodont dentition?
A) Rat
B) Man
C) Lizard
D) Pigeon
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The lizard has homodont dentition, meaning all its teeth are essentially the same shape, size, and function, unlike mammals such as rats and humans, which have heterodont dentition (different tooth types — incisors, canines, molars — specialized for different functions). Pigeons, being birds, have no teeth at all.

Q: In which of the following does external fertilization take place?
A) Toad
B) Lizard
C) Bird
D) Cockroach
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Toads (and most amphibians) release their eggs and sperm directly into water, where fertilization occurs externally. Lizards, birds, and cockroaches all undergo internal fertilization within the female's body.

Q: The gill rakers of fishes take part in
A) feeding
B) respiration
C) swimming
D) diffusion
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Gill rakers are comb-like projections on the gill arches that act as strainers, trapping food particles and preventing them from escaping through the gill slits back out of the mouth cavity — directly assisting in feeding, even though they are located on the respiratory gill structures.

Q: Cold blooded animals are referred to as
A) poikilothermic
B) homoiothermic
C) polythermic
D) homeostatic
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: "Poikilothermic" is the correct technical term for animals whose body temperature varies with their surrounding environment (cold-blooded animals), as opposed to "homoiothermic" (warm-blooded animals that maintain a constant internal temperature).

Q: Which of the organisms has lost the pentadactyl limb structure?
A) Bat
B) Fish
C) Frog
D) Pigeon
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The basic five-digit (pentadactyl) limb pattern, inherited from early tetrapod ancestors, has been entirely lost in fish, whose paired fins represent a more primitive limb form rather than a modified pentadactyl limb — unlike bats, frogs, and pigeons, whose wings/limbs are all clearly modified but still recognizably derived from the pentadactyl pattern.

Q: Which of the following combinations differentiates a bony fish from a cartilaginous fish? (I) Presence of gills (II) Absence of gill slits (III) Possession of bony skeleton (IV) Possession of a laterally compressed body (V) Possession of a dorso-ventrally compressed body
A) I, II and III
B) I, III and IV
C) II, III and IV
D) II, III and V
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Bony fish are specifically distinguished from cartilaginous fish by having their gill slits covered by an operculum (so gill slits are not individually visible externally — II), a skeleton made of true bone rather than cartilage (III), and typically a laterally compressed body shape (IV) — unlike cartilaginous fish (e.g. sharks), which have visible individual gill slits, a cartilage skeleton, and often a more dorso-ventrally flattened body.

Q: Which is the most important adaptation of a bony fish to life in water?
A) The possession of a streamlined shape
B) The presence of overlapping scales
C) The covering of the body by a thin film of slime
D) The possession of a caudal fin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A streamlined (fusiform) body shape is the single most fundamental adaptation for efficient aquatic life, dramatically reducing water resistance/drag as the fish swims — while scales, mucus/slime, and the caudal fin are all useful supporting adaptations, none is as fundamentally important as the overall streamlined body shape.

Q: In which of the following vertebrates does the skin function as a respiratory surface?
A) Rat
B) Lizard
C) Fish
D) Frog
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The frog's moist, thin, well-vascularized skin is capable of significant gas exchange (cutaneous respiration), supplementing its lung breathing — a feature not typically shared by the drier or scaled skin of rats, lizards, or the gill-breathing skin of fish.

Q: In which of the following groups of vertebrates would the largest amount of yolk be found in the egg?
A) Mammals
B) Fishes
C) Amphibians
D) Reptiles
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Reptile eggs are laid on land and must sustain the full development of the embryo entirely within the shell (without additional external nourishment), requiring a comparatively very large yolk reserve — larger than the yolk typically found in fish or amphibian eggs, and far more than the minimal yolk in mammalian eggs, which instead receive nourishment directly from the mother via the placenta.

Q: The structures that prevent food particles from escaping through the fish gills are called
A) rakers
B) lamellae
C) arches
D) filaments
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As covered earlier, gill rakers are the comb-like structures on the gill arches specifically responsible for straining out and retaining food particles, preventing them from being expelled with the outgoing respiratory water flow.

Q: The possession of scales, laying of eggs with shells and bony structure of the head are characteristics shared by
A) fishes and birds
B) reptiles and birds
C) reptiles and fishes
D) birds and molluscs
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Both reptiles and birds share scaly coverings (birds retain scales on their legs), lay shelled eggs, and have a bony skull structure — a combination of features reflecting their close evolutionary relationship (birds having evolved from reptilian ancestors).

Q: The key event in the transition of the amphibians from water to land is the
A) replacement of the gills with lungs
B) possession of webbed limbs
C) development of long hind limbs
D) possession of tympanic membrane
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The single most critical adaptation enabling amphibians to move from an aquatic to a terrestrial existence is the replacement of gill-based respiration with lung-based breathing, allowing them to draw oxygen directly from air rather than relying on water for gas exchange.

Q: The set of fins that controls steering, balancing and change of direction and pitch in fish is
A) dorsal and anal
B) pectoral and pelvic
C) caudal and dorsal
D) anal and pelvic
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The paired pectoral and pelvic fins act analogously to limbs, providing the fine control needed for steering, balancing, and adjusting pitch (up/down tilt) as the fish swims, while the caudal fin mainly provides forward propulsion and the dorsal/anal fins mainly prevent rolling.

Q: Amphibians are normally found
A) on dry land and in water
B) in water and on moist land
C) on moist land
D) in water
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Amphibians characteristically lead a "double life" (the literal meaning of "amphibian"), requiring both an aquatic environment (especially for breeding and larval development) and moist land habitats (for their adult terrestrial activities), since their skin cannot tolerate prolonged dry conditions.

Q: The main function of the caudal fin in Tilapia is to
A) propel it forward in water
B) steer it while changing direction
C) balance it in water
D) enable it to float in water
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The caudal (tail) fin's primary role in fish is to generate the powerful thrust that propels the fish forward through the water, via side-to-side sweeping movements — steering and balance are more the roles of the paired and median fins.

Q: In lizards, the lowering of the gular fold is used to
A) catch insects
B) attract mates
C) defend their territory
D) frighten enemies
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Lowering the gular fold (throat flap) as a visible display is a courtship behaviour in lizards, used specifically to attract mates, distinct from behaviours like raising a nuchal crest, which is more associated with territorial displays or intimidation.

Q: An example of a fish that aestivates is
A) shark
B) catfish
C) lung fish
D) croaker
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The lungfish is well known for its ability to aestivate — surviving periods when its pond or water body dries up by burrowing into mud and breathing air using its lung-like swim bladder until water returns — a survival strategy not shared by sharks, ordinary catfish, or croakers.
