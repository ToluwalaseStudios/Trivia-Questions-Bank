# TOPIC 7: CLASSIFICATION V — HIGHER INVERTEBRATES
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: Which of these is not true of the insect? The possession of
A) two pairs of antennae
B) jointed appendages
C) exoskeleton
D) three pairs of legs
E) segmented body
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: An insect has only one pair of antennae, not two. Jointed appendages, an exoskeleton, three pairs of legs, and a segmented body are all genuine, defining features of insects.

Q: Insects show considerable powers of water conservation. This is due to the fact that
A) the chitinous cuticle is impregnated with tanned proteins
B) nitrogenous waste is eliminated as urea
C) chitin is impermeable to water
D) the Malpighian tubules are impermeable to water
E) the cuticle is covered with wax
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The insect's outer chitinous cuticle is coated with a thin waxy layer, which is highly effective at preventing water loss through the body surface — this waxy coating is the specific adaptation responsible for insects' impressive water conservation, more so than the chitin itself or any other listed feature.

Q: A mosquito which produces eggs with a float, and whose larvae lie horizontal to the water surface, and which also rests at an angle on a vertical surface, is
A) Aedes
B) Anopheles
C) Culex
D) Tiger mosquito
E) none of the above
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: These are all classic identifying features of the Anopheles mosquito (the malaria vector): its eggs have floats and are laid singly, its larvae rest horizontally/parallel just under the water surface, and the resting adult characteristically tilts its body at an angle to the surface it perches on — unlike Culex or Aedes mosquitoes, which show different egg, larval, and resting postures.

Q: Which of the following insects has an incomplete metamorphosis during its life cycle?
A) Butterfly
B) Bee
C) Mosquito
D) House-fly
E) Grasshopper
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The grasshopper develops through incomplete (hemimetabolous) metamorphosis — egg → nymph → adult — where the nymph closely resembles a smaller, wingless version of the adult. Butterflies, bees, mosquitoes, and houseflies all undergo complete metamorphosis instead, passing through a distinct larval and pupal stage that looks nothing like the adult.

Q: Which of these statements is NOT true of insects? They
A) are arthropods
B) have a body divided into three distinct regions of head, thorax and abdomen
C) have a thorax comprising three segments, only two of which bear a pair of appendages each
D) respire by means of trachea
E) undergo metamorphosis
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: In fact, all three segments of an insect's thorax (prothorax, mesothorax, and metathorax) each bear one pair of walking legs — making the claim that "only two" segments bear appendages the false statement here. Insects genuinely are arthropods, have a head-thorax-abdomen body plan, breathe through a network of tracheae, and (in most cases) undergo metamorphosis.

Q: A centipede differs from a millipede by its
A) colour
B) numerous abdominal segments
C) paired legs on each abdominal segment
D) poison claws
E) cylindrical body
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Centipedes possess a pair of venomous poison claws (modified first legs, called forcipules) just behind the head, used to inject venom into prey — a feature millipedes entirely lack. (Millipedes, in contrast, tend to have a more cylindrical body and two pairs of legs per body segment, rather than one.)

Q: 'Jointed skeleton' is absent in the
A) cockroach
B) spider
C) millipede
D) snail
E) dragon fly
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The snail is a mollusc, not an arthropod, and so it lacks a jointed external skeleton entirely (having instead a soft body sometimes protected by a coiled calcareous shell). Cockroaches, spiders, millipedes, and dragonflies are all arthropods with a jointed chitinous exoskeleton.

Q: Which of the following invertebrates does NOT possess antennae?
A) Centipede
B) Crustacean
C) Millipede
D) Insect
E) Spider
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Spiders belong to the arachnids, which lack antennae altogether; instead, they use a pair of pedipalpi (grasping/sensory appendages) near the mouth. Centipedes, crustaceans, millipedes, and insects all possess antennae.

Q: Each of the following is an arthropod EXCEPT the
A) crab
B) spider
C) snail
D) millipede
E) cockroach
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established in an earlier question, the snail is a mollusc, not an arthropod — crabs, spiders, millipedes, and cockroaches are all true arthropods.

Q: Incomplete metamorphosis occurs in the
A) butterfly
B) grasshopper
C) mosquito
D) housefly
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As noted earlier, the grasshopper undergoes incomplete (hemimetabolous) metamorphosis, while the butterfly, mosquito, and housefly all undergo complete metamorphosis with a distinct larval and pupal stage.

Q: When the original king and queen of termites die, they are replaced by
A) the king and queen of another colony
B) some adult reproductives from the same colony
C) some adult workers which are specially fed to breed
D) developing nymphs nurtured as secondary reproductives
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A termite colony maintains its egg-laying rate by rearing several developing nymphs into secondary (replacement) reproductives, which can then take over the roles of a deceased king or queen — the colony does not simply promote ordinary workers or import royalty from another colony.

Q: The male cockroach differs from the female by having
A) mandibles
B) a pair of styles
C) spiracles
D) a pair of cerci
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A pair of small style-like structures near the tip of the abdomen is a feature unique to the male cockroach. A pair of cerci, mandibles, and spiracles are present in both sexes.

Q: The crayfish is an arthropod because
A) its body consists of a cephalothorax and an abdomen
B) it has a pair each of antennae and antennules
C) every segment of its body carries a pair of appendages
D) its body is covered with an exoskeleton made of chitin
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The defining feature that places the crayfish (and all arthropods) into the phylum Arthropoda is the possession of a hard, jointed, chitinous exoskeleton — the other features listed (body regions, antennae, appendages per segment) are true of crayfish but are not, by themselves, the defining criterion for arthropod classification.

Q: In the life history of a butterfly, destruction of crops is caused by the
A) maggot
B) nymph
C) caterpillar
D) pupa
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The caterpillar (larval stage of a butterfly) is the voracious, leaf-eating stage responsible for damaging crops, whereas the adult butterfly is actually beneficial, acting as a pollinator.

Q: Insects and millipede have many features in common EXCEPT
A) exoskeleton
B) jointed appendages
C) compound eyes
D) segmented body
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: While insects possess a pair of true compound eyes, millipedes may have simple eyes, compound eyes, or none at all, depending on the species — making compound eyes not a consistently shared feature. Both groups do have a chitinous exoskeleton, jointed appendages, and segmented bodies.

Q: Which of the following is a common characteristic of crustaceans?
A) Possession of a pair of antennae
B) Possession of two pairs of antennae
C) Each segment has a pair of walking legs
D) Four pairs of walking legs on the cephalothorax
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A defining feature of crustaceans (such as crayfish and crabs) is that they possess two pairs of antennae (antennae and antennules), unlike insects, which have only one pair.

Q: The pedipalpi in spiders are used for
A) grasping
B) walking
C) feeling
D) web spinning
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Spiders primarily use their pedipalpi to grasp and manipulate captured prey, though they also serve a secondary sensory role (being sensitive to touch). They are not used for walking (spiders have separate walking legs) or for spinning silk (which is done by spinnerets).

Q: Which of the following insects undergoes incomplete metamorphosis?
A) mosquito
B) Termite
C) Housefly
D) Moth
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Termites, like grasshoppers, develop through incomplete metamorphosis (egg → nymph → adult), while mosquitoes, houseflies, and moths all undergo complete metamorphosis with distinct larval and pupal stages.

Q: The butterfly is of great economic importance because
A) of its use in scientific studies
B) it sucks nectar from flowers
C) it adds to the beauty of the environment
D) it pollinates flowers of crops and other plants
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: While butterflies do feed on nectar and are admired for their beauty, their most significant economic importance lies in pollination — as they visit flowers for nectar, they transfer pollen between plants, directly benefiting the reproduction of crops and other flowering plants.

Q: The mouth part of the insect is adapted for
A) biting and chewing
B) suckling and chewing
C) biting and sucking
D) piercing and sucking
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The cockroach has strong mandibles adapted for biting and chewing solid food, unlike insects such as the mosquito (piercing and sucking) or housefly (sponging/lapping), which have very differently adapted mouthparts for liquid feeding.

Q: Which of the following insects lays its eggs in horny, purse-like cases?
A) Mosquito
B) Butterfly
C) Termite
D) Cockroach
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The cockroach lays its eggs enclosed within a hardened, purse-shaped protective case called an ootheca, which shields the developing eggs until they hatch.

Q: The group of insects that undergoes complete metamorphosis is
A) houseflies, beetles and cockroaches
B) cockroaches, grasshoppers and bees
C) houseflies, beetles and butterflies
D) aphids, grasshoppers and butterflies
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Houseflies, beetles, and butterflies all undergo complete metamorphosis, passing through egg, larva, pupa, and adult stages. Cockroaches, grasshoppers, and aphids, by contrast, develop through incomplete metamorphosis.

Q: The three classes in a termitarium are
A) soldiers, queen and workers
B) workers, soldiers and reproductives
C) soldiers, workers and kings
D) kings, queen and soldiers
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A termite colony's caste system is organized into three broad functional classes: workers (who feed and maintain the colony), soldiers (who defend it), and reproductives (including the king, queen, and any secondary reproductives responsible for producing offspring).

Q: The ability of the cockroach to live in cracks and crevices is enhanced by the possession of
A) wings and segmented body
B) compound eyes
C) claws on the legs
D) dorso-ventrally flattened body
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The cockroach's characteristically flattened (dorso-ventrally compressed) body shape allows it to squeeze into narrow cracks and crevices for shelter and protection, a key adaptation for its lifestyle.

Q: The caste of termites that lacks pigmentation is the
A) king
B) worker
C) soldier
D) queen
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Worker termites are typically pale, whitish, and unpigmented, since they spend their entire lives inside the dark termite mound/nest and have no need for protective pigmentation — this pale appearance is also why termites are commonly (though incorrectly) nicknamed "white ants."

Q: In the honey bee colony, the drones are
A) sterile males with reduced mouth parts
B) sterile males with well-developed mouth parts
C) fertile males with reduced mouth parts
D) fertile males with well-developed mouth parts
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Drones are the fertile male bees of the colony, whose sole role is to mate with the queen; their mouthparts are comparatively reduced since they are fed by the workers rather than needing to forage for themselves.

Q: The group of Arthropods that has no antennae is the
A) crustacea
B) chilopoda
C) arachnida
D) diplopoda
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As noted earlier, arachnids (spiders, scorpions, mites, ticks) are the arthropod group that lacks antennae entirely, using pedipalpi instead for sensory and grasping functions.

Q: Water fleas, wood lice and barnacles belong to the group
A) Arachnida
B) Crustacea
C) Insecta
D) Chilopoda
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Water fleas, wood lice, and barnacles are all classified within the Crustacea, a diverse arthropod group typically characterized by two pairs of antennae and (often) an aquatic lifestyle.

Q: The role of the male adult honey bee is to
A) clean the hive
B) ventilate the hive
C) mate with the queen
D) care for the young
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The sole functional role of the male honey bee (drone) is reproductive — to mate with the queen. Cleaning the hive, ventilating it, and caring for the young are all duties carried out by the sterile female worker bees.

Q: When oil is poured into the breeding site of mosquitoes, it
A) deprives the larvae of water
B) kills the adults
C) suffocates the larvae
D) slows down egg development
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A film of oil poured over standing water spreads across the surface and blocks the mosquito larvae's access to atmospheric air (which they breathe through a surface-piercing siphon), effectively suffocating them.

Q: A feature of the caste systems of bees and termites is that
A) the workers are sterile
B) the kings are bigger than queens
C) only the workers perform duties
D) nuptial flight is performed by all members
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In both bee and termite colonies, the worker caste consists of sterile individuals who forgo reproduction to instead perform maintenance, foraging, and caregiving tasks for the colony — reproduction is reserved for the queen (and king, in termites) and any reproductive castes.

Q: Insects are considered the most successful among Invertebrates because they
A) possess exoskeletons
B) survive in various environmental conditions
C) have wings for flight
D) possess the ability to change their forms
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Insects are considered the single most successful animal group largely because of their remarkable adaptability — they are found thriving in an enormous range of habitats and environmental conditions worldwide, far more so than would be explained by any one single feature like wings, exoskeletons, or metamorphosis alone.

Q: The larval stage of a mosquito is called
A) wriggler
B) grub
C) maggot
D) caterpillar
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The aquatic, actively wriggling larval stage of a mosquito is commonly called a "wriggler," distinct from the terms used for other insect larvae (maggot for flies, grub for beetles, caterpillar for butterflies/moths).

Q: The function of the long-winged reproductives in a termite colony is to
A) disperse the population
B) feed the young
C) participate in swarming
D) protect the young
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The long-winged reproductive termites exist specifically to fly off from the colony in large numbers (swarming) in order to disperse and establish entirely new colonies elsewhere, preventing overcrowding in the original nest.

Q: The stage in the life history of a moth responsible for the destruction of agricultural crops is the
A) nymph
B) imago
C) pupa
D) caterpillar
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As with the butterfly, it is the caterpillar (larval) stage of the moth that feeds voraciously on plant leaves and is responsible for crop damage, while the adult moth (imago) generally does far less feeding-related damage.

Q: The only caste in the termite colony whose members can feed themselves is the
A) reproductive
B) workers
C) nymph
D) soldiers
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Worker termites are the only caste capable of independently feeding themselves (and are also uniquely able to digest cellulose); they are then responsible for feeding all the other castes, including the reproductives, soldiers, and developing young, who cannot feed themselves.

Q: Which of the following is common to the mosquito, housefly and blackfly?

B) They are parasites of man
C) Their immature stages are aquatic

Q: The most abundant group of organisms in the animal kingdom is
A) Mammalia
B) Aves
C) Annelida
D) Insecta
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Insects (class Insecta) make up roughly 70% of all known animal species, vastly outnumbering mammals, birds, and annelids combined, making them by far the most abundant and diverse animal group.

Q: Which of the following animals is most adapted for water conservation?
A) Earthworms
B) Mammals
C) Flatworms
D) Insects
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As discussed earlier, insects possess a waxy, waterproof cuticle that dramatically reduces water loss by evaporation, and they excrete nitrogenous waste as nearly solid uric acid (rather than urine), further conserving water — making them exceptionally well adapted to water conservation compared with earthworms, mammals, or flatworms.

EXPLANATION: Soldier termites are specifically distinguished by being wingless and having a disproportionately large head equipped with strong, well-developed mandibles (or, in some species, a pointed nasus for chemical defence) — features specialized for defending the colony against invaders.

Q: Which of the following describes a characteristic of arthropods?
A) The organism has a pair of jointed appendages
B) The body is not divided into a number of segments
C) The body is covered by chitin
D) The organism finds it easy to grow freely
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A chitinous exoskeleton covering the body is a universal, defining characteristic of arthropods. (Their bodies are, in fact, segmented, they have many jointed appendages rather than just "a pair," and their rigid exoskeleton actually restricts free growth, requiring periodic moulting instead.)

Q: Which of the following distinguishes a butterfly from a moth?
A) Both are active during the day
B) They have similar antennae
C) The abdomen of a moth is fatter than that of a butterfly
D) The wings of a butterfly rest horizontally but those of a moth rest vertically
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Moths typically have a noticeably fatter, hairier abdomen than the slimmer-bodied butterfly. (Butterflies are generally diurnal while moths are typically nocturnal, their antennae differ in shape — butterflies have knobbed antennae, moths often feathery ones — and it is actually butterflies that rest with wings held vertically, while moths rest with wings spread horizontally, the reverse of option D.)

Q: Proboscis is a structure that is mostly found in
A) tapeworms
B) amphibians
C) molluscs
D) insects
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A proboscis — a long, often tubular or coiled mouthpart used for feeding on liquids — is a structure most characteristically associated with insects (such as the coiled proboscis of a butterfly, used for sucking nectar).
