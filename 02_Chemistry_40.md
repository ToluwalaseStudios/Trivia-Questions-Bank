Q: Which method is most suitable for separating ethanol from water?
A) Filtration
B) Sublimation
C) Fractional distillation
D) Decantation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Ethanol and water are miscible liquids (they mix completely) with different boiling points — ethanol boils at about 78°C and water at 100°C. Fractional distillation separates such liquids by gently heating the mixture so the liquid with the lower boiling point vaporizes first and is collected separately.

Q: The separation of components in paper chromatography is primarily based on differences in their
A) nuclear masses
B) solubility and attraction to the stationary phase
C) melting points only
D) densities only
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In paper chromatography, different substances in a mixture travel at different speeds up the paper because they differ in how soluble they are in the solvent (the mobile phase) and how strongly they are attracted to the paper (the stationary phase).

Q: Which observation provides the strongest evidence that a liquid is pure?
A) It is colourless
B) It has a constant boiling point
C) It has no smell
D) It evaporates rapidly
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A pure liquid boils at one fixed, sharp temperature. If a liquid contains impurities, its boiling point tends to rise and spread over a range instead of staying constant, so a constant boiling point is the reliable test for purity.

Q: How many moles are present in 18 g of water? (H = 1, O = 16)
A) 0.5 mol
B) 1 mol
C) 2 mol
D) 18 mol
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Molar mass of water (H2O) = (2 × 1) + 16 = 18 g/mol. Number of moles = mass ÷ molar mass = 18 ÷ 18 = 1 mol.

Q: What mass of CO2 is produced when 12 g of carbon burns completely in oxygen? C + O2 → CO2 (C = 12, O = 16)
A) 12 g
B) 22 g
C) 32 g
D) 44 g
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: 12 g of carbon = 12 ÷ 12 = 1 mole of carbon. From the balanced equation, 1 mole of carbon produces 1 mole of CO2. Molar mass of CO2 = 12 + (2 × 16) = 44 g/mol, so 1 mole of CO2 has a mass of 44 g.

Q: A compound contains 40% carbon, 6.67% hydrogen and 53.33% oxygen. Its empirical formula is
A) CHO
B) CH2O
C) C2H4O
D) C6H12O6
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Assume 100 g of compound, so the percentages become masses in grams. Divide each mass by its atomic mass to get moles: carbon = 40 ÷ 12 = 3.33, hydrogen = 6.67 ÷ 1 = 6.67, oxygen = 53.33 ÷ 16 = 3.33. Dividing all three by the smallest value (3.33) gives a ratio of C:H:O = 1:2:1, so the empirical formula is CH2O.

Q: At constant temperature, reducing the volume of a gas causes its pressure to
A) decrease
B) increase
C) remain constant
D) become zero
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This is Boyle's law — at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume. Squeezing a gas into a smaller volume forces its particles to collide with the container walls more often, raising the pressure.

Q: An atom has atomic number 17 and mass number 37. The number of neutrons is
A) 17
B) 20
C) 37
D) 54
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Number of neutrons = mass number − atomic number = 37 − 17 = 20.

Q: Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same
A) mass number but different atomic numbers
B) atomic number but different mass numbers
C) number of neutrons only
D) number of nucleons only
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons, so the same atomic number) but with different numbers of neutrons, which gives them different mass numbers.

Q: Elements in the same group generally have the same number of
A) electron shells
B) neutrons
C) valence electrons
D) nucleons
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Elements in the same group of the periodic table have the same number of electrons in their outermost shell (valence electrons), which is why they show similar chemical behaviour.

Q: Across a period from left to right, atomic radius generally
A) increases
B) decreases
C) remains constant
D) becomes infinite
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Moving across a period, protons are added to the nucleus, increasing its positive charge. This pulls the electron shells in more tightly, so atomic radius decreases from left to right.

Q: Which compound contains predominantly ionic bonding?
A) CH4
B) CO2
C) NaCl
D) NH3
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: NaCl forms when sodium (a metal) transfers an electron to chlorine (a non-metal), producing Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions held together by strong electrostatic attraction — this is ionic bonding. The other options are covalent compounds formed between non-metal atoms sharing electrons.

Q: The bond formed when two atoms share electrons is
A) ionic
B) covalent
C) metallic
D) coordinate only
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A covalent bond forms when two atoms share one or more pairs of electrons, typically between two non-metal atoms.

Q: Which substance turns blue litmus paper red?
A) Alkali
B) Acid
C) Salt only
D) Water
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Acids turn blue litmus paper red, while alkalis (bases) turn red litmus paper blue. This simple colour-change test is used to identify acids and bases.

Q: A solution with pH 2 is
A) strongly acidic
B) weakly acidic
C) neutral
D) alkaline
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The pH scale runs from 0 to 14, with 7 as neutral. Values well below 7, such as pH 2, indicate a strongly acidic solution.

Q: The products of an acid-base neutralization reaction are generally
A) salt and hydrogen
B) salt and water
C) water and oxygen
D) acid and salt
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In neutralization, an acid reacts with a base to produce a salt and water — for example, hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water.

Q: Oxidation can be described as
A) gain of electrons
B) loss of electrons
C) gain of protons only
D) loss of neutrons
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In terms of electron transfer, oxidation is the loss of electrons by a substance. A simple way to remember this is "OIL RIG" — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain (of electrons).

Q: In the reaction Zn + CuSO4 → ZnSO4 + Cu, the substance oxidized is
A) Cu
B) CuSO4
C) Zn
D) SO4²⁻
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Zinc loses two electrons to become Zn²⁺, forming ZnSO4 — losing electrons means zinc is oxidized. At the same time, Cu²⁺ ions gain those electrons to form solid copper, so copper is reduced.

Q: During electrolysis, positively charged ions move towards the
A) anode
B) cathode
C) electrolyte surface only
D) salt bridge
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Positive ions (cations) are attracted to the negatively charged electrode, called the cathode, where they gain electrons. Negative ions (anions) move to the positive electrode, the anode.

Q: The quantity of electricity required to deposit one mole of a singly charged ion is approximately
A) one electron charge
B) one Faraday
C) one mole of water
D) one volt
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: One Faraday (about 96,500 coulombs) is the charge carried by one mole of electrons — exactly the amount of charge needed to deposit one mole of a singly charged ion during electrolysis.

Q: The functional group characteristic of alcohols is
A) −COOH
B) −OH
C) −CHO
D) −COO−
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Alcohols are identified by the hydroxyl group, −OH, attached to a carbon chain. −COOH is the carboxylic acid group, and −CHO is the aldehyde group.

Q: Successive members of a homologous series differ by
A) CH
B) CH2
C) C2H2
D) H2
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Each member of a homologous series differs from the next by one CH2 unit (which adds 14 to the relative molecular mass), while all members share the same general formula and similar chemical properties.

Q: The general formula of an acyclic alkene is
A) CnH2n+2
B) CnH2n
C) CnH2n-2
D) CnHn
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Alkenes contain one carbon-to-carbon double bond and follow the general formula CnH2n. Alkanes (all single bonds) follow CnH2n+2, and alkynes (triple bonds) follow CnH2n-2.

Q: Which compound is unsaturated?
A) Methane
B) Ethane
C) Propane
D) Ethene
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Ethene contains a carbon-to-carbon double bond, making it unsaturated. Methane, ethane, and propane are alkanes, which contain only single bonds and are saturated.

Q: Ethene reacts with bromine water mainly by
A) substitution
B) addition
C) neutralization
D) condensation
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Ethene's double bond opens up so that bromine atoms can add directly across it, decolourising the bromine water. This is a typical addition reaction, characteristic of unsaturated compounds.

Q: Polyethene is formed from
A) ethane
B) ethene
C) ethanol
D) ethanoic acid
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Polyethene (polythene) is made by joining many ethene molecules together through addition polymerization, where the double bonds open up and the molecules link into a long chain.

Q: Compounds having the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements are called
A) isotopes
B) isomers
C) allotropes
D) homologues
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula (the same numbers and types of atoms) but arranged differently, which often gives them different physical or chemical properties.

Q: Fractional distillation of crude petroleum separates components mainly according to differences in their
A) density
B) colour
C) boiling points
D) viscosity only
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Crude oil is a mixture of hydrocarbons with different boiling points. Fractional distillation heats the mixture and collects each fraction as it condenses at its own characteristic boiling point.

Q: A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction by
A) increasing the enthalpy change
B) lowering activation energy
C) increasing product concentration permanently
D) changing the equilibrium constant
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. This allows more particles to react successfully per unit time, speeding up the reaction, while the catalyst itself is not used up.

Q: For a reversible reaction at equilibrium,
A) the reaction stops completely
B) reactants disappear
C) forward and backward reaction rates are equal
D) only products remain
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: At chemical equilibrium, the forward and reverse reactions are both still happening, but at exactly the same rate, so the concentrations of reactants and products stay constant over time even though the reaction hasn't stopped.

Q: For an exothermic equilibrium reaction, increasing temperature generally shifts equilibrium towards the
A) products
B) reactants
C) catalyst
D) solvent
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In an exothermic reaction, heat is released, almost like an extra "product." Raising the temperature pushes the equilibrium in the direction that absorbs the added heat — the reverse (endothermic) direction — which favours the reactants.

Q: Which factor generally increases the rate of reaction between a solid and a liquid?
A) Decreasing surface area
B) Increasing particle size
C) Increasing surface area
D) Lowering temperature
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Breaking a solid into smaller pieces increases its surface area, exposing more particles to the liquid at once. This raises the frequency of collisions between reacting particles, which speeds up the reaction.

Q: Which element is most likely to form a 2- ion?
A) Sodium
B) Magnesium
C) Oxygen
D) Aluminium
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Oxygen has 6 electrons in its outer shell and needs 2 more to complete a stable octet, so it readily gains 2 electrons to form the O²⁻ ion. Sodium, magnesium, and aluminium are metals that typically lose electrons to form positive ions instead.

Q: A white precipitate formed when silver nitrate solution is added to a solution containing chloride ions is
A) AgCl
B) AgBr
C) AgI
D) Ag2O
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Silver ions react with chloride ions to form silver chloride, an insoluble white precipitate. This reaction is the standard laboratory test for chloride ions.

Q: Temporary hardness of water is mainly caused by
A) sodium chloride
B) calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates
C) potassium nitrate
D) sodium hydroxide
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Temporary hardness comes from dissolved calcium and magnesium hydrogencarbonates. It's called "temporary" because boiling the water decomposes these compounds into insoluble carbonates, removing the hardness.

Q: The Haber process is used industrially to manufacture
A) sulfuric acid
B) ammonia
C) ethanol
D) sodium carbonate
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The Haber process combines nitrogen and hydrogen gases, under high pressure and with an iron catalyst, to produce ammonia (NH3), which is used mainly to make fertilizers.

Q: The Contact process is used to manufacture
A) nitric acid
B) sulfuric acid
C) hydrochloric acid
D) ethanoic acid
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The Contact process converts sulfur dioxide into sulfur trioxide, using a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst. The sulfur trioxide is then used to manufacture sulfuric acid.

Q: Acid rain is mainly associated with atmospheric oxides of
A) sodium and potassium
B) sulfur and nitrogen
C) calcium and magnesium
D) hydrogen and helium
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides, released mainly from burning fossil fuels, dissolve in atmospheric moisture to form sulfuric and nitric acids, which fall as acid rain.

Q: The emission of an alpha particle causes the mass number of a nucleus to decrease by
A) 1
B) 2
C) 4
D) 8
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: An alpha particle is made up of 2 protons and 2 neutrons (essentially a helium nucleus). Emitting one reduces the parent nucleus's mass number by 4 and its atomic number by 2.

Q: What volume of oxygen at STP is required for the complete combustion of 2 moles of methane? CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O (molar gas volume at STP = 22.4 dm³)
A) 22.4 dm³
B) 44.8 dm³
C) 67.2 dm³
D) 89.6 dm³
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: From the balanced equation, 1 mole of methane needs 2 moles of oxygen. So 2 moles of methane needs 2 × 2 = 4 moles of oxygen. Volume of oxygen = 4 × 22.4 = 89.6 dm³.
