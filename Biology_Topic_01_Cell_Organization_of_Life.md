# TOPIC 1: THE CELL ORGANIZATION OF LIFE
(JAMB Past Questions — 1978 to 2014)

Q: Which of the following is characteristic of the animal cell?
A) Presence of chloroplasts
B) Possession of a cellulose cell wall
C) Absence of large vacuoles
D) Presence of large vacuoles
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Animal cells only have small, scattered vacuoles (unlike plant cells, which have one large permanent central vacuole). Chloroplasts and cellulose cell walls belong to plant cells, and chromosomes/genetic material are present in the cells of all living organisms, so they cannot be used to distinguish animal cells specifically.

Q: All living organisms
A) photosynthesize
B) respire
C) move
D) feed
E) transpire
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Respiration (the release of energy from food) is a life process common to every living organism, plant or animal. Photosynthesis is restricted to green plants and some bacteria/algae; visible movement, feeding, and transpiration are not shown by every organism (e.g. plants do not "feed" the way animals do, and transpiration is a plant-specific water loss process).

Q: The vacuole of a plant cell is
A) a large empty space
B) smaller than that of an animal cell
C) filled with air
D) an ordinary vacuum
E) surrounded by a membrane
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The plant cell vacuole is bounded by a special membrane called the tonoplast, which controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell sap it contains. It is not empty (it is filled with cell sap, not air or a vacuum), and it is actually much larger than an animal cell's vacuole, not smaller.

Q: The part of the cell solely responsible for respiration is the
A) nucleus
B) nucleolus
C) mitochondria
D) Golgi apparatus
E) endoplasmic reticulum
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Mitochondria are the sites of aerobic respiration, where energy stored in food is converted to ATP through the Krebs cycle and the electron transport chain. This is why mitochondria are nicknamed the "powerhouse of the cell." The other organelles listed have entirely different functions (heredity, ribosome production, packaging/secretion, and transport respectively).

Q: Which of these combinations is common to plant and animal cells?
A) Centriole, middle lamella, Golgi bodies, mitochondrion
B) Cytoplasm, sap vacuole, starch grains, leucoplasts
C) Plasma membrane, chromosomes, mitochondria, lysosomes
D) Nucleus, nucleolus, cellulose cell wall, endoplasmic reticulum
E) Cytoplasm, centriole, cellulose cell wall, nucleolus
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Plasma membrane, chromosomes, mitochondria, and lysosomes are found in both plant and animal cells. Centrioles are normally absent in higher plant cells, cellulose cell walls and starch grains/leucoplasts and sap vacuoles are plant-specific features, so any option containing these cannot be "common to both."

Q: Where is energy produced in a cell?
A) Nucleus
B) Nucleolus
C) Lysosomes
D) Chloroplast
E) Mitochondria
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Energy (in the form of ATP) is generated in the mitochondria during aerobic respiration. The nucleus controls heredity, the nucleolus makes ribosomes, lysosomes digest materials, and chloroplasts trap light energy for photosynthesis (a different process from respiration, which releases energy).

Q: Which of the following cell constituents is NOT common to both plants and animals?
A) Mitochondria
B) Chloroplasts
C) Ribosomes
D) Golgi apparatus
E) Vacuoles
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Chloroplasts are the organelles responsible for photosynthesis and are found only in plant cells (and some protists), never in animal cells. Mitochondria, ribosomes, Golgi apparatus, and (small) vacuoles are present in both plant and animal cells.

Q: A group of similar cells performing the same function is
A) an organ
B) a system
C) an organelle
D) a tissue
E) an enzyme
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: This is the definition of a tissue — a collection of similar cells working together to perform a specific function (e.g. muscle tissue). An organ is made of different tissues working together; a system is made of organs; an organelle is a structure inside a single cell; an enzyme is a biological catalyst, not a cell grouping at all.

Q: The function of endoplasmic reticulum is
A) protein synthesis
B) intracellular transport of materials
C) digestion and destruction of foreign bodies
D) production of energy from glucose
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) forms a network of membranes/channels that transports materials (proteins, lipids) around inside the cell. Protein synthesis itself is carried out by ribosomes (which may be attached to the rough ER), digestion of foreign material is done by lysosomes, and energy production from glucose happens in the mitochondria.

Q: The oxidative part of the respiration process takes place in the
A) mitochondria
B) ribosomes
C) endoplasmic reticulum
D) Golgi bodies
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The oxidative stages of respiration (the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation/electron transport chain, which both require oxygen) occur inside the mitochondria. Only the very first, non-oxidative stage (glycolysis) occurs outside the mitochondria, in the cytoplasm.

Q: The function of ribosomes in cells is
A) protein synthesis
B) starch synthesis
C) transport of materials
D) lipid storage
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Ribosomes are the cell structures where amino acids are joined together to form proteins (translation of mRNA). They play no role in starch synthesis, general material transport (that is the ER's job), or lipid storage.

Q: Which of the following structures is common to Euglena, white blood cell and Amoeba?
A) Vacuole
B) Cell wall
C) Cilia
D) Cell membrane
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A cell (surface) membrane is a universal feature of all cells, including Euglena, white blood cells, and Amoeba. Cell walls are absent in all three (they are all "naked" cells without a rigid wall), cilia are not a defining shared feature here, and while contractile vacuoles occur in Euglena and Amoeba, white blood cells do not have one.

Q: Which of the following structures is associated with aerobic respiration?
A) 1
B) 2
C) 3
D) 4
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The structure labelled 1 in the diagram represents the mitochondrion, the organelle where the oxygen-requiring (aerobic) stages of respiration take place.

Q: The structure labelled 5 is used for
A) protein synthesis
B) energy production
C) secretion
D) excretion
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Position 5 corresponds to ribosomes (or rough ER studded with ribosomes) in the diagram, which are the structures responsible for synthesizing proteins from amino acids.

Q: The organelle involved in tissue respiration is the
A) endoplasmic reticulum
B) ribosome
C) Golgi body
D) mitochondrion
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: "Tissue respiration" refers to the cellular process of releasing energy from food, which takes place in the mitochondrion — this organelle contains the enzymes for the Krebs cycle and electron transport chain.

Q: A tissue can best be defined as
A) an aggregate of cells
B) an aggregate of cells performing a similar function
C) an aggregate of similar cells performing the same function
D) a mixture of different cell types performing the same function
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The precise biological definition of a tissue requires the cells to be similar (alike in structure/origin) AND working together to perform one common function — option C captures both conditions, while the other options are either too vague or describe a mixture of different cell types, which would actually describe an organ, not a tissue.

Q: The membrane surrounding the vacuole in a plant cell is called the
A) plasmalemma
B) tonoplast
C) nuclear membrane
D) endoplasmic reticulum
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The specific membrane that encloses the central vacuole and its cell sap in a plant cell is called the tonoplast. The plasmalemma is the outer cell surface membrane, and the other two options refer to entirely different structures (nucleus and cytoplasmic membrane network).

Q: Which of the following organelles are likely to be present in cells that are actively respiring and photosynthesizing?
A) Nucleolus and centrioles
B) Mitochondria and chloroplast
C) Lysosomes and ribosomes
D) Golgi apparatus and endoplasmic reticulum
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Respiration occurs in mitochondria and photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts, so a cell actively doing both (e.g. a green leaf mesophyll cell in daylight) would show large numbers of both organelles.

Q: The nucleus is considered the control organelle of a cell because it
A) contains the genetic material
B) contains the nuclear sap
C) is bounded by the nuclear membrane
D) is located at the centre of the cell
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The nucleus controls all cell activities because it houses the DNA (chromatin/genetic material), which carries the coded instructions for making proteins and regulating every cell process. The nuclear membrane, nuclear sap, and central position are structural features, not the reason it exercises control.

Q: The structure that is most commonly identified in all living cells under the light microscope is the
A) mitochondrion
B) chloroplast
C) nucleus
D) ribosome
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The nucleus is large and stains densely, making it the most easily seen and identified structure in cells under an ordinary light microscope. Mitochondria and ribosomes are generally too small to resolve clearly with a light microscope, and chloroplasts are absent from animal cells.

Q: Which of the following is an organ?
A) guard cell
B) liver
C) xylem bundle
D) blood
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The liver is a distinct organ made up of different tissues (epithelial, connective, vascular, etc.) working together for a common overall function. A guard cell is a single cell, a xylem bundle/vessel is a tissue, and blood is a (liquid connective) tissue, not an organ.

Q: In plant cells, the chloroplasts are located
A) inside the cell wall
B) outside the protoplasm
C) within the vacuole
D) within the cytoplasm
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Chloroplasts are cytoplasmic organelles — they float within the cytoplasm of the cell (which itself lies inside the cell wall), not inside the vacuole and not outside the living protoplasm.

Q: Which of the following organisms does not exist as a single free-living cell?
A) Paramecium
B) Volvox
C) Amoeba
D) Chlamydomonas
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Volvox is a colonial organism — many individual cells are grouped together to form a hollow ball-shaped colony, so a single Volvox cell does not normally live independently. Paramecium, Amoeba, and Chlamydomonas are all true free-living unicellular organisms.

Q: An Amoeba and an unlaid chicken egg are
A) animal tissues
B) organelles
C) single cell
D) organisms
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Both an Amoeba and an unfertilized/unlaid chicken egg (the ovum) consist of just one single cell, even though the chicken egg cell is unusually large. Neither is a tissue (many cells), an organelle (a sub-cellular structure), nor, on its own, a fully developed multicellular organism.

Q: The structures found only in plant cells are
A) cell membrane and cytoplasm
B) chromatin and nucleolus
C) cell wall and chloroplast
D) cell membrane and lysosome
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A cellulose cell wall and chloroplasts are structures unique to plant cells (and are absent from typical animal cells). Cell membrane, cytoplasm, chromatin, nucleolus, and lysosomes are all found in both plant and animal cells.

Q: The rods in the retina of the eye are examples of
A) cells
B) tissues
C) organs
D) systems
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Rods (like cones) are individual photoreceptor cells found in the retina that detect light. They are single specialized cells, not a tissue, organ, or system on their own.

Q: The organelle common to both plant and animal cell is
A) centriole
B) plasmalemma
C) cell wall
D) chloroplast
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The plasmalemma (cell surface/plasma membrane) is present in every living cell, plant or animal. Centrioles are typically absent in higher plants, while cell walls and chloroplasts are plant-specific structures absent in animal cells.

Q: Which of the following is likely to have a higher concentration of mitochondria?
A) Sperm cell
B) White blood cell
C) Egg cell
D) Red blood cell
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A sperm cell needs a large amount of energy to power the whip-like movement of its tail as it swims through the female reproductive tract to reach the egg, so its mid-piece is packed with mitochondria. Mature red blood cells in mammals actually lack mitochondria (and even a nucleus) entirely.

Q: The role of the Golgi complex in a eukaryotic cell is to
A) conduct ions in and out of the cell
B) transport genetic material out of the cell
C) provide attachment for ribosomal granules
D) transport organic material in and out of the cell
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The Golgi complex (apparatus) processes, packages, and transports substances such as proteins and other organic materials, often forming secretory vesicles that move materials into or out of the cell. It does not conduct ions, move genetic material, or provide attachment sites for ribosomes (that is a function of rough ER).

A) III
B) IV

ANSWER: D

A) nuclear membrane
B) cell wall
C) endoplasmic reticulum
D) plasmalemma
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The double membrane enclosing the nucleus in the diagram, labelled I, is the nuclear membrane (nuclear envelope), which separates the nuclear contents from the surrounding cytoplasm and has pores that allow material exchange.

Q: A characteristic that can possibly be shared by both living and non-living things is
A) locomotion
B) irritability
C) increase in biomass
D) increase in size
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Both living and non-living things can increase in size — for example, a crystal or a stalactite can grow larger by adding material to its outer surface, even though it is not alive. However, this growth is purely additive from outside, unlike true biological growth (increase in biomass through internal assimilation of food), which is unique to living things and is why "increase in biomass" is not a shared characteristic. Locomotion (self-directed movement) and irritability (response to stimuli) are also hallmarks of life not shared with non-living matter.

Q: The cell of an onion bulb can be differentiated from a cheek cell by the presence of
A) plasmalemma
B) chloroplast
C) cell wall
D) nucleus
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: An onion bulb cell is a plant cell and therefore possesses a rigid cellulose cell wall, which a human cheek (epithelial) cell lacks. Both cell types have a plasmalemma and a nucleus, and neither typically contains chloroplasts (onion bulb cells store food but are not photosynthetic; cheek cells are animal cells).

Q: What is the level of organization of an onion bulb?
A) Tissue
B) Organ
C) Systemic
D) Organismal
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: An onion bulb is not just a single organ — it is an entire underground shoot system (comprising the stem and fleshy leaf bases) combined with its root system, together forming a complete organism capable of independent growth.

Q: A characteristic exhibited by all living organisms is
A) sexual reproduction
B) aerobic respiration
C) the ability to move from one place to another
D) the ability to remove unwanted substances
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: All living organisms must excrete — that is, remove metabolic waste products — to avoid the build-up of toxic substances. Not all organisms reproduce sexually (many reproduce asexually), not all respire aerobically (some respire anaerobically), and not all organisms are capable of locomotion (e.g. plants are fixed in place).

Q: In a cell, the genes are carried by
A) nuclear membranes
B) chromatin threads
C) lysosomes
D) mitochondria
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Genes are segments of DNA, and DNA in the nucleus exists in the form of chromatin threads (DNA wound around histone proteins), which condense into visible chromosomes during cell division. The nuclear membrane simply encloses the nucleus, while lysosomes and mitochondria are unrelated organelles with digestive and respiratory roles respectively (mitochondria do carry a small separate DNA, but this is not what is being tested here).

Q: Which of the following characterizes a mature plant cell?
A) The nucleus is pushed to the centre of the cell
B) The cell wall is made up of cellulose
C) The nucleus is small and irregular in shape
D) The cytoplasm fills up the entire cell space
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A defining feature of plant cells at every stage is a cell wall made of cellulose. As a plant cell matures, the large central vacuole actually enlarges and pushes the nucleus and a thin layer of cytoplasm to the edge of the cell (the opposite of options A and D), while the nucleus retains a normal defined shape.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a function of the nucleus of a cell?
A) It translates genetic information for the manufacture of proteins
B) It stores and carries hereditary information
C) It is a reservoir of energy for the cell
D) It controls the life processes of the cell
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Storing and releasing energy is the job of the mitochondrion, not the nucleus. The nucleus does store hereditary information (DNA), directs protein manufacture by sending instructions (mRNA) to the ribosomes, and generally controls the cell's activities.

A) Golgi apparatus and endoplasmic reticulum
B) nucleus and centrioles
C) mitochondria and chloroplast
D) lysosomes and ribosomes
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, respiration takes place in mitochondria and photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts, so an actively respiring and photosynthesizing cell (such as a leaf cell in sunlight) will be rich in both organelles.

Q: The lowest level of organization in living organisms is
A) organ
B) cell
C) system
D) tissue
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The hierarchy of biological organization runs from cell → tissue → organ → system → organism, in increasing order of complexity. The cell is therefore the most basic (lowest) level of organization that can still be considered a complete living unit.

Q: Which of the following is the most complex according to their cellular level of organization?
A) Heart
B) Hair
C) Euglena
D) Hydra
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The heart is a full organ, built from several different tissue types (muscle, connective, nervous, vascular) working together — this makes it the most complex option listed. Hair is simpler tissue-level material, Euglena is a single-celled organism, and although Hydra is a whole organism, its body organization does not progress beyond the tissue level, so it remains simpler than a true organ like the heart.

Q: Which of the following organisms is multicellular?
A) Chlamydomonas
B) Spirogyra
C) Amoeba
D) Euglena
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Spirogyra is a filamentous green alga made up of a chain of many cells joined end to end, making it multicellular. Chlamydomonas, Amoeba, and Euglena are all single-celled (unicellular) organisms.
