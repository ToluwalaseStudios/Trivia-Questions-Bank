Q: A small quantity of solid ammonium chloride (NH4Cl) was heated gently in a test-tube, the solid gradually disappeared to produce a mixture of two gases. Later a white cloudy deposit was observed on the cooler part of the test tube. The ammonium chloride is said to have undergone
A) distillation
B) sublimation
C) precipitation
D) evaporation
E) decomposition
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: NH4Cl decomposes on heating into NH3 and HCl gases, which recombine on the cooler part of the tube to reform solid NH4Cl - this solid-to-gas-to-solid change without melting is sublimation.

Q: Which of the following changes is a physical change?
A) adding iron fillings to aerated water
B) adding sodium metal to water
C) cooling a solution of iron(II) sulphate to obtain the hydrated salt
D) cooling water to obtain ice
E) burning domestic gas (Utilgas)
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Cooling water to ice only changes its physical state; no new substance is formed, unlike the other options which involve chemical reactions.

Q: By means of filtration one component can be obtained pure from an aqueous mixture of sodium chloride and
A) potassium nitrate
B) sand
C) lead nitrate
D) sugar (glucose)
E) starch
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Sand is insoluble in water, so it can be filtered out of a sodium chloride solution, leaving pure sand as residue and NaCl solution as filtrate.

Q: Methanoic acid mixes with water in all proportions and has the same boiling point as water. Which method would you adopt to obtain pure water from sand and methanoic acid?
A) fractional distillation
B) filtration followed by distillation
C) neutralization with sodium hydroxide followed by distillation
D) neutralization with sodium hydroxide followed by filtration
E) esterification with ethanol followed by distillation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Since methanoic acid and water share the same boiling point, distillation alone cannot separate them. Neutralizing the acid with NaOH converts it to non-volatile sodium methanoate, allowing pure water to be distilled off.

Q: One of the following techniques can be used to show that chlorophyll pigments is a mixture of chemical compounds and not a single coloured compound
A) crystallization
B) hydrolysis
C) sublimation
D) filtration
E) chromatography
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Chromatography separates a mixture into its different coloured components based on their differing movement through a medium, revealing chlorophyll is made of several pigments.

Q: In an attempt to remove sugar from a beaker containing sand and sugar, 2M ammonium chloride solution was accidentally added instead of water. Which method could be used to remove ammonium chloride from the mixture?
A) fractional distillation
B) crystallization
C) filtration followed by sublimation
D) evaporation followed by sublimation
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: After filtering out the sand, the sugar and dissolved ammonium chloride remain in solution. Evaporating the water gives a solid mixture, and heating sublimes off the ammonium chloride, leaving pure sugar.

Q: Which of the following compounds and elements would sublime on exposure to the atmosphere?

Q: Which of the following processes does NOT lead to chemical change?

EXPLANATION: Stirring sodium carbonate in water is simple dissolution - no new substance is formed, so it is a physical change, unlike the others which involve reactions.

Q: A mixture of common salt, ammonium chloride and barium sulphate can be best separated by

B) addition of water followed by sublimation then filtration
C) sublimation followed by addition of water then filtration

EXPLANATION: First sublime off the ammonium chloride. Then add water to dissolve the common salt (barium sulphate is insoluble), and filter to remove the barium sulphate, leaving a salt solution.

EXPLANATION: Brine is a solution of salt in water; evaporating the water leaves the solid sodium chloride behind.

Q: In an experiment, which of the following observations would suggest that a solid sample is a mixture?
A) The solid can be ground to a fine powder

EXPLANATION: Pure substances melt sharply at a fixed temperature. Melting over a wide range (573K-648K) indicates the presence of impurities, i.e. a mixture.

Q: Changes in the physical states of a chemical substance T are shown in a scheme where Liquid T connects to Solid T (Z), Liquid T connects to Gaseous T (Y), and Solid T connects directly to Gaseous T (X). The letters X, Y and Z respectively represent

B) sublimation, vaporization and solidification

Q: A mixture of sand, ammonium chloride and sodium chloride is best separated by
