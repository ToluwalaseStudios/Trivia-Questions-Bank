# TOPIC 14: EXCRETION, EXCRETORY PRODUCTS

Q: The function of lenticels is
A) to remove excess water in the plant
B) to absorb water from the atmosphere
C) for gaseous exchange
D) to absorb light
E) to store light
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Lenticels are small pores found on stems (and some roots), specifically allowing gaseous exchange (oxygen in, carbon dioxide out) directly with the atmosphere through the otherwise impermeable bark/cork layer.

Q: Which one of the following statements about the mammalian kidney is false?
A) The glomerulus is formed by a branch of the renal artery
B) The glomerulus is a part of uriniferous tubules
C) The Bowman's capsule is in the cortex of the kidney
D) The uriniferous tubules are richly supplied with blood vessels
E) The glomerulus is lodged in the Bowman's capsule
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Strictly speaking, the glomerulus (a tuft of capillaries) is not itself part of the uriniferous tubule structure — it is a separate blood vessel structure that sits enclosed within the Bowman's capsule, which is the actual start of the tubule. The other statements are accurate: the glomerulus does form from a renal artery branch, sits within the Bowman's capsule, the capsule is in the cortex, and the tubules are richly vascularized.

Q: A potometer with a leafy shoot weighed 18.9g initially and 17.9g after 1 hour. From this it follows that
A) the shoot absorbed 1g of water per hour
B) 1g of water per hour evaporates from the potometer
C) the shoot lost 1g of water per hour
D) the potometer water receded at the rate of 1g per hour
E) the shoot was respiring and lost weight at the rate of 1g per hour
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A potometer measures water uptake/loss by a leafy shoot, and the observed 1g weight loss over the hour directly reflects the shoot losing water through transpiration at a rate of 1g per hour.

Q: Which of these is not a function of the mammalian skin?
A) Protection against bacterial infection
B) Excretion of wastes and water
C) Production of vitamin D when exposed to light
D) Production of pigments to reduce temperature

EXPLANATION: Skin pigments (like melanin) primarily function to protect against UV light damage and determine skin colour — they are not produced specifically "to reduce temperature." Protection, excretion (via sweat), vitamin D production, and temperature regulation are all genuine, well-established skin functions.

Q: A potometer was used to compare the rate of loss of the same volume of water from a plant under different environmental conditions such as under a fan, at the sea shore, under the sun, in the airy laboratory, and in a cupboard. Which of the items was likely to have been obtained in a cupboard?
A) 15 secs
B) 30 secs
C) 25 secs
D) 20 secs

ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A cupboard is an enclosed space with minimal air movement and humidity buildup, meaning transpiration would proceed most slowly there — this slowest rate corresponds to the longest recorded time (30 seconds) for the same volume of water loss, compared to faster conditions like under a fan.

Q: Which of these products obtained from analyzing sweat from the skin is not an excretory product?

ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Dust is simply external debris that may accumulate on the skin's surface — it is not a genuine metabolic waste product excreted by the body, unlike water, salts, urea, and urates, all of which are true excretory products found in sweat.

Q: Which of the following is not an excretory organ?
A) Lungs
B) Kidney
C) Leaf
D) Large intestine
E) Skin
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The large intestine's main role is absorbing water from undigested food residue and forming/eliminating faeces — since faeces (undigested food) is not technically a product of the body's own metabolism, the large intestine is not considered a true excretory organ, unlike the lungs, kidney, leaf (in plants), and skin, which all remove genuine metabolic waste products.

Q: Which one of the following is not an excretory product?

E) Carbon dioxide
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established in the previous question, faeces consists of undigested food material, not a product of the body's internal metabolism, so it is technically not classified as an excretory product, unlike urine, sweat, excess salts, and carbon dioxide.

Q: The major function of the contractile vacuole in Amoeba is to
A) secrete enzymes to digest food particles
B) store excess food materials
C) excrete nitrogenous wastes
D) absorb water from the surroundings
E) collect and expel excess water from the cytoplasm
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The contractile vacuole's specific role is osmoregulation — continually collecting the excess water that enters the Amoeba by osmosis (since it lives in a hypotonic freshwater environment) and periodically expelling this water from the cell to prevent it from bursting.

Q: Which one of the following parts of the mammalian body is most closely associated with the production of urine?
A) Malpighian capsule
B) urinary bladder
C) Ureter
D) vas deferens
E) Urethra
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The Malpighian capsule (Bowman's capsule, containing the glomerulus) is where the initial filtration step of urine production begins — the urinary bladder, ureter, and urethra are all involved in storing and transporting the already-formed urine, not producing it.

Q: A potometer is used to determine the rate at which a shoot

B) sucks air bubbles
C) loses weight
D) absorbs water
E) transpires
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: A potometer is specifically designed to measure the rate of transpiration (water loss) from a leafy shoot, typically by tracking the movement of an air bubble as water is drawn up to replace what is lost.

Q: During excretion of urea there is also a corresponding re-absorption of water into the blood. This re-absorption takes place in the
A) uriniferous tubules
B) Bowman's capsule
C) glomerulus
D) Malpighian capsule
E) renal artery
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As filtrate passes along the uriniferous (renal) tubules, useful substances and a significant proportion of the filtered water are selectively reabsorbed back into the surrounding blood capillaries, concentrating the remaining fluid into urine.

Q: The amount of water loss from a leaf can be detected using
A) lime-water
B) red litmus paper
C) blue litmus paper
D) blue cobalt chloride paper
E) pink cobalt chloride paper
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Blue cobalt chloride paper is specifically used to detect water, as it changes colour from blue to pink upon contact with moisture — this colour change allows it to indicate and roughly measure the amount of water vapour lost from a leaf's surface.

Q: The rate of transpiration of a leafy shoot would be highest under
A) damp, cold, still air
B) damp, warm, moving air
C) dry, warm, moving air
D) dry, cold, moving air
E) dry, warm, still air
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Transpiration is fastest under conditions that maximize the evaporation gradient and remove humid air quickly — dry air (low humidity increases the gradient), warm temperature (increases evaporation rate), and moving air (continually replaces humid air near the leaf surface with drier air) together create the ideal combination for maximum transpiration.

Q: The functional unit of a mammalian kidney is the
A) urethra
B) malpighian capsule
C) glomerulus
D) nephron
E) ureter
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The nephron (comprising the Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, and the full length of the renal tubule including the loop of Henle and collecting duct) is the complete functional unit responsible for carrying out the entire process of urine formation within the kidney.

Q: Which of the following is not associated with excretion in mammals?

B) Urea

D) Carbondioxide
E) Bowman's capsule
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, faeces is not a true excretory product (it is undigested food waste, not a product of internal metabolism) — the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule are structures directly involved in excretion, and urea/carbon dioxide are genuine excretory products.

Q: Which of the following substances is NOT found in urine?
A) Water
B) Sodium chloride
C) Nitrogenous compounds
D) Calcium chloride
E) Nitrogenous salts
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Calcium chloride is not a normal, characteristic component of urine, unlike water, sodium chloride, and various nitrogenous compounds/salts (such as urea and uric acid), which are all standard constituents of healthy urine.

Q: The kidneys of all vertebrates act as osmo regulators. This means that they
A) keep the composition of plasma constant
B) regulate osmotic processes
C) Control the volume of blood entering the kidneys
D) decrease the osmotic pressure of blood
E) increase the osmotic pressure of blood
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Being an "osmoregulator" specifically means the kidneys work to maintain the blood plasma's composition (particularly its water and solute balance) at a relatively constant, stable level, rather than simply increasing or decreasing osmotic pressure in one fixed direction.

Q: Which of the following statements about the rate of transpiration is INCORRECT? It is
A) dependent on temperature
B) affected by changes in light intensity
C) unaffected by humidity
D) dependent on air movement
E) affected by availability of water
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Humidity is actually one of the most significant factors affecting transpiration rate — high humidity slows transpiration (by reducing the water vapour concentration gradient), while low humidity speeds it up — making the claim that transpiration is "unaffected by humidity" the incorrect statement.

Q: Urea is produced in the
A) liver
B) Ladder (bladder)
C) spleen
D) kidneys
E) gallbladder
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Urea is specifically synthesized in the liver, through the process of deamination and the ornithine (urea) cycle, converting toxic ammonia from excess amino acids into the less toxic urea, which the kidneys then filter out and excrete.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a waste product of plants?
A) Tannins
B) Oxygen
C) Carbondioxide
D) Sap
E) Alkaloids
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Sap (the fluid containing water, dissolved sugars, and nutrients) is a substance actively transported and used within the plant, not a waste product — tannins, alkaloids, and gases like oxygen/carbon dioxide (depending on the metabolic process) are all genuine excretory products of plants.

Q: The function of the loop of Henle is to
A) increase the flow of urine

EXPLANATION: The loop of Henle's primary role is to establish and maintain a strong concentration gradient of sodium chloride within the kidney's medulla, which subsequently allows the collecting duct to reabsorb water efficiently and produce concentrated (hypertonic) urine.
