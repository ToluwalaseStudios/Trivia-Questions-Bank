# TOPIC 11: DIGESTIVE SYSTEM, DIGESTIVE ENZYME, DENTITION
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: When proteins are broken down they also provide
A) oxygen
B) carbohydrates
C) energy
D) amino acids
E) enzyme
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The final products of protein digestion (hydrolysis) are amino acids, the individual building-block units that made up the original protein chain.

Q: The mammalian stomach can carry out a number of processes except
A) absorption of glucose
B) secretion of hydrochloric acid
C) secretion of gastric juice
D) churning of food
E) production of trypsin
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Trypsin is produced by the pancreas, not the stomach. The stomach genuinely does secrete hydrochloric acid and gastric juice (containing pepsin and rennin), churns food mechanically, and can absorb only very limited substances (glucose absorption mainly occurs later, in the small intestine, though minimal absorption in the stomach itself is not its primary role).

Q: Which of these is the terminal portion of the alimentary canal of a mammal?
A) Oesophagus
B) Stomach
C) Rectum
D) Colon
E) Appendix
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The rectum is the final section of the alimentary canal, immediately preceding the anus, where faecal waste is temporarily stored before elimination.

Q: Which of these is not an enzyme?
A) Pepsin
B) Gastrin
C) Amylase
D) Chymotrypsin
E) Trypsin
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Gastrin is a hormone (produced by the stomach lining, stimulating acid secretion), not an enzyme — pepsin, amylase, chymotrypsin, and trypsin are all genuine digestive enzymes.

Q: The digestive enzyme that coagulates protein in milk is
A) ptyalin
B) pepsin
C) renin
D) trypsin
E) amylase
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Renin (rennin) specifically curdles milk by converting soluble caseinogen protein into insoluble casein, which then gets broken down further by pepsin — this specific curdling action is unique to renin among the enzymes listed.

Q: The arrangements below are steps in protein digestion. Which is the correct sequence? (a) polypeptides (b) protein (c) amino acids (d) peptones
A) a→b→c→d
B) c→d→a→b
C) b→c→a→d
D) b→d→a→c
E) b→a→c→d
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Protein digestion proceeds through progressively smaller fragments: the whole protein (b) is first broken into peptones (d), then into polypeptides (a), and finally into individual amino acids (c) — giving the correct sequence b→d→a→c.

Q: Which of the following organs produces bile?
A) Gall bladder
B) Pancreas
C) Spleen
D) Liver
E) Stomach
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Bile is produced by the liver, then stored and concentrated in the gall bladder (not produced there), before being released into the duodenum when needed for fat digestion.

Q: Which of the following food substances is digested in the stomach?
A) Carbohydrates
B) Fats and Oils
C) fats and proteins
D) Proteins
E) Carbohydrates and Fats
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The stomach's digestive enzymes (pepsin and rennin) act specifically on proteins; carbohydrate digestion (started by salivary ptyalin) is actually paused in the acidic stomach environment, and fat digestion mainly occurs later in the small intestine, not the stomach.

EXPLANATION: The correct human dental formula is 2 incisors, 1 canine, 2 premolars, and 3 molars per quadrant (i2/2, c1/1, pm2/2, m3/3), giving a total of 32 teeth — matching option E exactly.

Q: The kind of teeth used for tearing food materials is the
A) fang
B) canine
C) premolar
D) carnassial
E) Incisor
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The canine teeth, being pointed and prominent, are specifically adapted for gripping and tearing flesh or food materials, distinct from incisors (cutting/nibbling) or premolars/molars (grinding/crushing).

Q: Milk protein is hydrolysed by
A) renin
B) amylyse
C) lipase
D) lactase
E) pepsin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, renin (rennin) is the specific enzyme responsible for curdling (coagulating) milk protein (caseinogen), the first step before pepsin further hydrolyses the resulting casein.

Q: All the following are digestive enzymes except
A) bile
B) lipase
C) maltase
D) pepsin
E) ptyalin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Bile is not an enzyme at all — it is a fluid secretion that physically emulsifies fats (breaking them into smaller droplets) to aid enzymatic digestion, but it contains no digestive enzymes itself, unlike lipase, maltase, pepsin, and ptyalin.

Q: The correct route for a piece of bread eaten by a mammal is
A) oral cavity-oesophagus-stomach-ileum-rectum
B) oral cavity-stomach-oesophagus-duodenum-ileum-rectum

D) oral cavity-oesophagus-ileum-stomach-duodenum-rectum
E) oral cavity-oesophagus-stomach-ileum-duodenum-rectum
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Although this option's listed order technically omits an explicit separate mention of the duodenum (implicitly included within "ileum" as part of the small intestine sequence), it is the only option that keeps the organs in a broadly correct anatomical order: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum/ileum) → rectum, unlike the other options, which incorrectly reorder the stomach, oesophagus, or duodenum out of sequence.

Q: In the enzyme reaction starch → sugar (via amylase), starch is referred to as the
A) substrate
B) product
C) enzymes
D) enzymes-substrate complex
E) reaction mixture
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In any enzyme-catalyzed reaction, the starting substance that the enzyme acts upon (here, starch) is called the substrate, while the resulting substance (sugar) is called the product.

Q: If the gall bladder of a man is removed by surgery, which of the following processes will be most seriously affected?
A) Digestion of fats and oil
B) Formation of Urea
C) Digestion of starch
D) conversion of proteins
E) Storage and release of urine
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Since the gall bladder's specific role is to store and release bile (which emulsifies fats to aid their digestion), its removal would most seriously affect the efficient digestion of fats and oils — the liver would still produce bile, but its convenient storage and controlled release would be lost.

Q: The mammalian pancreas is involved with the
A) digestion of food only
B) digestion and absorption of food
C) digestion of food and regulation of blood sugar
D) digestion of hormone and the regulation of blood sugar
E) digestion of food, and the production of insulin
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The pancreas has this important dual role: its exocrine function produces digestive enzymes (for food digestion), and its endocrine function produces insulin and glucagon (for regulating blood sugar levels) — matching option C's description of both roles together.

Q: Which of the following statements about the dentition of man is INCORRECT?
A) more molars than incisors
B) no diastema
C) the same number of teeth on upper and lower jaws
D) a total of thirty-two teeth
E) a total of six molars
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Man has a total of 12 molars (3 molars × 2 sides × 2 jaws = 12), not just six, making this the incorrect statement. Man genuinely has more molars (12) than incisors (8), no diastema, an equal tooth count on both jaws, and a total of 32 teeth overall.

Q: The mouth parts of the housefly are adapted for
A) lapping and sponging
B) sucking and chewing
C) piercing and sucking
D) chewing and lapping
E) biting and chewing
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The housefly digests solid food externally by regurgitating saliva onto it, then uses its spongy, sponge-like proboscis to lap and soak up (sponge) the resulting liquefied food — a feeding method distinct from biting/chewing insects or piercing-sucking insects like mosquitoes.

Q: Which of the following statements is NOT true of enzymes? They
A) are proteins
B) need cofactors to activate them
C) are sensitive to hydrogen ion
D) can withstand high temperatures
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Enzymes are proteins, and like all proteins, they are denatured (permanently lose their functional shape and activity) by high temperatures — making the claim that they "can withstand high temperatures" the false statement. Enzymes genuinely can require cofactors, and their activity is indeed sensitive to pH (hydrogen ion concentration).

Q: Pepsin is a digestive enzyme which breaks
A) cellulose into glucose molecules
B) carbohydrates into simple sugars
C) protein into peptones
D) fats into glycerol and fatty acids
E) sucrose into glucose and fructose
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Pepsin is a protein-digesting (proteolytic) enzyme found in the stomach, specifically breaking down proteins into smaller peptone/polypeptide fragments — it plays no role in digesting carbohydrates, cellulose, fats, or sucrose.

Q: The removal of a man's pancreas by surgical operation can affect only the digestion of
A) starch
B) protein and fats
C) oils and fats
D) proteins
E) carbohydrate and fats
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The pancreas produces enzymes for digesting carbohydrates (amylopsin), fats (steapsin/lipase), and proteins (trypsin) — but since some protein digestion can still occur via stomach pepsin, the most directly and exclusively pancreas-dependent digestive processes affected are those of carbohydrates and fats.

Q: The presence of diastema in the jawbone indicates that the mammal lacks the teeth suitable for
A) tearing
B) chewing
C) cutting
D) chewing and tearing
E) cutting and grinding
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A diastema (a gap in the jaw, typically where canines would normally sit) indicates the animal lacks the canine teeth needed for tearing/gripping flesh — consistent with the jaw structure of herbivores, which have no functional need for tearing teeth.

Q: Which of the following represents the sequence of protein hydrolysis? (1) Polypeptides (2) Amino acids (3) Proteins (4) Peptones

Q: Partially digested food ready to leave the stomach is referred to as
A) chyme
B) curd
C) glycogen
D) paste
E) roughage
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The semi-liquid, partially digested food mass that leaves the stomach and enters the duodenum is specifically termed chyme (distinct from "chyle," the term used for the similarly partially digested food ready to leave the duodenum after further digestion).

Q: The three important organs that are situated close to the stomach are
A) kidney, liver and gall bladder
B) pancreas, liver and kidney
C) liver, kidney and spleen
D) gall bladder, pancreas and spleen
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The gall bladder (embedded in a lobe of the liver near the stomach), the pancreas (nestled in the duodenal loop close by), and the spleen (attached along the stomach's undersurface) are all anatomically positioned close to the stomach, unlike the kidneys, which sit further back in the abdominal cavity.

Q: Evidence that a tooth is a living part of the mammalian body can be found within the
A) gum
B) pulp cavity
C) cement
D) enamel
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The pulp cavity, located at the centre of the tooth, contains living tissue — including blood vessels and nerves — providing direct evidence that the tooth is a living structure, unlike the enamel or cement, which are hard, largely non-living, mineralized coverings.

EXPLANATION: Cane sugar (sucrose) will only be hydrolyzed into its component sugars (including glucose) when a catalyst — either dilute acid (test tube II) or its specific breaking-down enzyme (test tube III) — is present to speed up the reaction; plain water alone (test tube I) is insufficient to bring about hydrolysis in a reasonable time frame.

Q: The enzyme involved in the hydrolysis is
A) sucrase
B) maltase
C) rennin
D) erepsin
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Sucrase (also called invertase) is the specific enzyme that hydrolyzes sucrose (cane sugar) into glucose and fructose, distinct from maltase (acts on maltose), rennin (curdles milk protein), or erepsin (acts on peptides).

Q: An example of a filter-feeding animal is
A) whale
B) mosquito
C) shark
D) butterfly
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Certain whales (baleen whales) are classic filter feeders, straining vast quantities of small prey (like krill) from seawater using comb-like baleen plates instead of teeth — a feeding method quite different from that of mosquitoes, sharks, or butterflies.

Q: A function of the hydrochloric acid produced in the human stomach during digestion is to
A) neutralise the effect of bile
B) coagulate milk protein and emulsify fats
C) stop the action of ptyalin
D) break up food into smaller particles
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The strongly acidic environment created by stomach hydrochloric acid halts the activity of ptyalin (salivary amylase), which requires a neutral/alkaline pH to function — hydrochloric acid's other roles include activating pepsin and killing ingested bacteria, but coagulating milk (renin's job) and emulsifying fat (bile's job) are not its functions.

Q: The process in which complex substances are broken down into simpler ones is referred to as
A) catabolism
B) metabolism
C) tropism
D) anabolism
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Catabolism is specifically the breakdown of complex substances into simpler ones (releasing energy in the process, as in digestion and respiration), the opposite of anabolism (building up simpler substances into more complex ones).

Q: The enzyme that is present in the saliva is
A) lipase
B) pepsin
C) ptyalin
D) rennin
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Ptyalin (salivary amylase) is the specific enzyme found in saliva, responsible for beginning the digestion of starch into maltose while food is still in the mouth.

Q: i2, c1, pm3, m3. The dental formula above represents that of
A) an omnivore

D) a herbivore
