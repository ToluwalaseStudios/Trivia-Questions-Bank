Q: What is the difference between the approaches of Socrates and Aristotle?
A) Aristotle felt the need for repetition to develop good habits in students; Socrates felt that students need to be constantly questioned.
B) Aristotle felt the need for rote-learning; Socrates emphasized on dialogic learning
C) There was no difference.
D) Aristotle emphasized on the importance of paying attention to human nature; Socrates emphasized upon science.
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The passage clearly contrasts Aristotle’s use of systematic teaching and repetition to form good habits with Socrates’ method of questioning listeners to draw out their own ideas. Option A captures this exact difference.

Q: Why do educationists consider philosophy a “weak and woolly” field?
A) It is not practically applicable.
B) Its theoretical concepts are easily understood.
C) It is irrelevant for education.
D) Its tenets are not educative enough.
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The passage states that many educationalists consider philosophy “a weak and woolly field, too far removed from the practical applications of the real world to be useful.” Hence it is seen as lacking practical application.

Q: What do you understand by the term “Perennialism”, in the context of the given comprehension passage?
A) It refers to something which is of ceaseless importance.
B) It refers to something which is quite unnecessary.
C) It refers to something which is abstract and theoretical.
D) It refers to something which existed in the past and no longer exists now.
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The passage defines Perennialism as teaching those things “deemed to be of everlasting importance to all people everywhere.” “Everlasting” = ceaseless importance.

Q: What were Plato’s beliefs about education?
A) He believed that only the rich have the right to acquire education.
B) He believed that education should be holistic.
C) He believed that only a select few are meant to attend schools.
D) He believed that not all pupils are talented.
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The passage states that Plato believed education should be holistic, including facts, skills, physical discipline, music and art.

Q: Why did Aquinas propose a model of education which did not lay much emphasis on facts?
A) Facts are not important.
B) Facts do not lead to holistic education.
C) Facts change with the changing times.
D) Facts are frozen in time.
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The passage explains that Perennialism (Aquinas) focuses on principles and reasoning rather than facts “which are apt to change over time.”

NOVEL – THE LEKKI HEADMASTER
(Replaced original Life Changer questions 18–25)
========================================

Q: Who is the protagonist and principal of Stardom Schools in The Lekki Headmaster?
A) Mrs Ibidun Gloss
B) Mr Adewale Adebepo (Bepo)
C) Mr Audu
D) Chief Ogba
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mr Adewale Adebepo, popularly called Bepo or “The Lekki Headmaster”, is the central character and principal of Stardom Schools.

Q: What major event opens the novel The Lekki Headmaster?
A) A student election scandal
B) Mr Bepo breaking down in tears at morning assembly
C) The MD announcing a fee increase
D) A parent attacking a teacher
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The novel opens dramatically with the usually confident principal unable to speak and weeping uncontrollably during assembly.

Q: Why was Mr Bepo under intense pressure to leave Nigeria?
A) He had been dismissed from Stardom Schools
B) His wife Seri and children were already in the UK and wanted him to join them
C) He had received a better job offer from another school
D) He wanted to start a commercial transport business
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Bepo’s wife Seri (a nurse in the UK) and their daughters had already relocated; she constantly pressured him to “japa” and reunite the family.

Q: What is the central physical setting of The Lekki Headmaster?
A) Beesway Group of Schools
B) Black Heritage Museum, Badagry
C) Stardom Schools, Lekki, Lagos
D) Murtala Muhammed Airport
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Almost the entire story unfolds at Stardom Schools located in the affluent Lekki area of Lagos.

Q: What does the term “Japa” primarily refer to in the novel?
A) A school strike action
B) The mass migration of Nigerians abroad in search of a better life
C) A traditional cultural festival
D) The school’s excursion programme
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Japa” is used throughout the novel to describe the contemporary Nigerian phenomenon of relocating abroad for better opportunities.

Q: How much was the boarding-house fee per session at Stardom before it was reduced?
A) One hundred and sixty-three thousand naira
B) Two hundred and fifty thousand naira
C) One hundred and sixty thousand naira
D) Ninety-three thousand naira
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The novel states that the boarding fee was reduced from ₦250,000 to a lower figure as part of the school’s fee-restructuring strategy.

Q: What was the common ritual at the morning assembly at Stardom on Tuesdays and Thursdays?
A) Christian and Muslim prayers
B) Words of exaltation
C) The second stanza of the national anthem
D) The principal’s address
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: On Tuesdays and Thursdays the school recited the second stanza of the national anthem as a regular assembly ritual.

Q: At the end of the novel, what decision does Mr Bepo finally take?
A) He boards the flight to the UK
B) He resigns and starts his own school immediately
C) He returns to Stardom Schools, declaring that his heart is in Nigeria
D) He accepts a transfer to another branch of the school
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: After the grand farewell, Bepo surprisingly appears at assembly and announces that he is not going abroad; his heart and mission remain at Stardom.

LEXIS AND STRUCTURE / NEAREST IN MEANING
========================================

Q: The governor asked the corrupt official to leave the public service forthwith.
A) immediately
B) willy-nilly
C) compulsorily
D) unprepared
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: “Forthwith” means at once / immediately.

Q: The meeting was cancelled because of the protest.
A) called up
B) called off
C) cut off
D) phased out
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Called off” is the standard phrasal verb meaning cancelled.

Q: The word is stressed on the penultimate syllable.
A) last
B) second
C) second to the last
D) first
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Penultimate = second to the last.

Q: He was simultaneously a director of cultural affairs and a visiting lecturer.
A) incessantly
B) consecutively
C) contemporaneously
D) alternately
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Simultaneously” means at the same time = contemporaneously.

Q: The only impediment to the project is finance.
A) disturbance
B) hindrance
C) advantage
D) difficulty
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Impediment = obstacle / hindrance.

Q: His remarks were pejorative.
A) imperative
B) complimentary
C) complementary
D) derogatory
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Pejorative means expressing contempt or disapproval = derogatory.

Q: The ostensible reason for his resignation is lack of interest in the job, but it is beyond that.
A) main
B) apparent
C) remote
D) absolute
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Ostensible = apparent / seeming (not necessarily the real reason).

Q: The sailor inadvertently killed the albatross.
A) deliberately
B) accidentally
C) carefully
D) stealthily
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Inadvertently = unintentionally / accidentally.

Q: It was a deluge of blessing.
A) rain
B) flood
C) surplus
D) bombardment
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Deluge literally means a great flood; figuratively an overwhelming quantity.

Q: The student had to perform the herculean task of digging trenches as punishment of his offence.
A) arduous
B) stimulating
C) wounding
D) provoking
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Herculean = requiring great strength or effort = arduous.

INTERPRETATION OF SENTENCES
========================================

Q: Habib: Do you mind if I sit down here? Umana: Well, yes I do actually.
A) Umana is reluctantly granting the request
B) Umana is willingly accepting the request
C) Umana is certainly rejecting the request
D) Umana is objecting to the request
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Yes I do (mind)” is a clear refusal of the request.

Q: The Governor held forth for several minutes on the need to fight corruption in the state.
A) The Governor was silent for minutes when corruption was being discussed.
B) The Governor whispered a few things about corruption.
C) The Governor spent time saying a few things about corruption.
D) The Governor gave a rather long speech on the need to fight corruption.
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: “Held forth” means spoke at length / delivered a speech.

Q: The boss is fond of throwing his weight about.
A) The boss is overweight.
B) The boss likes healthy exercise.
C) The boss gives orders to people
D) The boss is respected by his employees.
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Throwing one’s weight about” means using one’s authority in an overbearing way / giving orders.

Q: Ronaldo is the live wire of the team.
A) Ronaldo’s absence is rarely felt
B) Ronaldo is the captain of the team.
C) Ronaldo is the most energetic in the team.
D) Ronaldo’s absence is hardly felt.
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A “live wire” is an energetic, lively person who animates the group.

Q: If I were in London I would have watched the game.
A) I did not watch the game although I was in London.
B) I was in London and I did not watch the game.
C) I did not watch the game so I hope to travel to London
D) I was not in London and I did not watch the game.
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The conditional “If I were… I would have…” indicates that the speaker was neither in London nor watched the game.

OPPOSITE IN MEANING
========================================

Q: The student gave evasive answers to the questions being asked by her teacher.
A) direct
B) appropriate
C) clever
D) indirect
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Evasive = avoiding the truth; opposite is direct.

Q: The increase in transport fares deterred our school from planning an excursion this year.
A) deferred
B) encouraged
C) restricted
D) irritated
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Deterred = discouraged / prevented; opposite is encouraged.

Q: Abu is naturally taciturn.
A) friendly
B) cheerful
C) quiet
D) garrulous
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Taciturn = habitually silent; opposite is garrulous (talkative).

Q: The corpulent woman is his mother.
A) fat
B) obese
C) slender
D) ugly
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Corpulent = fat / obese; opposite is slender.

Q: He describes his wife as pusillanimous.
A) brave
B) cowardly
C) wild
D) immoral
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Pusillanimous = cowardly / lacking courage; opposite is brave.

FILL-IN-THE-GAP (Questions 46–55)
========================================

Q: Our ______ are the teenagers.
A) targeted audience
B) targeted audiences
C) target audience
D) targeted audiences
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The correct fixed expression is “target audience” (singular collective).

Q: Babajide deceived my father; Babajide is a ______.
A) cheat
B) dupe
C) cheater
D) cheetah
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The person who deceives is a cheat (or cheater); “dupe” is the victim.

Q: He is an ______ liar.
A) inverterate
B) invertebrate
C) inveterate
D) inveterate
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Inveterate” means confirmed / habitual. (Note: option D is identical; in a real exam only one correct form appears. Correct spelling is inveterate.)

Q: The teacher told the students to ______ the girl for her brilliant performance.
A) clap with
B) clap for
C) clap
D) clap to
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The standard collocation is “clap for” someone.

Q: The man teaches in the university, so he is ______.
A) an academic
B) an academician
C) an academia
D) an academics
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A university teacher is an academic. “Academician” usually refers to a member of an academy.

Q: The old man, with his wives, ______ guilty.
A) is
B) are
C) were
D) being
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The subject is “the old man” (singular); the phrase “with his wives” is parenthetical and does not change the verb.

Q: The novel ______ twenty five chapters.
A) comprises of
B) comprises
C) consists
D) consists in
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Comprises” does not take “of”. Correct: The novel comprises twenty-five chapters.

Q: He sold me a ______ radio.
A) doff
B) duff
C) dope
D) dwarf
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Duff” means worthless / of poor quality (informal British English).

Q: His eldest sister is instrumental ______ his success.
A) to
B) for
C) in
D) at
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The fixed preposition is “instrumental to”.

Q: The interpreter was wrong because he gave the congregation a ______ translation of the pastor’s statement.
A) lateral
B) literary
C) literal
D) unilateral
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A word-for-word rendering is a literal translation.

VOWEL & CONSONANT SOUNDS
========================================

Q: Which option has a different vowel sound from the others?
A) foal
B) foe
C) fowl
D) mow
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: foal /əʊ/, foe /əʊ/, mow /əʊ/; fowl /aʊ/ has a different diphthong.

Q: Which option has a different vowel sound from the others?
A) spoon
B) put
C) cook
D) good
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: spoon has /uː/; the others have /ʊ/.

Q: beige – same consonant sound as the underlined letter(s)
A) goal
B) jeep
C) genre
D) yes
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The “ge” in beige is /ʒ/ (as in genre, measure, vision).

Q: renege – same consonant sound as the underlined letter(s)
A) revenge
B) big
C) journey
D) camouflage
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The “g” in renege is /g/ (hard g), same as in revenge.

Q: house – same consonant sound as the underlined letter(s)
A) honour
B) honest
C) who
D) hauteur
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The “h” in house is pronounced /h/; who also begins with /h/. Honour, honest and hauteur have silent “h”.
