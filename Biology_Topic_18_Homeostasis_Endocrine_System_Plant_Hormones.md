# TOPIC 18: HOMEOSTASIS, ENDOCRINE SYSTEM, PLANT HORMONES
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: The hormone which tones up the muscles of a person in time of danger is from the
A) thyroid gland
B) pancreas
C) adrenal gland
D) liver
E) spleen
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The adrenal gland (specifically its medulla) releases adrenaline in response to danger or stress, preparing the body for "fight or flight" by increasing heart rate, boosting blood flow to muscles, and generally toning up the body for rapid action — earning adrenaline the nickname "emergency hormone."

Q: A gland in the human body secretes two hormones, one of which causes a disease called diabetes when there is a deficiency. Which of these organs produces the hormones?
A) Spleen
B) parathyroid
C) Adrenal body
D) Thyroid
E) Pancreas
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The pancreas produces two key hormones regulating blood sugar — insulin (whose deficiency causes diabetes mellitus) and glucagon — making it the gland described.

Q: The conversion of excess amino acids into urea occurs in the
A) kidney
B) pancreas
C) villi
D) liver
E) spleen
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Deamination (the removal of the amino group from excess amino acids) and the subsequent formation of urea both take place in the liver; the kidney's role is only to filter and excrete the already-formed urea, not to produce it.

Q: Which of the following hormones is produced during or when agitated?
A) insulin
B) adrenalin
C) thyroxine
D) Pituitrin
E) Progesterone
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established in the first question of this set, adrenaline is specifically the hormone released in response to agitation, fear, or danger, driving the body's fight-or-flight response.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a function of the liver?
A) regulation of blood sugar
B) storage of iron
C) Formation of bile
D) Breakdown of excess amino acids
E) Excretion of urea from the blood
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The liver produces urea (from breaking down excess amino acids) but does not itself excrete it from the blood — that specific filtering and excretion role belongs to the kidneys. Regulating blood sugar, storing iron, forming bile, and deaminating excess amino acids are all genuine liver functions.

Q: Which of the following organs regulates the amount of amino acids and glucose in the body?
A) Kidney
B) Liver
C) Pancreas
D) Spleen
E) Stomach
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The liver plays the central regulatory role in maintaining stable levels of both amino acids (via deamination of excess) and glucose (via glycogen storage/release) circulating in the blood.

Q: The mammalian endocrine system is responsible for
A) transmitting impulses
B) regulating body temperature
C) regulating osmotic pressure of blood
D) chemical co-ordination
E) the manufacture of blood
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The endocrine system's defining, overarching role is chemical coordination — regulating body processes through hormones released into the bloodstream — which is the broader category that specific functions like temperature or osmotic regulation actually fall under, and which distinguishes it from the nervous system's electrical impulse transmission.

Q: Deamination occurs in the
A) kidney
B) pancreas
C) spleen
D) liver
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, deamination (removal of the amino group from excess amino acids) specifically takes place in the liver.

Q: The hormone which regulates the amount of glucose in the blood is called
A) adrenalin
B) auxins
C) insulin
D) thyroxin
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Insulin (along with glucagon) is the specific hormone responsible for regulating blood glucose levels, primarily by promoting the uptake and storage of excess glucose as glycogen.

Q: A severe deficiency of thyroxin results in
A) diabetes mellitus
B) sexual underdevelopment
C) cretinism
D) gigantism
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A severe thyroxin deficiency occurring before maturity in a young, developing individual results in cretinism, a condition characterized by stunted physical growth, mental retardation, and delayed sexual development, since thyroxin (alongside growth hormone) is essential for normal development.

Q: Unlike auxins, gibberellins
A) induce the formation of adventitious roots
B) do not affect leaf and fruit abscission
C) cannot stimulate stem elongation
D) are quite effective as herbicides
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: While auxins directly influence abscission (the shedding of leaves and fruits), gibberellins do not have this same regulatory effect on abscission — gibberellins instead specialize more in promoting stem elongation, seed germination, and dormancy breaking.

Q: Fruit enlargement can be induced by spraying young ovary with
A) cytokinin, abscisic acid and ethylene
B) gibberellin, ethylene and abscisic acid
C) auxin, abscisic acid and ethylene
D) auxin, cytokinin and gibberellin
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Auxin, cytokinin, and gibberellin together are the combination of growth-promoting hormones that can be applied to a young ovary to stimulate fruit enlargement — ethylene and abscisic acid, by contrast, are more associated with ripening/senescence and growth inhibition/dormancy respectively, not enlargement.

Q: A fundamental similarity between nervous and hormonal systems is that both
A) involve chemical transmission
B) have widespread effects
C) shed chemicals into the blood stream
D) evoke rapid responses
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Although nerve impulses travel as electrical signals along nerve fibres, the actual transmission of the signal across the gap between nerve cells (synapse) fundamentally relies on chemical transmission (neurotransmitters) — this shared reliance on chemical signalling is the deep similarity connecting the nervous and hormonal (chemical) coordination systems, even though hormonal responses are typically slower and more widespread/diffuse than the rapid, localized nervous response.

Q: A dwarf plant can be stimulated to grow to normal height by the application of
A) thyroxin
B) gibberellin
C) insulin
D) kinnin
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Gibberellins are well known for their ability to promote stem elongation, and applying gibberellin to a genetically dwarf plant can stimulate it to grow to a normal height by overcoming its natural growth limitation.

Q: When it is cold, the blood vessels of the skin
A) dilate to increase the amount of blood flowing to the skin
B) constrict to reduce the amount of blood flowing to the skin
C) dilate to reduce the amount of blood flowing to the skin
D) constrict to increase the amount of blood flowing to the skin
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In cold conditions, skin blood vessels constrict (vasoconstriction), reducing blood flow near the cold body surface, which helps conserve internal body heat by minimizing heat loss to the environment.

Q: What would happen to a man whose pancreas has been surgically removed?
A) The level of blood sugar would increase
B) The glycogen content of the liver would increase
C) His blood pressure would decrease
D) His weight would increase appreciably
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Without a pancreas, the body can no longer produce insulin, which is essential for regulating and lowering blood glucose — without insulin, blood sugar levels would rise uncontrollably (a diabetic state), rather than glycogen storage increasing (which actually requires insulin).

Q: In Amoeba, osmoregulation is carried out by the
A) pseudopodium
B) food vacuole
C) contractile vacuole
D) nucleus
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: As established in earlier topics, the contractile vacuole is specifically responsible for collecting and expelling excess water from Amoeba's cytoplasm, carrying out osmoregulation.

Q: The gland directly affecting metabolic rate, growth and development is known as
A) adrenal gland
B) thyroid gland
C) mammary gland
D) parathyroid gland
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The thyroid gland, through its production of thyroxine, is the specific gland that directly regulates the body's overall metabolic rate as well as normal growth and development.

Q: The substance that is responsible for apical dominance in plants is known as
A) gibberellin
B) tannin
C) auxin
D) kinnin
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Auxin, produced at the growing shoot tip, specifically inhibits the growth of lateral (side) buds, maintaining the dominant growth of the main apical shoot — this is the defining mechanism of apical dominance.

Q: Regulation of blood sugar level takes place in the
A) pancreas
B) ileum
C) liver
D) kidney
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: While the liver stores and releases glucose, the actual regulatory hormones (insulin and glucagon) that control this process are produced by the pancreas, making it the organ most directly responsible for blood sugar regulation.

Q: Urea formation occurs in the

ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established repeatedly, urea is specifically synthesized in the liver (through deamination and the urea/ornithine cycle), not in the heart, lung, or kidney (which merely filters and excretes the already-formed urea).

Q: Which of the following is a homeostatic response in humans?
A) Withdrawing the hand from a hot object
B) The mouth getting watery when food is sighted
C) Yawning owing to tiredness
D) Shivering in a cold environment
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Shivering is specifically a homeostatic response, generating body heat through rapid, involuntary muscle contractions to help maintain a stable internal body temperature in cold conditions — the other options are reflexes or responses unrelated to maintaining internal physiological balance in this specific temperature-regulation sense.

Q: The part of the mammalian skin involved in temperature regulation is the
A) sweat gland
B) hair papilla
C) hair follicle
D) sebaceous gland
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The sweat gland plays a direct, active role in temperature regulation, producing sweat that cools the body through evaporative heat loss — the hair papilla, hair follicle, and sebaceous gland do not perform this same active thermoregulatory function.

A) tongue rolling
B) behavioral patterns

Q: Which of the following organs regulates the levels of water, salts, hydrogen ions and urea in the mammalian blood?
A) Bladder
B) colon
C) Liver
D) Kidney

EXPLANATION: The kidney is specifically responsible for regulating and maintaining appropriate blood levels of water, salts, hydrogen ions (pH), and urea, through its filtration and selective reabsorption/excretion processes.

Q: Plant growth can be artificially stimulated by the addition of
A) gibberelin
B) kinin
C) abscisic acid
D) ethylene
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Gibberellin is a growth-promoting plant hormone that can be artificially applied to stimulate plant growth (such as stem elongation), unlike abscisic acid (a growth inhibitor) or ethylene (associated with ripening/ageing rather than growth stimulation).

Q: The gland that is found just below the hypothalamus is the
A) adrenal
B) pituitary
C) thyroid
D) parathyroid
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The pituitary gland is anatomically located directly just below the hypothalamus in the brain, connected to it by a thin stalk (the infundibulum), through which the hypothalamus exerts direct control over pituitary hormone release.

Q: The most important plant hormone is
A) abscisic acid
B) auxin
C) gibberellin
D) cytokinin
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Auxin is generally regarded as the single most important and foundational plant hormone, given its central, wide-ranging role in growth, tropisms, apical dominance, and root/fruit development, historically being the first plant hormone discovered and most extensively studied.

Q: The absence of anti-diuretic hormone in humans results in
A) a drastic dehydration
B) eliminating dehydration
C) increasing dehydration
D) decreasing dehydration
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Without anti-diuretic hormone (ADH), the kidneys cannot reabsorb sufficient water back into the blood, leading to excessive urine production (diuresis) and consequently drastic, severe dehydration of the body — a condition known as diabetes insipidus.

Q: Oestrogen is a hormone that is synthesized in the
A) testis
B) anterior pituitary
C) adrenal cortex
D) ovaries
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Oestrogen is the primary female sex hormone, synthesized specifically in the ovaries (with the adrenal cortex producing only comparatively minor amounts).
