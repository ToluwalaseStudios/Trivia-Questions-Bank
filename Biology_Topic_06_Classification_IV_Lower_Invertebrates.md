# TOPIC 6: CLASSIFICATION IV — LOWER INVERTEBRATES
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: Which one of these animals is NEVER a secondary host of tapeworms?
A) cow
B) Fish
C) Pig
D) Man
E) Dog
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: In the tapeworm (Taenia) life cycle, man is always the primary (definitive) host — the one in which the adult tapeworm lives and reproduces in the intestine — never a secondary (intermediate) host. Cattle and pigs, by contrast, commonly serve as secondary hosts, harboring the larval bladderworm stage in their muscles.

Q: The two types of human tapeworm can be distinguished by the presence or absence of
A) Scolex
B) Hook
C) head
D) sucker
E) Proglottis
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Taenia solium (the pork tapeworm) has a scolex armed with hooks as well as suckers, whereas Taenia saginata (the beef tapeworm) has a scolex with suckers only and no hooks. This presence or absence of hooks is the key distinguishing feature between the two species.

Q: A tapeworm has no alimentary canal because
A) it is autotrophic
B) it does not feed
C) it has no enzymes
D) its body absorbs digested food
E) the suckers on the scolex suck blood
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A tapeworm lives permanently within the host's small intestine, bathed in food that the host has already fully digested. It simply absorbs these ready-made nutrients directly across its body surface, so it has no need for its own digestive/alimentary canal at all.

Q: Flatworms and roundworms are said to be invertebrates because
A) they are small animals
B) they can live inside vertebrates
C) some of them are unicellular
D) they have no backbones
E) they are parasitic
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: "Invertebrate" specifically means lacking a backbone (vertebral column) — flatworms (platyhelminthes) and roundworms (nematodes) fit this definition regardless of their size, parasitic lifestyle, or where they live.

Q: Which of these worms is beneficial to man?
A) Hookworm
B) Tapeworm
C) Roundworm
D) Earthworm
E) Guinea worm
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The earthworm is beneficial because it aerates and improves soil structure, adds humus by breaking down organic matter, and improves overall soil fertility and texture. The other worms listed (hookworm, tapeworm, roundworm, guinea worm) are all human/animal parasites that cause disease.

Q: Exoskeleton is NOT found in the
A) maggot
B) mosquito larva
C) earthworm
D) caterpillar
E) termite
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The earthworm belongs to the annelids, which have soft, segmented bodies without any hard external skeleton — instead they rely on a fluid-filled hydrostatic skeleton for support. Maggots, mosquito larvae, caterpillars, and termites are all insect (arthropod) forms that possess a chitinous exoskeleton, even at their larval stages.

Q: The parts used by tapeworm to fasten itself to the host's intestine are the
A) neck and suckers
B) hooks and suckers
C) rostellum and suckers
D) young proglottis and neck
E) rostellum, hooks and suckers
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: The scolex (head) of the tapeworm is armed with all three attachment structures together — a rostellum (a small protruding structure), hooks, and suckers — which combine to grip firmly onto the wall of the host's intestine.

Q: Which of these animals is radially symmetrical?
A) Squid
B) Hydra
C) Snail
D) Cockroach
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Hydra, a coelenterate, has a body plan organized around a central axis, so it can be divided into two identical halves along any longitudinal plane passing through that axis — this is radial symmetry. Squid, snail, and cockroach are all bilaterally symmetrical, with only one plane of symmetry (dividing left from right).

Q: Which of the following lacks chaetae, tentacles and antennae?
A) Snail
B) Crab
C) Millipede
D) Earthworm
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A snail possesses tentacles (for sensing) but lacks the bristle-like chaetae (found in earthworms) and the antennae (found in arthropods like crabs and millipedes) — so overall it is the odd one out lacking chaetae AND antennae among these structures.

Q: A good example of a diploblastic organism is
A) Amoeba
B) Hydra
C) Earthworm
D) Roundworm
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Hydra (a coelenterate) is built from just two cell/tissue layers — an outer ectoderm and inner endoderm — making it diploblastic. Amoeba is unicellular (not applicable), while earthworms and roundworms are triploblastic, having a third middle layer, the mesoderm.

Q: The function of the clitellum in the earthworm is to
A) aid digestion
B) prevent desiccation
C) assist locomotion
D) secrete cocoon
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The clitellum is the visibly swollen, glandular band found on an earthworm's body, and its specific function is to secrete the mucus-based cocoon in which the fertilized eggs are stored and protected during development.

Q: In tapeworm, the two structures that run through the length of the body are the
A) nerve cord and the excretory duct
B) sperm duct and the nerve cord
C) genital pore and the excretory duct
D) sperm duct and the genital pore
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A tapeworm has a longitudinal nerve cord and paired excretory (osmoregulatory) ducts that both run the entire length of its ribbon-like body, joined by a cross-connecting tube near the posterior end of each segment (proglottis).

Q: Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of snails?
A) Bilateral symmetry
B) Chitinous exoskeleton
C) Muscular foot
D) Soft unsegmented body in a mantle
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Snails secrete a hard calcareous (calcium carbonate) shell, not a chitinous exoskeleton (which is a feature of arthropods like insects). Snails do show bilateral symmetry, move using a muscular foot, and have a soft, unsegmented body enclosed by a mantle.

Q: The following organisms are hermaphrodites EXCEPT
A) snail
B) taenia (tapeworm)
C) schistosoma
D) earthworm
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Unlike snails, tapeworms, and earthworms, which are all hermaphroditic (each individual possesses both male and female reproductive organs), Schistosoma (the blood fluke) is unusual among flatworms in having separate sexes — distinct male and female individuals.

Q: Parasitic forms are NOT found among
A) platyhelminthes
B) nematodes
C) molluscs
D) annelids
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Molluscs (like snails, clams, octopuses) are essentially all free-living, with no significant parasitic representatives — unlike platyhelminthes (e.g. tapeworms, flukes), nematodes (e.g. hookworms, roundworms), and even annelids (e.g. leeches), all of which include well-known parasitic members.

Q: Which of the following sets of organisms represents the correct trend from simple to complex structural organization? (1) Mollusca (2) Platyhelminthes (3) Nematoda (4) Protozoa
A) 4,1,2,3
B) 4,3,2,1
C) 4,2,1,3
D) 4,2,3,1
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Structural complexity increases from single-celled Protozoa (4, simplest) to the simple flatworm body plan of Platyhelminthes (2), then to the slightly more complex tube-within-a-tube body plan of Nematoda (3), and finally to the more elaborately organized body of Mollusca (1, most complex among these) — giving the order 4, 2, 3, 1.

Q: A characteristic of the phylum coelenterate is that
A) most of them are marine
B) they possess a gut with a single opening
C) they possess numerous pores on their body
D) they are bilaterally symmetrical
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Coelenterates (e.g. Hydra, jellyfish, coral) have a simple body plan with a single gut opening (called the enteron) that serves as both mouth and anus, unlike more advanced animals with a separate mouth and anus. (Numerous body pores is a feature of sponges, not coelenterates, and coelenterates are radially, not bilaterally, symmetrical.)

Q: Which of the following phyla have members with both internal and external segmentation?
A) Platyhelminthes
B) Nematoda
C) Algae
D) Mollusca
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In tapeworms (Platyhelminthes), the external segmentation of the body into proglottides is matched by corresponding internal segmentation (each proglottis containing its own reproductive organs), representing both internal and external division — a feature not shared in the same way by nematodes, algae, or molluscs.

Q: The body of a snail is divided into
A) head, thorax and abdomen
B) visceral mass and abdomen
C) thorax and foot
D) visceral mass and foot
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: A snail's body is fundamentally organized into two main parts: the visceral mass (containing the internal organs, housed within the coiled shell) and the muscular foot (used for locomotion) — a body plan quite different from the head-thorax-abdomen arrangement seen in insects.

Q: Hydra removes undigested food by
A) passing it through the anus
B) passing it through the mouth
C) means of a contractile vacuole
D) egesting it through the body surface
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Since Hydra has only a single opening (the mouth, which also serves as the "enteron" opening) leading into its gut cavity, undigested food waste must be expelled back out through this same mouth opening, as there is no separate anus.

Q: Which of the following groups of invertebrates reproduces by budding?
A) Arthropoda
B) Annelida
C) Mollusca
D) Coelenterata
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Coelenterates such as Hydra commonly reproduce asexually by budding — a small outgrowth forms on the body wall, develops into a miniature version of the parent, and eventually detaches to live independently — a method not typical of arthropods, annelids, or molluscs.

Q: In the tapeworm, the suckers on the scolex are used for
A) sucking the blood of the host
B) holding fast to the host
C) reproduction
D) locomotion
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The suckers on a tapeworm's scolex (head) function purely as attachment organs, gripping firmly onto the wall of the host's intestine to prevent the worm from being swept away by the flow of digested food — tapeworms do not feed on blood at all, since they absorb pre-digested nutrients through their body surface.

Q: Sting cells are normally found in
A) Flatworms
B) Hydra
C) Snails
D) Paramecium
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Stinging cells (cnidoblasts, containing nematocysts) are a defining feature of coelenterates such as Hydra, used for both capturing prey and for defence — they are not found in flatworms, snails, or Paramecium.

Q: In the earthworm, the cocoon is secreted by the
A) chaeta
B) prostomium
C) peristomium
D) clitellum
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As noted earlier, the clitellum (the visibly swollen glandular band on the earthworm's body) is specifically responsible for secreting the mucus cocoon used to protect the fertilized eggs.

Q: The respiratory organ in the land snail is the
A) radula
B) mantle
C) tentacle
D) foot
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In land snails, the mantle (specifically a well-vascularized region of it, forming a simple "lung") is the organ responsible for gaseous exchange with the air. The radula is a feeding structure, and the tentacles and foot are sensory/locomotory structures, unrelated to respiration.

Q: A major difference between platyhelminthes and coelenterates is that platyhelminthes
A) are multicellular
B) have developed a mesoderm
C) reproduce sexually
D) reproduce asexually
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Platyhelminthes (flatworms) are triploblastic, possessing a third middle tissue layer, the mesoderm, in addition to ectoderm and endoderm — unlike coelenterates, which remain diploblastic (only ectoderm and endoderm). Both groups are multicellular, and both can reproduce sexually or asexually depending on species.

Q: The essential structural difference between Hydra and tapeworm is that while Hydra
A) has tentacles, tapeworm is parasitic
B) is diploblastic, tapeworm is triploblastic
C) has a mouth, tapeworm feeds by suckers
D) has mesoderm, tapeworm has mesogloea
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This distinction directly parallels the previous question: Hydra is a coelenterate with only two tissue layers (diploblastic), while the tapeworm, a flatworm, has three tissue layers including a mesoderm (triploblastic) — this is the fundamental structural difference between them.

Q: The soil swallowed by the earthworm to form the worm cast is ground up in the
A) clitellum
B) prostomium
C) mouth
D) gizzard
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: As the earthworm swallows soil (along with organic matter) while burrowing, this material passes into the muscular gizzard, where it is ground up against small stones the worm has also ingested, before being expelled as the familiar worm cast.

Q: The cnidoblast cells found in Hydra are used for
A) reproduction
B) offence and defence
C) locomotion and nutrition
D) food collection
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Cnidoblasts (nematoblasts) are specialized stinging cells that fire out thread-like, sometimes venomous structures used both to capture prey (offence) and to fend off potential predators (defence).

Q: Annelids differ from nematodes in that they
A) exhibit bilateral symmetry
B) are triploblastic
C) are metamerically segmented
D) possess complete digestive system
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Annelids (like earthworms) are distinguished by true metameric segmentation — their bodies are divided into a series of repeating, similar segments, each often with its own set of internal structures — a feature nematodes (roundworms) lack, even though both groups are bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, and have complete digestive systems.

Q: In snails, the hard calcareous shells are secreted by the
A) radula
B) ctenidium
C) pneumostome
D) mantle
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The mantle, a specialized layer of tissue that covers the snail's visceral mass, is responsible for secreting the layers of calcium carbonate that build up the snail's hard external shell.

Q: The habitat of the Cysticercus of Taenia solium is
A) alimentary canal of cattle
B) muscles of pig
C) alimentary canal of pig
D) muscles of cattle
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Taenia solium is specifically the pork tapeworm, so its larval bladderworm (Cysticercus) stage develops and resides in the muscle tissue of the pig, its intermediate host — not in cattle, which are instead the intermediate host for the beef tapeworm, Taenia saginata.

Q: The division of a body into two equal halves along a longitudinal plane is called
A) bilateral symmetry
B) transverse section
C) radial symmetry
D) longitudinal section
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: When an animal's body can only be divided into two identical (mirror-image) halves along one specific plane running from front to back, this is the definition of bilateral symmetry.

Q: Coelom is absent in the class of animals termed
A) Mollusca
B) Reptilia
C) Arthropoda
D) Coelenterata
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Coelenterates are diploblastic, with no mesoderm layer at all, so they cannot have a true coelom (a mesoderm-lined body cavity) — molluscs, reptiles, and arthropods, all being more complex triploblastic animals, do possess (or evolved from ancestors with) a coelom.

Q: An example of a radially symmetrical organism is
A) Planaria
B) Hydra
C) Tapeworm
D) Roundworm
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, Hydra's body is organized around a central axis, allowing it to be divided into identical halves along any of several longitudinal planes — the defining feature of radial symmetry. Planaria, tapeworm, and roundworm are all bilaterally symmetrical.

Q: The leech and the earthworm belong to the
A) molluscs
B) crustaceans
C) arachnids
D) annelids
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Both leeches and earthworms are segmented worms belonging to the phylum Annelida, sharing features such as a soft, ringed (segmented) body.

Q: One primitive feature of the coelenterates is the possession of
A) a dorsal mouth
B) radial symmetry
C) bilateral symmetry
D) a false foot
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Radial symmetry (a body organized around a central axis rather than a left-right mirror plan) is considered an evolutionarily simpler, more primitive body arrangement, characteristic of the coelenterates, compared to the more advanced bilateral symmetry seen in higher animal groups.

B) Earthworm
C) Snail
D) Cockroach
ANSWER: D

C) Asymmetrical
D) Symmetry
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: This is precisely the definition of radial symmetry — the body can be divided into matching halves through multiple different planes passing through its central axis, unlike bilateral symmetry, where only one specific plane produces mirror-image halves.
