Q: Which of the following is not true about the perception of the black men in the passage?
A) They are more sexually active than their European counterparts
B) Their sexual organs are not fully developed as their European counterparts
C) They are oversexed and sexually virile than their European counterparts
D) They are sexually animalistic and more physically grown than their European counterparts
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The passage portrays black men as oversexed, animalistic and more bodily than Europeans. It does not say their sexual organs are underdeveloped; rather it refers to the opposite stereotype of being over-endowed. Therefore B is not true according to the passage.

Q: According to the author, the perception of the black men and women in the passage is
A) mythical but not racist
B) stereotypical but not racist
C) racist but not stereotypical
D) racist and stereotypical
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The passage repeatedly links the negative images of black men and women to both racist ideology and long-standing sexual stereotypes. Hence the perception is both racist and stereotypical.

Q: The two words that can replace “bodily” and “rape” as used in the first paragraph are
A) physical and invade
B) fleshly and frustrate
C) virile and violate
D) hefty and trespass
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In the context, “more bodily” means more physical/animal, and “rape” is described as the invasion of personal space. “Physical and invade” best captures the intended meanings.

Q: The black women, according to the passage, can never truly be raped because
A) Their awareness of their right to sexual modesty is low
B) They hardly prosecute when such act is performed on them
C) Their sexual organs are more highly developed

ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The passage states that black women are stereotyped as amoral Jezebels who lack a personal space of modesty and reserve; therefore, within that racist ideology, they “can never truly be raped.” Option A is the closest match to this reasoning.

Q: Corresponding to the popular sexual myth that black men are genitally over-endowed are notions that

ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The passage explicitly draws a parallel: the myth of black male genital over-endowment corresponds to the notion that African women’s sexual organs are more highly developed (and differently configured) than those of European women.
