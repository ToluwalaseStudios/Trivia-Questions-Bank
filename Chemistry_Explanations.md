# CHEMISTRY: Explanations (40 questions)

Format: **Answer** | your pick (✔ correct, ✘ wrong) | explanation.

**Q1. Why transition elements form complex ions**
**Answer: A, Have vacant orbitals** | You picked D ✘
Transition metals have vacant, low-energy d-orbitals (and often s and p orbitals close in energy). These accept lone pairs of electrons from ligands (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻) to form dative (coordinate) bonds, giving complex ions such as [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺. Their small size and high charge density also help.
Why not D: ligands are the species that attach to the metal; the metal is not the ligand. Being paramagnetic (B) or being metals (C) does not explain complex formation.

**Q2. Removing silicon impurities in the extraction of aluminium from bauxite**
**Answer: A, Filtration** | ✔
In the Bayer process, bauxite is digested in hot concentrated NaOH. Al₂O₃ dissolves as sodium aluminate, while Fe₂O₃, TiO₂ and the silicon impurities (as insoluble sodium aluminosilicate) stay solid. They are removed by filtration as "red mud". Al(OH)₃ is then precipitated from the filtrate and heated to give pure Al₂O₃.

**Q3. Aqueous NaHCO₃**
**Answer: B, Alkaline** | ✔
NaHCO₃ is the salt of a strong base (NaOH) and a weak acid (H₂CO₃). The HCO₃⁻ ion hydrolyses: HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ + OH⁻. The excess OH⁻ makes the solution weakly alkaline (pH about 8.3). HCO₃⁻ is amphiprotic, but the solution itself is alkaline.

**Q4. Lone pairs in HF**
**Answer: D, 3** | You picked C ✘
Fluorine has 7 valence electrons (2s² 2p⁵). One electron is shared with hydrogen in the H–F bond, so 6 electrons remain as 3 lone pairs. Hydrogen has no lone pair.

**Q5. White precipitate with chloride, soluble on boiling**
**Answer: D, Pb²⁺** | ✔
Pb²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → PbCl₂(s), a white precipitate that dissolves in hot water and reappears on cooling. Chlorides of Al³⁺, Zn²⁺ and Cu²⁺ are soluble and give no precipitate.

**Q6. pH from 3 to 5**
**Answer: B, decreases by 100 times** | ✔
[H⁺] = 10⁻ᵖᴴ. At pH 3, [H⁺] = 10⁻³; at pH 5, [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵. 10⁻³ ÷ 10⁻⁵ = 100, so the concentration falls 100-fold. Each pH unit is a tenfold change.

**Q7. Oxide unchanged when heated in hydrogen**
**Answer: B, ZnO** | You picked D ✘
Hydrogen can remove oxygen only from oxides of the less reactive metals such as copper, lead and iron: CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O; PbO + H₂ → Pb + H₂O; Fe₂O₃ + 3H₂ → 2Fe + 3H₂O. Zinc is more reactive and holds oxygen too strongly for hydrogen to reduce ZnO, so it stays unchanged (it needs carbon at high temperature).

**Q8. Oxidation number of N in HNO₂**
**Answer: A, +3** | ✔
H = +1, each O = −2 (total −4). The compound is neutral: (+1) + N + (−4) = 0, so N = +3.

**Q9. Bond between molecules of ethanoic acid**
**Answer: B, Hydrogen bond** | You picked D ✘
The question asks about bonds between molecules (intermolecular). The O–H of one CH₃COOH molecule bonds to the C=O oxygen of another, forming hydrogen-bonded dimers. Covalent bonds hold atoms inside each molecule, not between molecules. Ionic bonds do not exist in a covalent acid.

**Q10. Electrolysis of CuSO₄ with copper electrodes (10 A, 1 hr)**
**Answer: D, III only** | You picked C ✘
Charge = 10 × 3600 = 36,000 C. Faradays = 36,000 ÷ 96,500 = 0.373 F. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, so moles of Cu = 0.373 ÷ 2 = 0.186 mol. The number in I is right, but the electrode is wrong.
- Cu dissolves from the **anode** (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻), so I is false.
- Cu deposits at the **cathode**, so II is false.
- The Cu that dissolves at the anode equals the Cu deposited at the cathode, so [Cu²⁺] stays the same. III is true and IV is false.

**Q11. % by volume of H₂S in air**
**Answer: A, 4.70** | ✔
PbS = 207 + 32 = 239 g/mol. Moles of PbS = 5.02 ÷ 239 = 0.0210 mol. The equation is 1:1, so H₂S = 0.0210 mol. Volume at s.t.p. = 0.0210 × 22.4 = 0.470 dm³. % = (0.470 ÷ 10.0) × 100 = **4.70%**.

**Q12. Period of element 38**
**Answer: D, 5** | You picked C ✘
Configuration: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p⁶ 5s² (2,8,18,8,2). The outermost shell is n = 5, so it is in period 5 (this is strontium, Group 2).

**Q13. Diamond in abrasives**
**Answer: C, Hardness** | ✔
Each carbon is bonded to four others by strong covalent bonds in a rigid giant 3-D lattice, making diamond the hardest natural substance. That hardness is what lets it cut and grind other materials.

**Q14. Gunpowder constituents**
**Answer: C, Potassium trioxonitrate (V)** | ✔
Gunpowder = sulphur + charcoal + potassium nitrate (KNO₃, potassium trioxonitrate(V)). KNO₃ is the oxidiser that supplies oxygen for rapid burning.

**Q15. Crude oil fraction at 60–100 °C**
**Answer: A, Ligroin** | You picked C ✘
Fractional distillation separates crude oil by boiling point. Petroleum gases come off at the lowest temperatures (below about 40 °C). Petroleum ether (the "ether" option) comes off below 60 °C. The 60–100 °C cut is **ligroin** (light naphtha). Petrol is listed as a separate, higher-boiling cut.

**Q16. Industrial conversion of ammonia to nitric acid**
**Answer: B, Ostwald process** | You picked D ✘
Ostwald process: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt/Rh catalyst, about 800 °C); 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂; 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO. The Haber process makes ammonia from N₂ and H₂; the Contact process makes H₂SO₄; the Sabatier process is catalytic hydrogenation.

**Q17. Relative rate of diffusion (ρA = 0.25, ρB = 1.0)**
**Answer: B, 2:1** | You picked A ✘
Graham's law: rate ∝ 1/√density. R_A/R_B = √(ρB/ρA) = √(1.0/0.25) = √4 = 2. So A diffuses twice as fast: **2:1**. The lighter gas diffuses faster, so the ratio cannot be 1:2.

**Q18. What decreases as steam changes to water**
**Answer: D, Entropy of the system** | You picked C ✘
Steam → water is condensation. Gas molecules are very disordered; liquid molecules are far more ordered, so entropy decreases.
- Number of molecules: unchanged (same substance).
- Temperature: stays at 100 °C during the change of state.
- Activation energy: not a property that changes with a change of state.

**Q19. Enthalpy change from ΔG and ΔS**
**Answer: D, +2,081 J/mol** | You picked B ✘
ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, so ΔH = ΔG + TΔS. T = 25 + 273 = 298 K. ΔH = −899 + (298 × 10) = −899 + 2,980 = **+2,081 J/mol**. Remember to use kelvin and to add TΔS.

**Q20. Indicator for NaOH + HCOOH**
**Answer: C, Phenolphthalein** | ✔
This is a strong base with a weak acid. At the end point the salt HCOONa hydrolyses and gives an alkaline solution (pH above 7). Phenolphthalein changes colour in the pH range 8.3–10, which matches. Methyl orange, methyl red and bromocresol change in acidic ranges, so they would change too early.

**Q21. Millikan's contribution**
**Answer: A, charge on electron** | ✔
Millikan's oil-drop experiment gave the charge on the electron (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C). Thomson found the charge-to-mass ratio and worked with cathode rays; Goldstein discovered positive (canal) rays.

**Q22. Chlorine with metals, the exception**
**Answer: A, Cl₂ + Fe → FeCl₂** | ✔
Chlorine is a strong oxidising agent and oxidises iron to Fe³⁺: 3Cl₂ + 2Fe → 2FeCl₃. FeCl₂ is not formed by direct combination with chlorine, so equation A is the exception. The others are correct: Al → AlCl₃, Sn → SnCl₄ (excess Cl₂), Na → NaCl.

**Q23. Hydrogen bonding (I NH₃, II HF, III H₂O, IV CH₄)**
**Answer: B, I, II, III** | ✔
The wording means: which ones do have hydrogen bonds. Hydrogen bonding needs H bonded to a small, highly electronegative atom (N, O, F) with a lone pair. NH₃, HF and H₂O qualify. CH₄ does not: C is not electronegative enough and the molecule has no lone pair.

**Q24. Curing cancerous growths**
**Answer: A, γ-rays** | ✔
Gamma rays are highly penetrating and can be focused (for example from cobalt-60) to destroy cancer cells in radiotherapy. Alpha rays barely penetrate; beta rays penetrate only shallowly; infrared is just heat.

**Q25. Potassium flame**
**Answer: B, violet (lilac) flame** | ✔
Flame tests: sodium golden-yellow, potassium violet/lilac, copper blue-green, calcium brick-red.

**Q26. Mass increases when heated in air**
**Answer: C, Magnesium** | ✔
2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Magnesium burns vigorously in air and combines with oxygen, so the white ash weighs more than the original ribbon. Helium is inert. Copper and tin only oxidise slowly or on the surface under ordinary heating, so magnesium is the expected answer.

**Q27. Atomic mass of X in XO₂ (V.D. = 32)**
**Answer: A, 32** | ✔
Molar mass = 2 × V.D. = 64. XO₂ = X + 2(16) = 64, so X = 64 − 32 = **32** (sulphur, SO₂).

**Q28. Ethene + O₂ over silver at 250 °C**
**Answer: C, Epoxyethane** | ✔
2C₂H₄ + O₂ → 2C₂H₄O (ethylene oxide). The volume ratio 20 : 10 = 2 : 1 matches the equation. Ag as catalyst gives epoxyethane.

**Q29. Unpaired electrons in X⁺ (X = 16)**
**Answer: B, 3** | You picked C ✘
Element 16 is sulphur: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴. For X⁺, remove one electron from the 3p subshell: 3p³. By Hund's rule, three p electrons occupy three separate orbitals, giving **3 unpaired electrons**. (Neutral S has 2.)

**Q30. Why Mg has a smaller radius than Ca**
**Answer: D, Increases down the group** | You picked A ✘
Mg and Ca are both in Group 2, with Ca below Mg. Going down a group, each element has an extra electron shell, so atomic radius increases. Ca is therefore larger than Mg. Option A is a true trend, but across a period, and Mg (period 3) and Ca (period 4) are not in the same period.

**Q31. Ethyne passed over CuSO₄**
**Answer: C, Phosphine** | ✔
Ethyne from calcium carbide contains impurities such as PH₃ (and H₂S) from phosphide and sulphide impurities in the carbide. Copper(II) sulphate solution absorbs them, forming copper phosphide/sulphide precipitates, leaving purer ethyne.

**Q32. Mild antiseptic**
**Answer: C, Boric acid** | You picked D ✘
Dilute boric acid is used as an eye wash, mouthwash and antiseptic powder. Citric and tartaric acids are food acids (tartaric acid is also in baking powder); acetic acid (vinegar) is a food preservative and flavouring.

**Q33. Oxygen liberated in a coulometer in series with silver**
**Answer: D, 0.56 dm³** | ✔
Moles of Ag = 10.8 ÷ 108 = 0.1 mol. Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag, so 0.1 mol Ag needs 0.1 F. The same charge passes through the other cell. At the anode: 4OH⁻ → 2H₂O + O₂ + 4e⁻, so 1 mol O₂ needs 4 F. Moles of O₂ = 0.1 ÷ 4 = 0.025 mol. Volume = 0.025 × 22.4 = **0.56 dm³**.

**Q34. Value of y in Na₂CO₃·yH₂O**
**Answer: C, 5** | You picked B ✘
Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂.
Moles HCl = 0.097 × 32.10 ÷ 1000 = 0.003114 mol.
Moles Na₂CO₃ in 25 cm³ = 0.003114 ÷ 2 = 0.001557 mol.
In 500 cm³: 0.001557 × (500 ÷ 25) = 0.03114 mol.
Molar mass of the crystals = 6.02 ÷ 0.03114 = 193.3 g/mol.
Na₂CO₃ = 2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106. So 18y = 193.3 − 106 = 87.3, giving y = 4.85, which rounds to **5**.
(The 1:2 mole ratio of Na₂CO₃ to HCl is the step most often missed.)

**Q35. Straight unbranched carbohydrate polymer**
**Answer: C, Cellulose** | You picked D ✘
Cellulose is a long, unbranched chain of β-glucose units joined by β-1,4 links. Glycogen is highly branched. Starch is a mixture of amylose (unbranched) and amylopectin (branched), so starch as a whole is not described as unbranched. "All of the above" is therefore wrong.

**Q36. Principle of paper chromatography**
**Answer: C, Solubility** | ✔
Components of a mixture dissolve to different extents in the moving solvent and are held differently by the paper (stationary phase). The more soluble ones travel further with the solvent, which separates the mixture.

**Q37. Advantage of soaps over detergents**
**Answer: B, They don't cause water pollution** | You picked D ✘
Soaps are made from natural fats and oils and are biodegradable, so they do not persist and foam in rivers. Many detergents have non-biodegradable branched chains that cause pollution. The other options favour detergents: they work in hard and acidic water, have wider use, and clean better in hard water, where soap forms scum. Soap does not work well in acidic water.

**Q38. Making HCOOCH₂CH₃**
**Answer: B, Ethanol and methanoic acid** | ✔
The ester is named alkyl (from the alcohol) + alkanoate (from the acid). HCOO– comes from methanoic acid; CH₂CH₃ (ethyl) comes from ethanol. HCOOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ HCOOC₂H₅ + H₂O, so the ester is ethyl methanoate.

**Q39. Polymer for car rear lights and contact lenses**
**Answer: B, Perspex** | ✔
Perspex (polymethyl methacrylate, acrylic) is transparent, strong and light, and is used for rear light covers, contact lenses and aircraft windows.

**Q40. Mass of bromine from 10.35 g Pb (molten PbBr₂)**
**Answer: D, 8.00 g** | ✔
Moles of Pb = 10.35 ÷ 207 = 0.05 mol. Cathode: Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb. Anode: 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻. Both involve 2 electrons, so moles of Br₂ = 0.05 mol. Mass = 0.05 × 160 = **8.00 g**.
