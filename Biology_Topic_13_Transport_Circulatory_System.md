Q: A carbohydrate molecule in the right ventricle of the heart is pumped into the cell of the toe of man. Which of these structures is it unlikely to pass through en-route?
A) Pulmonary artery
B) Lungs
C) Heart
D) Liver
E) Aorta
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Blood leaving the right ventricle follows the route: pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → femoral artery → toe. The liver is not on this direct pulmonary-then-systemic circulation pathway to the toe, making it the one structure the blood would not need to pass through.

Q: The hepatic portal vein is characterized by
A) Beginning and ending with capillaries
B) connecting the liver and kidney
C) carrying deoxygenated blood
D) ending with large capillaries
E) having no capillaries
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: A "portal" vein, by definition, both begins and ends in a capillary network — the hepatic portal vein starts as capillaries in the intestinal wall (absorbing digested food) and ends as another capillary network within the liver itself, an unusual double-capillary arrangement.

Q: The blood vessel which carried digested food from the small intestine to the liver is the

E) coeliac artery
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The hepatic portal vein specifically carries blood rich in digested food (absorbed from the small intestine) directly to the liver for processing, distinct from the hepatic artery, which instead supplies the liver with oxygenated blood.

Q: Which of the following statements is NOT true of Mammalian erythrocytes?

B) They appear yellow when looked at singly

B) They are involved in blood clotting
C) They are respiratory pigments
D) They are most numerous and ramify all cells
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Leucocytes (white blood cells) are indeed generally larger than erythrocytes and retain their nucleus — they are not involved in blood clotting (platelets' role), are not respiratory pigments (that's haemoglobin, found in erythrocytes), and are actually far less numerous than erythrocytes.

Q: In mammals, the exchange of nutrients and metabolic products occurs in the
A) trachea
B) lymph
C) lungs
D) oesophagus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, the exchange of nutrients and metabolic waste products between blood and body cells occurs at the level of the lymph/interstitial fluid, not within the trachea, lungs, or oesophagus.

Q: The vascular tissues in higher plants are responsible for
A) transpiration pull
B) the transport of gases and water
C) the movement of food and water
D) suction pressure
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The vascular tissues (xylem and phloem together) are collectively responsible for moving both water/minerals (via xylem) and manufactured food (via phloem) throughout the plant — transpiration pull and suction pressure are physical forces/processes involved in xylem transport specifically, not the overall function of the vascular tissue as a whole.

Q: Movement of minerals and chemical compounds within a plant occurs during
A) translocation
B) transpiration
C) diffusion
D) osmosis
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Translocation is the specific term for the general movement of manufactured food, minerals, and other chemical compounds through a plant's vascular tissue, distinct from transpiration (water loss from leaves), diffusion, or osmosis (movement of water specifically).

Q: Which blood cells are involved in the immune response of vertebrates?
A) Lymphocytes
B) Erythrocytes
C) Monocytes
D) Phagocytes
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Lymphocytes are the specific white blood cells responsible for producing targeted antibodies as part of the vertebrate immune response against specific antigens/pathogens.

Q: The blood circulatory system of vertebrates consists of
A) heart, aorta, capillaries and veins
B) heart, aorta, arteries, veins
C) heart, vena cava, arteries and veins
D) heart, arteries, capillaries and veins
ANSWER: D

Q: Which of the following forms about 55% of the volume of the blood in a man?
A) Platelets
B) Plasma
C) Erythrocytes
D) Leucocytes
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Blood plasma (the liquid component of blood) typically makes up about 55% of total blood volume, with the remaining approximately 45% consisting of the various blood cells (predominantly erythrocytes).

Q: A circulatory system is very essential in mammals but not in smaller organisms like Amoeba because
A) Amoeba lives in freshwater
B) diffusion is sufficient to transport materials in Amoeba
C) Amoeba lacks blood, containing haemoglobin
D) Amoeba exhibits anaerobic respiration
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Being single-celled and very small, Amoeba has a large surface-area-to-volume ratio, meaning simple diffusion alone is entirely sufficient to transport nutrients, gases, and wastes throughout its body, without any need for a dedicated circulatory system as larger, multicellular mammals require.

Q: In vascular plants, the sieve tubes and companion cells are present in the
A) cambium
B) cortex
C) xylem
D) Phloem
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Sieve tubes and their associated companion cells are both defining structural components specifically found within phloem tissue, not xylem, cortex, or cambium.
