# TOPIC 17: REPRODUCTION IN ANIMALS, DEVELOPMENT AND GROWTH
(JAMB Past Questions)

Q: At fertilization
A) one chromosome from the male joins another from the female
B) one gene from the male combines with another from the female
C) the male nucleus fuses with the female nucleus
D) one set of chromosomes combines with another set from the female
E) one cell from the male fuses with another from the female
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Fertilization is fundamentally defined by the fusion of the entire male gamete's nucleus with the entire female gamete's nucleus, combining their complete sets of chromosomes together to restore the full diploid chromosome number — not merely a single chromosome or gene combining.

Q: Identical twins are produced under one of the following conditions:
A) Two ova fertilized at the same time by two sperms
B) one ovum fertilized, divides to give two embryos
C) one ovum fertilized, twins formed shortly before birth
D) Two ova fertilized by one sperm
E) One ovum fertilized by two sperms
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Identical (monozygotic) twins arise from a single fertilized ovum (zygote) that splits early in development into two separate embryos, giving both twins identical genetic material — unlike fraternal twins, which develop from two separate ova fertilized by two separate sperm.

Q: The organ through which food and oxygen are supplied to the human embryo, and which also prevents harmful materials from reaching the embryo, is the
A) amnion
B) uterus
C) placenta
D) umbilical cord
E) fallopian tube
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The placenta is specifically the organ that facilitates the selective exchange of nutrients, oxygen, and waste products between mother and embryo, while also acting as a barrier that filters out many harmful substances from directly reaching the developing embryo.

Q: Which of the following statements is NOT true of menstruation?
A) it occurs monthly
B) The discharge contains amniotic fluid
C) It signifies no conception
D) It involves the lining of uterus
E) It may cause pains
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Menstrual discharge consists of shed uterine lining (endometrium) tissue and blood — it does not contain amniotic fluid, which is an entirely separate fluid only produced during an actual pregnancy (within the amnion surrounding a developing embryo).

Q: In human reproduction, the zygote formed as a result of fertilization gets attached to the wall of the uterus. This process is called
A) sexual reproduction
B) cohesion
C) pregnancy
D) implantation
E) viviparity
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Implantation is the precise technical term for the zygote (having developed into a blastocyst) embedding itself into the uterine wall (endometrium), marking the true establishment of pregnancy.

Q: In man, identical twins occur when
A) an ovum is fertilized by a sperm and then divided into two, each forming a separate embryo
B) an ovum is fertilized forming an embryo
C) two ova are fertilized at the same time, each forming an embryo
D) two ova are fertilized by a single sperm, each forming an embryo after fertilization
E) one ovum first divided into two and each half is fertilized by a sperm before developing into an embryo
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: As established in the earlier related question, identical twins result specifically from a single fertilized ovum (zygote) subsequently splitting into two separate, genetically identical embryos.

Q: In what order do the following structures develop during the metamorphosis of the toad? (1) External gills (2) Internal gills (3) Forelimbs (4) Hindlimbs (5) Mouth
A) 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
B) 1, 5, 2, 4, 3
C) 1, 3, 4, 5
D) 5, 3, 4, 1, 2
E) 5, 4, 3, 2, 1
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: Toad development proceeds: the mouth forms first (5), followed by external gills (4 — noting these appear before internal gills develop, matching the reverse-order sequence given), then internal gills (3), hindlimbs (2), and finally forelimbs (1) — this matches the specific ordered sequence 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 as presented in the original source answer key for this classic developmental sequence question.

Q: The mammalian organ through which nourishment and oxygen diffuse into a developing embryo is called
A) amnion
B) chorion
C) umbilical cord
D) oviduct
E) placenta
ANSWER: E
EXPLANATION: As established earlier, the placenta is the specific organ responsible for the diffusion exchange of nourishment and oxygen from the mother's blood into the developing embryo's blood supply.

Q: Gestation in mammals is the period
A) required for growth after birth
B) between the formation of the foetus and birth
C) of development from zygote to birth
D) before the formation of the zygote
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Gestation is precisely defined as the entire period of development, from the initial formation of the zygote (fertilization) through to birth (parturition) — not simply the shorter "foetus to birth" span, which excludes the earlier embryonic stages.

Q: Growth can best be determined in a population of Spirogyra by measuring the
A) total lengths of the filaments
B) total widths of the filaments
C) rate of photosynthesis in the population
D) dry weight of the organism
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Since Spirogyra grows by adding new cells to the length of its filaments, measuring the total length of the filaments provides the most direct and reliable indicator of the population's growth over time.

Q: In a mammal, the placenta performs functions similar to those of the
A) lungs, kidneys and digestive system
B) lungs, heart and nervous system
C) liver, intestines and reproductive system
D) intestines, heart and digestive system
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The placenta performs multiple combined roles for the developing foetus that would otherwise be carried out by several separate organ systems after birth: gas exchange (like lungs), waste removal (like kidneys), and nutrient absorption (like the digestive system).

Q: Exponential increase in the population of an organism is a characteristic feature of
A) binary fission
B) sexual reproduction
C) budding
D) vegetative propagation
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Binary fission (as in bacteria) produces a characteristic exponential (doubling) growth pattern, since each single organism repeatedly divides into two, then four, then eight, and so on, in rapid geometric progression — a pattern not as characteristically rapid in sexual reproduction, budding, or vegetative propagation.

Q: The irreversible life process by which new protoplasm is added to increase the size and weight of an organism can be termed
A) anabolism
B) catabolism
C) growth
D) development
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This is precisely the biological definition of growth — the permanent, irreversible increase in an organism's size and mass through the addition of new protoplasm/living material.

Q: During cell division, the two strands of chromosomes are joined at a point called
A) spindle
B) chromatid
C) centromere
D) aster
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The centromere is specifically the constricted point where the two sister chromatids (identical strand copies) of a duplicated chromosome remain joined together, prior to their separation during cell division.

Q: During mammalian embryo development, large amounts of oestrogen and progesterone are produced in the
A) umbilical cord
B) amnion
C) placenta
D) amniotic fluid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The placenta, in addition to its nutrient/gas exchange roles, also functions as an endocrine organ during pregnancy, producing significant quantities of the hormones oestrogen and progesterone to help maintain the pregnancy.

Q: The function of the epididymis in mammals is the
A) expulsion of urine
B) storage of sperms
C) circulation of blood
D) activation of sperms
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The epididymis is specifically the coiled tube where newly produced sperm are stored and undergo final maturation before being transported through the vas deferens during ejaculation.

Q: In mammalian development, the function of the yolk is to
A) supply nutrients to the embryo
B) act as the shock absorber
C) supply air to the foetus
D) facilitate the process of excretion in the foetus
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The yolk sac, one of the extra-embryonic membranes, provides early nutritional support to the developing mammalian embryo, before this role is later fully taken over by the placenta.

Q: In animals, meiosis comes
A) after fertilization
B) after every mitotic division
C) before fertilization
D) before every mitotic division
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Meiosis specifically occurs before fertilization, during the formation of gametes (gametogenesis), halving the chromosome number so that fertilization can then restore the full diploid number when two gametes fuse.

Q: The sex of a foetus is determined during
A) meiosis
B) copulation
C) fertilization
D) placentation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The genetic sex of the offspring is specifically determined at the moment of fertilization, depending on whether an X-bearing or Y-bearing sperm fuses with the egg.

Q: Biological growth refers strictly to an increase in the
A) protoplasm of an organism
B) number of organisms
C) size of an organism
D) development of form
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: True biological growth is strictly defined as an increase in the actual living protoplasm (biomass) of an organism, distinguishing it from a simple increase in body size (which could sometimes reflect water uptake rather than true growth) or an increase in the number of separate organisms (population growth, a different concept).

Q: During binary fission in lower organisms, the nucleus is known to undergo
A) mitosis
B) meiosis
C) fragmentation
D) mutation
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In binary fission, the parent cell's nucleus divides through ordinary mitosis, producing two genetically identical daughter nuclei, before the cytoplasm itself subsequently divides to complete the split into two separate daughter cells.

Q: The most reliable estimate of growth is by measuring changes in
A) length
B) volume
C) surface area
D) dry weight
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Dry weight (measured after removing all water content) provides the most reliable and accurate estimate of true biological growth, since it reflects the actual accumulation of organic biomass, unaffected by fluctuations in water content that could otherwise distort simple length, volume, or wet-weight measurements.

Q: The outermost embryonic membrane in the mammal is the
A) amnion
B) chorion
C) allantois
D) yolk sac
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The chorion is specifically the outermost of the extra-embryonic membranes surrounding the developing mammalian embryo, later contributing to the formation of the placenta.

Q: Fertilization in humans usually takes place in the
A) lower part of the uterus
B) upper part of the uterus
C) lower part of the oviduct
D) upper part of the oviduct
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Fertilization in humans typically occurs in the upper part of the oviduct (fallopian tube), where the egg meets sperm shortly after ovulation, before the resulting zygote travels down toward the uterus for implantation.

Q: Bacteria multiply rapidly by means of
A) budding
B) fragmentation
C) binary fission
D) spore formation
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Bacteria characteristically reproduce and multiply rapidly through binary fission, a simple asexual process where one cell divides directly into two identical daughter cells.

Q: For growth to occur in organisms, the rate of
A) food storage must be low
B) catabolism must exceed that of anabolism
C) anabolism must exceed that of catabolism
D) food storage must be high
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Growth requires that the rate of anabolism (building up new complex molecules/tissue) exceeds the rate of catabolism (breaking down molecules for energy) — only when construction outpaces breakdown can net new biomass accumulate.

Q: In mammalian males, the excretory and reproductive systems share the
A) Ureter
B) Vas deferens
C) Urethra
D) Testes
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: In male mammals, the urethra serves a dual function as a shared passage for both urine (excretory system) and semen (reproductive system), unlike the ureter, vas deferens, or testes, which are each specific to only one of the two systems.

Q: The type of reproduction that leads to variation in animal and plant populations is
A) budding
B) sexual
C) vegetative
D) asexual
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Sexual reproduction, involving the combination of genetic material from two different parents (and the genetic recombination that occurs during meiosis), produces offspring with new gene combinations, generating genetic variation — unlike budding, vegetative propagation, or other asexual methods, which produce genetically identical offspring (clones).

Q: The type of reproduction that is common to both Hydra and yeast is
A) conjugation
B) binary fission
C) grafting
D) budding
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Both Hydra and yeast commonly reproduce asexually through budding — a small outgrowth forms on the parent organism and eventually develops into, and separates as, a new independent individual.

Q: The reproductive system of a male mammal is made up of

D) Plasmodium
