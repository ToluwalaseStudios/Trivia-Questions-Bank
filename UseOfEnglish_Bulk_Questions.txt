Q: The chairman's speech was concise. The word "concise" means:
A) lengthy
B) brief and clear
C) confusing
D) controversial
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Concise" means expressing much in few words, i.e. brief and clear.

Q: The manager was reluctant to approve the proposal. The opposite of "reluctant" is:
A) unwilling
B) hesitant
C) willing
D) doubtful
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Reluctant" means unwilling or hesitant; its direct opposite is "willing."

Q: The student's explanation was plausible. This means that the explanation was:
A) impossible
B) believable
C) humorous
D) incomplete
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Plausible" means seeming reasonable or probable, i.e. believable.

Q: The witness gave a credible account of the incident. The opposite of "credible" is:
A) convincing
B) reliable
C) doubtful
D) accurate
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Credible" means believable or trustworthy; its opposite is "doubtful," meaning not easily believed.

Q: Neither the principal nor the teachers ___ aware of the change in timetable.
A) was
B) were
C) is
D) has been
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: With "neither...nor," the verb agrees with the noun closer to it. Since "teachers" (plural) is nearer the verb, "were" is correct.

Q: If I ___ you, I would reconsider the decision.
A) am
B) was
C) were
D) be
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This is a hypothetical/subjunctive condition, which conventionally takes "were" regardless of subject: "If I were you..."

Q: By the time the doctor arrived, the patient ___ unconscious.
A) becomes
B) has become
C) had become
D) was becoming
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The past perfect tense ("had become") is used for an action completed before another past action (the doctor's arrival).

Q: The committee recommended that the proposal ___ immediately.
A) is implemented
B) was implemented
C) be implemented
D) has been implemented
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: After verbs like "recommend" expressing a demand or suggestion, the subjunctive form ("be implemented") is used.

Q: The quality of the products ___ improved considerably.
A) have
B) are
C) has
D) were
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The subject of the sentence is "the quality" (singular), not "products," so it takes the singular verb "has."

Q: The students were prohibited ___ entering the laboratory without supervision.
A) from
B) against
C) with
D) for
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The correct idiomatic collocation is "prohibited from" doing something.

Q: When the principal asked who had damaged the equipment, everyone kept mum. This means that everyone:
A) became angry
B) remained silent
C) left the room
D) denied responsibility
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The idiom "kept mum" means remained silent or said nothing.

Q: The new student cut corners while carrying out the assignment. This means that the student:
A) worked very carefully
B) avoided unnecessary work
C) used shortcuts, often at the expense of quality
D) refused to participate
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Cut corners" is an idiom meaning to do something in the easiest or cheapest way, often sacrificing quality.

Q: If the statement is: "It is not beyond the realm of possibility that he will win." It means that:
A) He will certainly win
B) He cannot possibly win
C) He may possibly win
D) He has already won
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Not beyond the realm of possibility" is a double negative meaning something is possible, so the correct reading is that he may possibly win.

Q: "Hardly had the examination begun when the power supply was interrupted." This means:
A) The examination ended before the power went off
B) The power went off shortly after the examination began
C) The examination never began
D) The power went off long before the examination
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Hardly...when" indicates that one event (power interruption) followed almost immediately after another (the exam beginning).

Q: The witness's statement was inconsistent with the evidence. The word "inconsistent" means:
A) compatible
B) contradictory
C) identical
D) supportive
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Inconsistent" means not in agreement or contradictory, i.e. not matching the evidence.

Q: The doctor advised the patient to ___ smoking.
A) desist from
B) desist of
C) desist with
D) desist against
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The correct idiomatic preposition is "desist from," meaning to stop doing something.

Q: Let us go for the lecture, ___?
A) will we
B) shall we
C) won't we
D) aren't we
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Sentences beginning with "Let us" (suggestions) take the question tag "shall we?"

Q: Nobody informed the students about the change, ___?
A) did they
B) didn't they
C) did he
D) wasn't it
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: "Nobody" is treated as negative, so the tag must be positive; informally, "they" is used to refer back to "nobody," giving "did they?"

Q: Which of the following words is most associated with medicine?
A) Verdict
B) Dosage
C) Plaintiff
D) Dividend
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Dosage" refers to the amount of a medicine to be taken, making it a medical term; the others relate to law and finance.

Q: Which of the following is most appropriate in the context of banking?
A) Affidavit
B) Premium
C) Overdraft
D) Diagnosis
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Overdraft" is a banking term referring to withdrawing more money than is available in an account.
