Q: The organelle primarily responsible for aerobic respiration is the
A) ribosome
B) mitochondrion
C) lysosome
D) Golgi apparatus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mitochondria contain the enzymes and electron transport systems used in aerobic respiration, which is why they are often called the "powerhouse of the cell."

Q: Which structure is present in plant cells but absent in animal cells?
A) Cell membrane
B) Ribosome
C) Cell wall
D) Mitochondrion
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Plant cells have a rigid cell wall, made mainly of cellulose, surrounding the cell membrane, giving the cell shape and support. Animal cells lack this structure — they have only a cell membrane.

Q: Osmosis is the movement of
A) solute molecules from high to low concentration
B) water molecules through a selectively permeable membrane
C) gases against their concentration gradient
D) proteins across a cell wall
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Osmosis is specifically the movement of water molecules from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration, through a selectively (partially) permeable membrane.

Q: A major difference between diffusion and active transport is that active transport
A) never involves membranes
B) requires energy
C) always involves water
D) occurs only in dead cells
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Diffusion is a passive process that happens naturally down a concentration gradient and needs no energy. Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient (from low to high), which requires the cell to spend energy, usually from ATP.

Q: Enzymes increase reaction rate mainly by
A) increasing activation energy
B) lowering activation energy
C) becoming permanent products
D) increasing temperature indefinitely
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Enzymes are biological catalysts. They speed up reactions by lowering the activation energy needed for the reaction to proceed, without being used up themselves in the process.

Q: The mineral element essential for chlorophyll formation is
A) magnesium
B) sodium
C) chlorine
D) iodine
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Magnesium is a central component of the chlorophyll molecule. Without enough magnesium, plants cannot make chlorophyll properly, which leads to yellowing of leaves (chlorosis).

Q: The oxygen released during photosynthesis comes directly from
A) carbon dioxide
B) glucose
C) water
D) chlorophyll
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: During the light-dependent stage of photosynthesis, water molecules are split apart (photolysis), releasing oxygen gas as a by-product — the oxygen does not come from carbon dioxide.

Q: The end products of aerobic respiration are
A) ethanol and carbon dioxide
B) lactic acid only
C) carbon dioxide, water and energy
D) glucose and oxygen
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Aerobic respiration fully breaks down glucose using oxygen, releasing carbon dioxide, water, and energy (as ATP) as the end products.

Q: In yeast, anaerobic respiration produces
A) lactic acid only
B) ethanol and carbon dioxide
C) glucose and oxygen
D) water and oxygen
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In the absence of oxygen, yeast cells carry out alcoholic fermentation, breaking down glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide — this is the process used in brewing and baking.

Q: Water is mainly transported through the
A) phloem
B) xylem
C) cortex
D) epidermis
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Xylem tissue is made of hollow, dead cells forming continuous tubes that carry water and dissolved minerals upward from the roots to the rest of the plant. Phloem, by contrast, transports manufactured food such as sugars.

Q: Which condition generally increases the rate of transpiration?
A) High humidity
B) Still air
C) High temperature
D) Low temperature
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Higher temperature gives water molecules more energy to evaporate from the leaf surface, and speeds up the rate at which water vapour diffuses out through the stomata, increasing transpiration.

Q: The blood vessel carrying oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart is the
A) pulmonary artery
B) pulmonary vein
C) vena cava
D) aorta
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood from the lungs back to the heart. This is an exception to the usual rule that veins carry deoxygenated blood — the pulmonary vein is one of the few veins that carries oxygen-rich blood.

Q: The blood cells mainly responsible for antibody production are
A) red blood cells
B) platelets
C) lymphocytes
D) neutrophils only
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell, produce antibodies that help the body recognize and fight specific pathogens as part of the immune response.

Q: Vaccination protects against disease primarily by
A) immediately killing every pathogen
B) stimulating immune memory
C) increasing blood pressure
D) destroying red blood cells
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A vaccine exposes the body to a harmless form (or part) of a pathogen, prompting the immune system to produce memory cells. If the real pathogen is encountered later, these memory cells allow a much faster and stronger immune response.

Q: The enzyme that begins protein digestion in the stomach is
A) amylase
B) pepsin
C) lipase
D) maltase
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Pepsin is secreted in the stomach and begins breaking down proteins into smaller polypeptides, working best in the acidic environment created by hydrochloric acid.

Q: Bile aids digestion mainly by
A) digesting proteins
B) emulsifying fats
C) converting glucose to glycogen
D) digesting starch directly
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Bile contains no digestive enzymes. Instead, it emulsifies fats — breaking large fat globules into smaller droplets — which increases the surface area available for lipase to act on.

Q: The functional unit of the kidney is the
A) nephron
B) neuron
C) alveolus
D) villus
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Each kidney contains thousands of tiny structures called nephrons, which filter the blood and form urine.

Q: The hormone mainly responsible for lowering blood glucose concentration is
A) glucagon
B) insulin
C) adrenaline
D) thyroxine
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Insulin, released by the pancreas, lowers blood glucose by encouraging cells to take up glucose from the blood and by promoting its storage as glycogen in the liver. Glucagon does the opposite — it raises blood glucose levels.

Q: The part of the brain responsible mainly for balance and coordination is the
A) cerebrum
B) medulla
C) cerebellum
D) hypothalamus
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The cerebellum coordinates muscle movement and helps maintain balance and posture.

Q: The integration centre for many simple reflex actions is the
A) spinal cord
B) cerebrum
C) cerebellum
D) retina
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Many simple reflexes are processed in the spinal cord rather than the brain. This allows for a very fast response (the reflex arc) without waiting for the brain to consciously process the stimulus.

Q: The fusion of male and female gametes is called
A) pollination
B) fertilization
C) germination
D) budding
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Fertilization is the fusion of a male gamete (such as sperm or a pollen nucleus) with a female gamete (the egg cell) to form a zygote.

Q: If T represents tallness and t represents dwarfness, the genotype Tt is
A) homozygous dominant
B) homozygous recessive
C) heterozygous
D) haploid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A genotype is heterozygous when it carries two different alleles for a gene — here, one dominant allele (T) and one recessive allele (t). Homozygous genotypes would instead have two identical alleles, either TT (dominant) or tt (recessive).

Q: A cross between Tt and Tt produces what proportion of offspring expected to be homozygous recessive?
A) 1/2
B) 1/4
C) 3/4
D) 1
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Crossing Tt × Tt produces offspring in the ratio 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Only the tt genotype is homozygous recessive, which makes up 1 out of every 4 offspring — that is, 1/4.

Q: Which of the following is an example of continuous variation?
A) ABO blood group
B) Ability to roll the tongue
C) Human height
D) Sex
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Continuous variation shows a smooth range of values with no sharp breaks between them, like human height, which can fall almost anywhere along a range. Blood group, tongue-rolling ability, and sex are examples of discontinuous variation, where individuals fall into distinct, separate categories.

Q: Natural selection favours organisms that
A) are always physically larger
B) possess advantageous heritable characteristics
C) never undergo mutation
D) cannot reproduce
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Natural selection favours individuals with heritable traits that give them an advantage in surviving and reproducing in their environment, allowing those traits to become more common in the population over successive generations.

Q: Which kingdom contains organisms such as mushrooms?
A) Plantae
B) Fungi
C) Animalia
D) Monera
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mushrooms belong to the kingdom Fungi, along with yeasts and moulds. Unlike plants, fungi obtain their nutrients by absorption rather than photosynthesis.

Q: The scientific name of an organism consists primarily of its
A) family and order
B) genus and species
C) kingdom and phylum
D) class and family
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Scientific names follow the binomial naming system, made up of the genus name followed by the species name — for example, Homo sapiens.

Q: Which organism is responsible for malaria?
A) Plasmodium
B) Trypanosoma
C) Salmonella
D) Rhizopus
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Malaria is caused by parasites of the genus Plasmodium, which are transmitted to humans through the bite of an infected female Anopheles mosquito.

Q: The vector of malaria is the
A) housefly
B) female Anopheles mosquito
C) tsetse fly
D) cockroach
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The female Anopheles mosquito is the vector (carrier) that transmits the Plasmodium parasite from an infected person to a healthy person when it bites to feed on blood.

Q: The ultimate source of energy for most ecosystems is
A) soil
B) decomposers
C) sunlight
D) water
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Sunlight is captured by producers (green plants) during photosynthesis and converted into chemical energy, which then flows through the food chain to all other organisms in the ecosystem.

Q: In the food chain grass → grasshopper → frog → snake, the frog is a
A) producer
B) primary consumer
C) secondary consumer
D) decomposer
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Grass is the producer, the grasshopper (which eats grass) is the primary consumer, and the frog (which eats the grasshopper) is the secondary consumer, since it feeds on a primary consumer.

Q: The gradual replacement of one community by another over time is called
A) succession
B) mutation
C) competition
D) predation
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Ecological succession is the gradual, step-by-step change in the species that dominate an area over time — for example, bare land slowly developing into grassland, then shrubland, and eventually forest.

Q: The maximum population size that an environment can sustainably support is its
A) natality
B) carrying capacity
C) mortality
D) population density
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Carrying capacity is the largest population size that a given environment's resources — food, water, space, and so on — can support over the long term without being used up faster than they can be replaced.

Q: The thick fur of animals living in very cold regions is primarily an adaptation for
A) increasing water loss
B) reducing heat loss
C) increasing digestion
D) preventing reproduction
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Thick fur traps a layer of insulating air close to the animal's skin, which reduces the rate at which body heat escapes into the cold surroundings.

Q: The transfer of pollen grains from anther to stigma is
A) fertilization
B) pollination
C) germination
D) dispersal
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Pollination is the transfer of pollen grains from the anther (the male part of a flower) to the stigma (the female part). This must happen before fertilization can occur.

Q: Auxins are strongly associated with
A) phototropic growth
B) blood clotting
C) digestion
D) antibody production
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Auxins are plant hormones that control growth responses to light (phototropism), causing plant shoots to bend and grow towards a light source.

Q: The joint between the skull and the first vertebra is an example of a
A) hinge joint
B) pivot joint
C) ball-and-socket joint
D) gliding joint
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The joint between the skull and the first vertebra (the atlas) is a pivot joint, allowing rotational movement — this is what lets you turn your head from side to side.

Q: The biceps and triceps operate as
A) synergistic muscles only
B) antagonistic muscles
C) involuntary muscles
D) cardiac muscles
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The biceps and triceps work as an antagonistic pair — when the biceps contracts to bend the arm, the triceps relaxes, and when the triceps contracts to straighten the arm, the biceps relaxes. Muscles that work in opposite directions like this are called antagonistic muscles.

Q: Which process removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere?
A) Respiration
B) Combustion
C) Photosynthesis
D) Decomposition
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: During photosynthesis, plants absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere and use it, along with water and sunlight, to produce glucose and oxygen. Respiration, combustion, and decomposition all release carbon dioxide instead of removing it.

Q: A man with blood group A whose genotype is IAi marries a woman with blood group B whose genotype is IBi. What proportion of their children could have blood group O?
A) 0%
B) 25%
C) 50%
D) 75%
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Crossing IAi × IBi gives possible offspring genotypes IAIB, IAi, IBi, and ii, each with an equal 1-in-4 chance of occurring. Only the ii genotype produces blood group O, so the proportion is 1 out of 4, or 25%.
