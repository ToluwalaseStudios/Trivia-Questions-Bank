Q: When the chairman said the proposal was a tall order, he meant that the proposal was
A) very expensive
B) difficult to accomplish
C) unnecessary
D) poorly written
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "A tall order" means something difficult or demanding to accomplish.

Q: The principal turned a deaf ear to the students' complaints. This means that he
A) could not hear them
B) ignored their complaints
C) punished them
D) agreed with them
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: To "turn a deaf ear" to something means to deliberately ignore it or refuse to listen to it — the principal chose not to pay attention to the complaints.

Q: If a politician sits on the fence, he
A) refuses to take a definite position
B) supports everyone equally
C) has resigned
D) has won an election
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Someone who "sits on the fence" avoids committing to either side of an issue — they refuse to take a clear, definite position.

Q: The manager said that the new employee had a chip on his shoulder. This means the employee
A) was extremely intelligent
B) was unnecessarily resentful
C) was physically injured
D) was very generous
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: To have "a chip on one's shoulder" means to carry a lingering feeling of resentment or grievance, often over feeling unfairly treated — it describes someone who is unnecessarily resentful, not someone who is injured or generous.

Q: The scientist's explanation left no stone unturned. This means that the scientist
A) searched thoroughly
B) made a mistake
C) refused to investigate
D) changed the subject
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: This idiom means to try every possible way or method to achieve something — it describes a very thorough effort, not carelessness.

Q: The man's audacious behaviour shocked everyone.
A) timid
B) bold
C) careless
D) dishonest
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Audacious" describes behaviour that is bold and daring, often in a way that surprises or shocks others.

Q: The teacher was meticulous in marking the scripts.

EXPLANATION: "Meticulous" describes someone who pays very careful, close attention to detail — being thorough rather than careless or quick.

Q: The witness gave a plausible explanation.

Q: The minister's speech was concise.

EXPLANATION: "Concise" means expressing something clearly using few words — in other words, brief.

Q: The student's answer was ambiguous.

Q: The government plans to curtail unnecessary expenditure.

Q: The man's benevolent actions were praised.
A) cruel

ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Benevolent" describes actions or a person that are kind, generous, and well-meaning — in other words, charitable.

Q: The chairman's decision was arbitrary.
A) deliberate
B) systematic
C) random
D) sudden
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Arbitrary" means based on random choice or personal whim rather than any reason or system. Its opposite is "systematic," meaning done according to a fixed plan or method.

Q: The witness gave a credible account.
A) believable
B) doubtful
C) convincing
D) reliable
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Credible" means believable or trustworthy. Its opposite is "doubtful," which suggests something is uncertain or hard to believe.

Q: The medicine had an adverse effect.
A) harmful
B) beneficial
C) negative
D) dangerous
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Adverse" means harmful or unfavourable. Its opposite is "beneficial," meaning helpful or advantageous.

Q: His argument was coherent.
A) logical
B) organized
C) confused
D) persuasive
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Coherent" describes something that is logical, clear, and easy to follow. Its opposite is "confused," describing something disorganized or hard to understand.

Q: The candidate was reluctant to accept the offer.
A) unwilling
B) hesitant
C) eager
D) doubtful
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Reluctant" means unwilling or hesitant to do something. Its opposite is "eager," which means enthusiastic and willing.

Q: Neither the students nor their teacher ______ aware of the change.
A) were
B) are
C) was
D) have been
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: With "neither...nor," the verb agrees with the nearer subject. The nearer subject here is "teacher," which is singular, so the verb must be singular: "was."

Q: If I ______ you, I would reconsider the decision.
A) am
B) was
C) were
D) be
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This is the subjunctive mood, used for hypothetical or unreal situations. In formal English, "were" is used with "I" in statements like this ("If I were you..."), even though "I" would normally take "was" elsewhere.

Q: By the time we arrived, the lecture ______.
A) begins
B) has begun
C) had begun
D) will begin
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The lecture had already started before the group arrived, so the past perfect tense ("had begun") is needed to show that one past action happened before another past action.

Q: The committee ______ divided in its opinion.
A) are
B) is
C) have
D) were
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Committee" is a collective noun. Since the sentence uses the singular possessive "its," the committee is being treated as one single unit here, so it takes the singular verb "is."

Q: She prefers reading ______ watching television.
A) than
B) from
C) to
D) over than
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The correct construction is "prefer something TO something else" (not "than"), so "to" is the correct word to complete this comparison.

Q: He has lived in Ibadan ______ 2022.
A) for
B) since
C) during
D) from
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Since" is used with a specific point in time (like a year or date) to show when an ongoing action started. "For" would instead be used to state a duration, such as "for three years."

Q: The students arrived ______ the examination had started.
A) before
B) after
C) until
D) while
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The examination had already begun (shown by the past perfect "had started") before the students arrived, so "after" correctly shows that their arrival happened later than the start of the exam.

Q: I would rather you ______ here tonight.
A) stay
B) stayed
C) staying
D) have stayed
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: After "would rather" when talking about someone else's action, English uses a past tense form even though the meaning is present or future — this subjunctive-like structure makes "stayed" the correct choice here.

Q: The news ______ shocking.
A) were

D) was

EXPLANATION: "News" looks plural because it ends in "-s," but it is grammatically singular, so it takes the singular verb "was."

Q: One of my friends ______ going to Lagos tomorrow.

Q: Hardly had the teacher entered ______ the students became silent.
A) than
B) when
C) then
D) and
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Hardly...when" is a fixed idiomatic pairing used to show that one event happened immediately after another. It is not paired with "than," which instead pairs with "no sooner."

Q: No sooner had he arrived ______ it began to rain.
A) when
B) than
C) and
D) while
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "No sooner...than" is the fixed idiomatic pairing used to show that one event happened immediately after another.

Q: The woman, together with her children, ______ arrived.
A) have
B) are
C) has
D) were
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The main subject of the sentence is "woman" (singular); the phrase "together with her children" is a parenthetical addition and does not change the number of the subject, so the verb stays singular: "has."

Q: In the sentence, "The boy ran quickly," the word "quickly" is a/an
A) adjective
B) adverb
C) noun
D) conjunction
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Quickly" describes how the boy ran — it modifies the verb "ran" — which is exactly what an adverb does.

Q: In "The book on the table is mine," the expression "on the table" functions as
A) a noun phrase
B) an adjective phrase
C) a prepositional phrase
D) an adverb clause
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "On the table" begins with the preposition "on," followed by its object "the table." A phrase built this way, starting with a preposition, is called a prepositional phrase.

Q: Which sentence is correctly punctuated?

B) However, we continued the journey.
C) However we, continued the journey.
D) However; we continued the journey.
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: When "however" starts a sentence as a linking word meaning "nevertheless," it should be followed by a comma to separate it from the rest of the sentence.

Q: Choose the word that has the same vowel sound as "seat."
A) sit
B) beat
C) set
D) said
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Seat" and "beat" both contain the long vowel sound /iː/. "Sit," "set," and "said" all have different, shorter vowel sounds.

Q: Choose the word that has the same vowel sound as "cup."
A) put
B) food
C) luck
D) boot
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Cup" and "luck" both contain the short vowel sound /ʌ/, as in "cut" or "much." The other options have different vowel sounds — "put" and "boot" have rounded vowel sounds, and "food" has a long /uː/ sound.

Q: Which word has a different vowel sound?
A) beat
B) seat
C) meat
D) great
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: "Beat," "seat," and "meat" all contain the vowel sound /iː/, but "great" is pronounced with /eɪ/ instead, making it the odd one out.

Q: Which word rhymes with "light"?
A) late
B) height
C) heat
D) hit
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Light" is pronounced /laɪt/, and "height" is pronounced /haɪt/ — both share the same ending vowel and consonant sounds, which is what makes them rhyme.

Q: Which word has the same initial consonant sound as "chair"?
A) chemist
B) church
C) character
D) chorus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: "Chair" and "church" both begin with the /tʃ/ sound. "Chemist," "character," and "chorus" all begin with a /k/ sound instead, even though they're all spelled with "ch."

Q: Which word contains a silent "k"?
A) knee
B) king
C) keep
D) kind
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In English, the letter "k" is silent when it comes before "n" at the start of a word, as in "knee," "knife," and "know." In "king," "keep," and "kind," the "k" is pronounced normally.

Q: The expression "once in a blue moon" means
A) regularly
B) immediately

D) unexpectedly
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: "Once in a blue moon" describes something that happens very rarely or almost never, since a "blue moon" — a second full moon in one calendar month — is itself an uncommon event.

Q: The student's success was attributed to hard work. The expression means that his success was
A) blamed on hard work
B) caused by hard work
C) prevented by hard work
D) unrelated to hard work

Q: The sentence "The manager had the report typed" means that
A) the manager typed the report personally
B) someone typed the report for the manager
C) the report typed the manager
D) the manager refused to type the report
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This is the causative structure "have + object + past participle." "Had the report typed" means the manager arranged for somebody else to type it, not that he typed it himself.
