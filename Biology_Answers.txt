Q: What is the powerhouse of the cell?
A) Nucleus
B) Golgi apparatus
C) Mitochondria
D) Endoplasmic reticulum
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Mitochondria generate ATP through cellular respiration and are therefore called the powerhouse of the cell.

Q: Which organelle is responsible for protein synthesis?
A) Golgi apparatus
B) Ribosome
C) Lysosome
D) Peroxisome
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis (translation of mRNA into polypeptide chains).

Q: What is the basic unit of heredity?
A) Protein
B) Gene
C) Enzyme
D) Carbohydrate
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A gene is a segment of DNA that codes for a specific trait and is the basic unit of heredity.

Q: What is the process by which green plants make their food?
A) Photosynthesis
B) Respiration
C) Digestion
D) Fermentation
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Photosynthesis converts light energy, carbon dioxide and water into glucose and oxygen in the presence of chlorophyll.

Q: Which of the following is not a function of the liver?
A) Detoxification
B) Carbohydrate storage
C) Protein synthesis
D) Hormone secretion
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The liver performs detoxification, stores glycogen and synthesises plasma proteins. Hormone secretion is primarily the function of endocrine glands.

Q: Which blood type is known as the universal donor?
A) Type A
B) Type AB
C) Type O
D) Type B
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Type O blood has neither A nor B antigens on the red cells, so it can be safely given to any ABO blood group.

Q: Which hormone is responsible for regulating blood sugar levels?
A) Insulin
B) Estrogen
C) Testosterone
D) Thyroxine
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Insulin, produced by the beta cells of the pancreas, lowers blood glucose by promoting its uptake into cells.

Q: Which of the following is an example of a renewable resource?
A) Coal
B) Natural gas
C) Wind
D) Petroleum
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Wind is continuously replenished by natural processes and is therefore renewable, unlike fossil fuels.

Q: Which of the following is a greenhouse gas?
A) Nitrogen
B) Oxygen
C) Carbon dioxide
D) Hydrogen
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Carbon dioxide traps heat in the atmosphere and is a major greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.

Q: Which gas is produced during cellular respiration?
A) Oxygen
B) Carbon dioxide
C) Nitrogen
D) Hydrogen
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In aerobic respiration, glucose is oxidised and carbon dioxide is released as a waste product (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy).

Q: Which part of the brain controls balance and coordination?
A) Cerebrum
B) Cerebellum
C) Brainstem
D) Hypothalamus
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The cerebellum is responsible for coordinating voluntary movements, balance and posture.

Q: What is the largest organ in the human body?
A) Liver
B) Brain
C) Skin
D) Heart
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The skin is the largest organ by both surface area and weight. The liver is the largest internal organ.

Q: Which of the following is not a function of the skeletal system?
A) Protecting internal organs
B) Producing blood cells
C) Controlling body temperature
D) Providing structural support
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The skeletal system supports the body, protects organs and produces blood cells in bone marrow. Temperature regulation is mainly a function of the skin and circulatory system.

Q: What is the function of white blood cells?
A) Transporting oxygen
B) Fighting infection
C) Carrying nutrients
D) Clotting blood
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: White blood cells (leucocytes) defend the body against pathogens by phagocytosis, antibody production and other immune responses.

Q: What is the purpose of the circulatory system?
A) Transportation of gases, nutrients, and waste products
B) Maintaining body temperature
C) Regulating hormones
D) Providing structural support
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The circulatory system transports oxygen, carbon dioxide, nutrients, hormones and metabolic wastes throughout the body.

Q: What is the role of DNA in cells?
A) Providing energy
B) Storing genetic information
C) Breaking down waste
D) Regulating cell growth
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: DNA stores the genetic instructions that determine the structure and function of all living organisms.

Q: What is the purpose of the endocrine system?
A) Controlling movement
B) Regulating metabolism, growth, and development
C) Excreting waste
D) Digesting food
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The endocrine system secretes hormones that regulate metabolism, growth, development, reproduction and homeostasis.

Q: Which of the following is not a component of blood?
A) Platelets
B) Plasma
C) Red blood cells
D) Bone marrow
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Blood consists of plasma, red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. Bone marrow is the tissue that produces blood cells, not a component of blood itself.

Q: What is the process by which plants release water vapour into the atmosphere?
A) Transpiration
B) Respiration
C) Photosynthesis
D) Germination
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from plant leaves through stomata.

Q: Which of the following is not a characteristic of living organisms?
A) Growth and development
B) Response to stimuli
C) Ability to reproduce
D) Inorganic composition
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Living organisms are composed of organic compounds (proteins, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids). Inorganic composition is characteristic of non-living matter.

Q: What is the process by which cells divide to produce new somatic cells?
A) Replication
B) Mitosis
C) Meiosis
D) Fertilization
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells for growth and repair. Meiosis produces haploid gametes.

Q: What is the main function of the kidneys?
A) Regulating blood sugar levels
B) Filtering waste from the blood
C) Producing hormones
D) Digesting food
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The kidneys filter blood to remove urea, excess salts and water, forming urine, and also help regulate blood pressure and pH.

Q: What is the role of chlorophyll in photosynthesis?
A) Absorbing carbon dioxide
B) Absorbing sunlight
C) Releasing oxygen
D) Breaking down glucose
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Chlorophyll absorbs light energy (mainly in the blue and red wavelengths) which is used to drive the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.

Q: Which of the following is not a type of muscle tissue?
A) Skeletal
B) Cardiac
C) Adipose
D) Smooth
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The three types of muscle tissue are skeletal, cardiac and smooth. Adipose is a type of connective tissue that stores fat.

Q: What is the function of the pancreas in the digestive system?
A) Producing bile
B) Absorbing nutrients
C) Producing digestive enzymes
D) Storing food
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The pancreas secretes pancreatic juice containing digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases) into the duodenum.

Q: What is the role of the myelin sheath in nerve cells?
A) Speeding up the transmission of nerve impulses
B) Protecting the cell from damage
C) Regulating hormone levels
D) Providing structural support
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: The myelin sheath insulates the axon and allows saltatory conduction, greatly increasing the speed of nerve impulse transmission.

Q: Which of the following is not a type of symbiotic relationship?
A) Mutualism
B) Parasitism
C) Predation
D) Commensalism
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Symbiosis involves a close, long-term association between two species (mutualism, parasitism, commensalism). Predation is a short-term feeding relationship.

Q: What is the role of ATP in cellular metabolism?
A) Storing genetic information
B) Providing energy
C) Regulating cell growth
D) Breaking down waste products
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the universal energy currency of the cell; its hydrolysis releases energy for metabolic processes.

Q: What is the function of the ribosomes in the cell?
A) Producing energy
B) Synthesizing proteins
C) Packaging molecules for export
D) Breaking down waste
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Ribosomes translate mRNA into polypeptide chains, i.e., they synthesise proteins.

Q: What is the process by which organisms maintain a stable internal environment?
A) Homeostasis
B) Respiration
C) Reproduction
D) Evolution
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Homeostasis is the maintenance of a constant internal environment (temperature, pH, water balance, etc.) despite external changes.

Q: Which of the following is not a type of passive transport?
A) Diffusion
B) Osmosis
C) Active transport
D) Facilitated diffusion
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion) does not require energy. Active transport requires ATP to move substances against a concentration gradient.

Q: What is the role of haemoglobin in the blood?
A) Fighting infections
B) Transporting oxygen
C) Clotting blood
D) Breaking down toxins
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Haemoglobin in red blood cells binds oxygen in the lungs and releases it to tissues.

Q: What is the function of the thymus gland in the immune system?
A) Producing antibodies
B) Filtering toxins from the blood
C) Maturation of T cells
D) Regulating body temperature
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The thymus is where immature T-lymphocytes mature and become immunocompetent.

Q: What is the primary function of the stomata in plant leaves?
A) Absorbing water
B) Exchanging gases
C) Storing nutrients
D) Producing sugars
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Stomata allow the exchange of carbon dioxide and oxygen and also control the rate of transpiration.

Q: What is the role of the Golgi apparatus in the cell?
A) Protein synthesis
B) Energy production
C) Packaging and shipping proteins
D) DNA replication
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids into vesicles for transport or secretion.

Q: What is the function of the hypothalamus in the brain?
A) Regulating body temperature only
B) Controlling hunger and thirst only
C) Maintaining homeostasis
D) All of the above
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The hypothalamus regulates body temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep, and many other homeostatic processes, and links the nervous and endocrine systems.

Q: Which of the following is a function of the smooth endoplasmic reticulum?
A) Protein synthesis
B) Lipid synthesis
C) Energy production
D) Waste disposal
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Smooth ER is involved in the synthesis of lipids, steroid hormones and the detoxification of drugs.

Q: What is the role of the gallbladder in the digestive system?
A) Producing bile
B) Storing bile
C) Digesting carbohydrates
D) Absorbing nutrients
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile produced by the liver and releases it into the duodenum when needed.

Q: What is the function of the centrioles in animal cells?
A) Protein synthesis
B) Cell division
C) Energy production
D) Waste disposal
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Centrioles organise the spindle fibres that separate chromosomes during mitosis and meiosis.

Q: What is the process by which water moves across a semipermeable membrane?
A) Osmosis
B) Diffusion
C) Active transport
D) Facilitated diffusion
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential across a selectively permeable membrane.
