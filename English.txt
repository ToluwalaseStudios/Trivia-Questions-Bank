ENGLISH LANGUAGE – 20 QUESTIONS WITH DETAILED EXPLANATIONS

Q1: The man should be known to you
A) You should be known to him
B) You should be knowing the man
C) You should have known the man
D) You should know the man
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The original sentence is in the passive voice. The correct active-voice equivalent that preserves the meaning and tense is “You should know the man.” Option A reverses the meaning, B uses an incorrect continuous form after “should,” and C changes the tense to past perfect.

Q2: We gave the graduating students a ______ they will never forget
A) send off
B) send forth
C) send-off
D) send-forth
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Send-off” is the correct compound noun (hyphenated) meaning a farewell party or ceremony. “Send off” without the hyphen is a phrasal verb, while “send forth” and “send-forth” are not standard English expressions for this context.

Q3: James ______ the letter to Abuja yesterday.
A) sent forth
B) sent off
C) send forth
D) send off
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The past-tense form required by “yesterday” is “sent.” The correct phrasal verb for posting or despatching a letter is “sent off.” “Sent forth” is archaic and not used for ordinary correspondence; the present-tense forms in C and D are grammatically wrong.

Q4: I wish I ______ to swim when I was younger
A) had learnt
B) learn
C) learnt
D) was learning
E) have learnt
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: “I wish” referring to a past unreal situation requires the past-perfect form (had + past participle). “Had learnt” correctly expresses the regret about not having acquired the skill earlier. The other options use the wrong tense or aspect.

Q5: I would have been surprised if you ______ the orange
A) plucked
B) have plucked
C) had plucked
D) did pluck
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This is a Type-3 (past unreal) conditional. The if-clause must therefore use the past-perfect “had plucked.” “Would have been” in the main clause confirms the past-perfect requirement in the if-clause.

Q6: This exercise must be done ______ possible
A) as quick as
B) as quicker as
C) as quickly as
D) as quickest as
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: “Quickly” is the adverb that correctly modifies the verb “done.” The comparative and superlative forms in B and D are ungrammatical in the “as \ldots as” structure, and “quick” is an adjective, not an adverb.

Q7: The young lady visited her mother ______
A) in the evening at 6 O’clock yesterday
B) at 6 O’clock yesterday in the evening
C) yesterday in the evening at 6 O’clock
D) at 6 O’clock in the evening yesterday
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: The conventional order of time adverbials in English is: time of day → part of day → day. “At 6 O’clock in the evening yesterday” follows this order and is the most natural and grammatically preferred arrangement.

Q8: The police ______ celebrating the arrest of Lawrence Anini
A) is
B) are
C) was
D) were
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In British and Nigerian English usage, “the police” is treated as a plural collective noun when referring to the members of the force, so the plural verb “are” is required.

Q9: One of the ______ who ______ in the premises ______ been arrested
A) woman/sell/have
B) women/sells/have
C) woman/sells/has
D) women/sell/has
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: “One of the \ldots” requires a plural noun (“women”). The relative clause “who sell” takes the plural verb because its antecedent is “women.” The main verb agrees with the singular subject “One,” so “has been arrested” is correct.

Q10: The young boy was so tired that he could not walk any ______
A) further
B) farther
C) farthest
D) furthest
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Farther” is the preferred form when referring to physical, measurable distance. “Further” is normally reserved for figurative or abstract advancement. The context is clearly physical distance, so “farther” is correct.

Q11: My ______ for you is to refuse the ______ from evil friends.
A) advice/advice
B) advise/advise
C) advice/advise
D) advise/advice
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Both blanks require the noun form “advice.” “Advise” is the verb. The sentence means “My counsel for you is to refuse the counsel from evil friends.”

Q12: Kindly ______ me your book because my friend has ______ mine
A) borrow/lent
B) borrow/borrowed
C) lend/lent
D) lend/borrowed
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: You ask someone to “lend” (give temporarily) you a book. The friend has already “borrowed” (taken temporarily) your book. The correct pair is therefore “lend \ldots borrowed.”

Q13: He alleged in court that he had been ______ by certain utterances which the accused made at a rally
A) slandered
B) assaulted
C) defiled
D) defrauded
E) charged
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Spoken false statements that damage a person’s reputation constitute slander. The context of “utterances” points directly to verbal defamation, i.e., slander.

Q14: The hen ______ another egg yesterday.
A) lay
B) lied
C) laid
D) lain
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The verb “lay” (to produce an egg) is transitive and its past tense is “laid.” “Lay” is present, “lied” is the past of “lie” (to tell an untruth), and “lain” is the past participle of “lie” (to recline).

Q15: Yesterday, Juan ______ on her sofa watching television for three hours.
A) lay
B) lied
C) laid
D) lain
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Here the verb means “to recline.” The past tense of the intransitive verb “lie” is “lay.” “Laid” would require a direct object, which is absent.

Q16: The girl ______ her mother’s picture on the wall last night
A) hung
B) hanged
C) hang
D) hanging
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: When the meaning is “to suspend or fasten an object,” the past tense is “hung.” “Hanged” is reserved exclusively for execution by hanging.

Q17: The prisoner was ______ two days ago
A) hung
B) hanged
C) hang
D) hanging
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The context is execution, so the correct past participle is “hanged.”

Q18: The congregation ______ large
A) is
B) are
C) was
D) were
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: When “congregation” is viewed as a single collective entity (its size), the singular verb “is” is used.

Q19: The magazine was ______ by the government for an offensive publication
A) prescribed
B) proscribed
C) suspended
D) condemned
E) persecuted
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: “Proscribe” means to forbid or ban by law. “Prescribe” means to recommend or order the use of something. The government banned the magazine, so “proscribed” is correct.

Q20: Must I finish the assignment today?
A) No, I needn’t
B) No, you needn’t
C) Yes, I must
D) No, you mustn’t
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The polite and grammatically correct negative response to a “Must I \ldots?” question that seeks permission to avoid an obligation is “No, you needn’t.” “Mustn’t” expresses prohibition, which is not the intended meaning.
