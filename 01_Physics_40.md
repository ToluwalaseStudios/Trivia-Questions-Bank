Q: A student measures the length of a metal rod as 25.0 cm. If the measurement has an uncertainty of 0.1 cm, the percentage uncertainty is approximately
A) 0.04%
B) 0.4%
C) 4.0%
D) 40%
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Percentage uncertainty = (uncertainty ÷ measured value) × 100 = (0.1 ÷ 25.0) × 100 = 0.4%.

Q: Which of the following has the dimensions of pressure?
A) MLT⁻²
B) ML⁻¹T⁻²
C) ML²T⁻²
D) ML⁻²T⁻¹
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Pressure = Force ÷ Area. Force has dimensions MLT⁻², and area has dimensions L². Dividing gives ML⁻¹T⁻².

Q: Which pair consists entirely of vector quantities?
A) Distance and speed
B) Mass and displacement
C) Velocity and acceleration
D) Work and momentum
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Velocity and acceleration both have magnitude and direction, so both are vectors. Distance, speed, mass, and work are scalars, so the other options don't qualify as "entirely" vectors.

Q: A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 4 m/s² for 5 s. What distance does it cover?
A) 20 m
B) 40 m
C) 50 m
D) 100 m
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Using s = ut + ½at², with u = 0: s = ½ × 4 × 5² = ½ × 4 × 25 = 50 m.

Q: A particle moving at 20 m/s is uniformly decelerated at 5 m/s². How long does it take to stop?
A) 2 s
B) 4 s
C) 5 s
D) 10 s
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Using v = u + at, with final velocity v = 0: 0 = 20 − 5t, so t = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 s.

Q: A ball is projected horizontally from the top of a 20 m high cliff. Neglecting air resistance, which quantity remains constant throughout its motion?
A) Vertical velocity
B) Horizontal velocity
C) Resultant velocity
D) Vertical displacement
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: With air resistance ignored, there is no horizontal force acting on the ball, so its horizontal velocity stays the same throughout the flight. Its vertical velocity keeps increasing because gravity still acts downward.

Q: A projectile has a maximum range when projected at an angle of
A) 30°
B) 45°
C) 60°
D) 90°
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Range R = u²sin(2θ) ÷ g. This is greatest when sin(2θ) = 1, which happens when 2θ = 90°, meaning θ = 45°.

Q: A body moves in a circle of radius 2 m at 4 m/s. Its centripetal acceleration is
A) 2 m/s²
B) 4 m/s²
C) 8 m/s²
D) 16 m/s²
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Centripetal acceleration a = v² ÷ r = 4² ÷ 2 = 16 ÷ 2 = 8 m/s².

Q: If the distance between two masses is doubled, the gravitational force between them becomes
A) twice as large
B) four times as large
C) half as large
D) one-quarter as large
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Gravitational force follows F ∝ 1/r² (inverse square law). Doubling the distance divides the force by 2² = 4, so the new force is one-quarter of the original.

Q: A body is in translational equilibrium when
A) its velocity is zero
B) its acceleration is maximum
C) the resultant force acting on it is zero
D) its kinetic energy is zero
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: By Newton's second law (F = ma), if the resultant force on a body is zero, its acceleration is also zero. The body may still be moving, just at constant velocity — that's the condition for equilibrium.

Q: A force of 20 N acts perpendicular to a spanner at a distance of 0.5 m from the pivot. The moment is
A) 10 Nm
B) 20 Nm
C) 40 Nm
D) 100 Nm
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot = 20 × 0.5 = 10 Nm.

Q: A force of 50 N moves an object through 4 m in the direction of the force. The work done is
A) 12.5 J
B) 46 J
C) 200 J
D) 400 J
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Work done = Force × distance = 50 × 4 = 200 J.

Q: A machine does 6000 J of work in 20 s. Its power is
A) 30 W
B) 300 W
C) 1200 W
D) 120,000 W
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Power = Work ÷ time = 6000 ÷ 20 = 300 W.

Q: A body of mass 2 kg moves at 10 m/s. Its kinetic energy is
A) 20 J
B) 50 J
C) 100 J
D) 200 J
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Kinetic energy = ½mv² = ½ × 2 × 10² = ½ × 2 × 100 = 100 J.

Q: A 2 kg metal has specific heat capacity 500 J/kg·K. How much heat is required to raise its temperature by 20 K?
A) 5,000 J
B) 10,000 J
C) 20,000 J
D) 50,000 J
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Heat required Q = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change = 2 × 500 × 20 = 20,000 J.

Q: During the melting of a pure solid at its melting point, the temperature remains constant because the supplied heat is mainly used to
A) increase the kinetic energy of molecules
B) break intermolecular bonds
C) increase the mass of the substance
D) increase atmospheric pressure
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: At the melting point, the heat supplied (latent heat of fusion) goes into overcoming the forces holding the molecules in a fixed structure, not into raising their kinetic energy. That's why temperature stays constant while melting is happening.

Q: A gas occupies 4 L at 300 K. At constant pressure, what volume will it occupy at 450 K?
A) 2.67 L
B) 4 L
C) 6 L
D) 8 L
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: By Charles' law, V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂. So V₂ = (4 × 450) ÷ 300 = 6 L.

Q: Which of the following waves cannot travel through a vacuum?
A) Light
B) Radio waves
C) X-rays
D) Sound
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Sound is a mechanical wave, so it needs particles of a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to travel through. Light, radio waves, and X-rays are electromagnetic waves, so they can travel through a vacuum.

Q: A wave has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.68 m. Its speed is
A) 340 m/s
B) 735 m/s
C) 500 m/s
D) 0.00136 m/s
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Wave speed = frequency × wavelength = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s.

Q: The phenomenon responsible for the apparent change in frequency of a sound due to relative motion between source and observer is
A) resonance
B) diffraction
C) Doppler effect
D) polarization
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: This apparent change in pitch caused by relative motion between a sound source and a listener is called the Doppler effect — for example, how a siren sounds higher-pitched as it approaches and lower as it moves away.

Q: An object is placed beyond 2F of a converging lens. The image formed is
A) virtual, erect and magnified
B) real, inverted and diminished
C) virtual, inverted and diminished
D) real, erect and magnified
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: When an object sits beyond 2F of a converging lens, the image forms between F and 2F on the other side of the lens. That image is real, inverted, and smaller than the object.

Q: When light travels from air into glass, it generally
A) speeds up and bends away from the normal
B) slows down and bends towards the normal
C) speeds up and bends towards the normal
D) maintains its speed
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Glass is optically denser than air, so light slows down as it enters. Because it slows down while entering the denser medium, it also bends towards the normal.

Q: Total internal reflection can occur only when light travels
A) from a rarer to a denser medium
B) from a denser to a rarer medium
C) between two equally dense media
D) through a vacuum only
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Total internal reflection happens when light tries to pass from a denser medium (like glass or water) into a rarer medium (like air) at an angle greater than the critical angle. It cannot happen the other way round.

Q: A current of 3 A flows through a 4 Ω resistor. The potential difference is
A) 0.75 V
B) 7 V
C) 12 V
D) 16 V
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: By Ohm's law, V = IR = 3 × 4 = 12 V.

Q: An electric heater rated 1000 W operates for 2 minutes. The electrical energy consumed is
A) 500 J
B) 2,000 J
C) 60,000 J
D) 120,000 J
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: 2 minutes = 120 seconds. Energy = Power × time = 1000 × 120 = 120,000 J.

Q: Two 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Their effective resistance is
A) 12 Ω
B) 6 Ω
C) 3 Ω
D) 1.5 Ω
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: For two equal resistors in parallel, the combined resistance is half of one resistor's value: 6 ÷ 2 = 3 Ω.

Q: In a series circuit, which quantity is the same through every resistor?
A) Potential difference
B) Current
C) Resistance
D) Power
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A series circuit has only one path for charge to flow, so the same current passes through every component. Potential difference is instead shared out and can differ across each resistor.

Q: The direction of force on a current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field is determined by
A) Fleming's left-hand rule
B) Fleming's right-hand rule
C) Lenz's law only
D) Ohm's law
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Fleming's left-hand rule gives the direction of the force (the motor effect) on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field. Fleming's right-hand rule is instead used for the induced current in generators.

Q: The production of an emf due to a change in magnetic flux through a circuit is called
A) electrolysis
B) electromagnetic induction
C) rectification
D) magnetization
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This is the definition of electromagnetic induction — a changing magnetic flux through a circuit induces an electromotive force (emf) in it.

Q: A transformer has 500 turns in its primary coil and 100 turns in its secondary coil. If the primary voltage is 240 V, the secondary voltage is
A) 24 V
B) 48 V
C) 480 V
D) 1200 V
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Secondary voltage ÷ Primary voltage = Secondary turns ÷ Primary turns. So secondary voltage = 240 × (100 ÷ 500) = 48 V.

Q: Which particle has no electric charge?
A) Proton
B) Electron
C) Neutron
D) Positron
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: A neutron carries no electric charge, unlike the proton (positive), electron (negative), and positron (positive).

Q: A radioactive sample has a half-life of 6 hours. What fraction remains after 18 hours?
A) 1/2
B) 1/4
C) 1/6
D) 1/8
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: 18 hours ÷ 6 hours = 3 half-lives. Each half-life halves the amount remaining, so the fraction left is ½ × ½ × ½ = 1/8.

Q: The photoelectric effect provides evidence that electromagnetic radiation can behave as
A) only a wave
B) particles
C) sound
D) matter waves only
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: In the photoelectric effect, light knocks electrons out of a metal surface in discrete packets of energy. This behaviour can only be explained by treating light as particles (photons), not purely as a wave.

Q: A diode is mainly used in a circuit to
A) amplify current
B) allow current predominantly in one direction
C) store electrical charge
D) increase resistance indefinitely
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A diode lets current flow easily in one direction while blocking (or heavily resisting) it in the reverse direction — this is why diodes are used for rectification.

Q: A pure semiconductor is called
A) extrinsic
B) intrinsic
C) metallic
D) electrolytic
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: A semiconductor in its pure form, with no added impurities, is called intrinsic. Once impurities are deliberately added (doping), it becomes extrinsic.

Q: A force of 200 N acts over an area of 0.5 m². The pressure is
A) 100 Pa
B) 200 Pa
C) 400 Pa
D) 1000 Pa
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Pressure = Force ÷ Area = 200 ÷ 0.5 = 400 Pa.

Q: A machine has an input work of 500 J and useful output work of 400 J. Its efficiency is
A) 20%
B) 40%
C) 80%
D) 125%
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Efficiency = (output work ÷ input work) × 100 = (400 ÷ 500) × 100 = 80%.

Q: Within the elastic limit, Hooke's law states that
A) extension is inversely proportional to force
B) extension is proportional to force
C) force is independent of extension
D) stress is always zero
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Hooke's law states that, within the elastic limit, the extension of a spring is directly proportional to the force (load) applied to it.

Q: The resistance offered by a fluid to the relative motion between its layers is called
A) density
B) viscosity
C) elasticity
D) surface tension
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: This internal friction between layers of a moving fluid is called viscosity — it's why thick liquids like honey flow more slowly than water.

Q: A 2 kg body is lifted vertically through 5 m. Taking g = 10 m/s², its gain in gravitational potential energy is
A) 10 J
B) 25 J
C) 50 J
D) 100 J
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Gravitational potential energy gained = mass × g × height = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J.
