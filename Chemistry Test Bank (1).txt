Q: The transition elements in the periodic table form complex molecules or ions because they
A) have vacant orbitals
B) are paramagnetic
C) are metals
D) are ligands
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Transition metals have vacant, low-energy d-orbitals (and often s and p orbitals close in energy). These accept lone pairs of electrons from ligands ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{Cl}^-$, $\text{CN}^-$) to form dative (coordinate) bonds, giving complex ions such as $[\text{Cu}(\text{NH}_3)_4]^{2+}$. Their small size and high charge density also help.

Q: In the extraction of aluminium from bauxite, the silicon impurities present are removed by
A) filtration
B) precipitation
C) coagulation
D) crystallization
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: In the Bayer process, bauxite is digested in hot concentrated $\text{NaOH}$. $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ dissolves as sodium aluminate, while $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{TiO}_2$ and the silicon impurities (as insoluble sodium aluminosilicate) stay solid. They are removed by filtration as "red mud". $\text{Al}(\text{OH})_3$ is then precipitated from the filtrate and heated to give pure $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$.

Q: An aqueous solution of $\text{NaHCO}_3$ is
A) acidic
B) alkaline
C) neutral
D) amphoteric
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: $\text{NaHCO}_3$ is the salt of a strong base ($\text{NaOH}$) and a weak acid ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$). The $\text{HCO}_3^-$ ion hydrolyses: $\text{HCO}_3^- + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{H}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{OH}^-$. The excess $\text{OH}^-$ makes the solution weakly alkaline ($\text{pH} \approx 8.3$).

Q: How many lone pairs of electrons are present in $\text{HF}$?
A) 0
B) 1
C) 2
D) 3
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Fluorine has 7 valence electrons ($2s^2 2p^5$). One electron is shared with hydrogen in the $\text{H}-\text{F}$ covalent bond, leaving 6 unshared valence electrons on fluorine. These 6 electrons form 3 lone pairs. Hydrogen contributes its single electron to the bond and has no lone pairs.

Q: A solution X, when added to another solution containing chloride ions, gives a white precipitate which is soluble on boiling. Solution X contains
A) $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ions
B) $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ ions
C) $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions
D) $\text{Pb}^{2+}$ ions
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: $\text{Pb}^{2+} + 2\text{Cl}^- \rightarrow \text{PbCl}_2(s)$, a white precipitate that dissolves in hot water and reappears as glistening crystals on cooling. Chlorides of $\text{Al}^{3+}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ and $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ are soluble and give no precipitate.

Q: If the $\text{pH}$ of a solution changes from 3 to 5, the concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions:
A) decreases by 2 times
B) decreases by 100 times
C) increases by 100 times
D) remains constant
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: $[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}}$. At $\text{pH}\ 3$, $[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-3}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$; at $\text{pH}\ 5$, $[\text{H}^+] = 10^{-5}\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}$. The ratio is $10^{-3} / 10^{-5} = 100$, so the concentration decreases by a factor of 100. Each unit change on the $\text{pH}$ scale represents a 10-fold change in hydrogen ion concentration.

Q: Which of the following oxides remains unchanged when heated in hydrogen gas?
A) $\text{CuO}$
B) $\text{ZnO}$
C) $\text{PbO}$
D) $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Hydrogen can reduce the oxides of metals lower than it in the reactivity series or moderately unreactive metals ($\text{CuO} + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{Cu} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{PbO} + \text{H}_2 \rightarrow \text{Pb} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{H}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Fe} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$). Zinc is more reactive than hydrogen and holds oxygen too strongly to be reduced by $\text{H}_2$ gas; $\text{ZnO}$ requires heating with carbon at high temperature for reduction.

Q: What is the oxidation number of nitrogen in $\text{HNO}_2$?
A) +3
B) +2
C) +1
D) 0
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Hydrogen has an oxidation number of $+1$ and each oxygen has $-2$ (total $-4$). Since $\text{HNO}_2$ is a neutral molecule, the sum of oxidation numbers is zero: $(+1) + N + (-4) = 0 \implies N = +3$.

Q: Which of the following bond types exists between different molecules of ethanoic acid?
A) Dative bond
B) Hydrogen bond
C) Covalent bond
D) Ionic bond
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The question refers to intermolecular forces. The hydrogen atom of the $-\text{OH}$ group in one ethanoic acid molecule is strongly attracted to the oxygen atom of the $\text{C}=\text{O}$ group of an adjacent molecule, forming hydrogen bonds that hold ethanoic acid molecules together as dimers.

Q: A current of $10\ \text{A}$ was passed for $1\ \text{hr}$ through a solution of copper(II) sulphate between copper electrodes. Which of the following statements is correct? [$1\ \text{Faraday} = 96,500\ \text{C}$]
A) $0.186\ \text{mol}$ of copper is dissolved from the cathode
B) $0.372\ \text{mol}$ of copper is deposited at the anode
C) The original $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ion concentration of the solution remains unchanged
D) The original $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ion concentration of the solution decreases
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Total charge $Q = I \times t = 10 \times 3600 = 36,000\ \text{C}$. Faradays passed $= 36,000 / 96,500 = 0.373\ \text{F}$. Copper dissolves at the anode ($\text{Cu} \rightarrow \text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^-$) and deposits at the cathode ($\text{Cu}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Cu}$). Moles of $\text{Cu}$ transferred $= 0.373 / 2 = 0.186\ \text{mol}$. Because copper dissolves from the anode at the exact same rate as it deposits onto the cathode, the concentration of $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ ions in the electrolyte stays constant.

Q: $10.0\ \text{dm}^3$ of air containing $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ as an impurity was passed through a solution of $\text{Pb}(\text{NO}_3)_2$ until all the $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ had reacted. The precipitate of $\text{PbS}$ was found to weigh $5.02\ \text{g}$ according to the equation:
$$\text{Pb}(\text{NO}_3)_2 + \text{H}_2\text{S} \rightarrow \text{PbS} + 2\text{HNO}_3$$
What is the percentage by volume of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ in the air? [$\text{Molar gas volume at s.t.p.} = 22.4\ \text{dm}^3\ \text{mol}^{-1}$; $\text{Pb} = 207, \text{S} = 32$]
A) $0.47\%$
B) $4.70\%$
C) $5.02\%$
D) $47.0\%$
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Molar mass of $\text{PbS} = 207 + 32 = 239\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. Moles of $\text{PbS} = 5.02 / 239 = 0.0210\ \text{mol}$. From the stoichiometry, $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{PbS}$ corresponds to $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{H}_2\text{S}$. Volume of $\text{H}_2\text{S} = 0.0210 \times 22.4 = 0.4704\ \text{dm}^3$. Percentage by volume $= (0.4704 / 10.0) \times 100 = 4.70\%$.

B) lustre

EXPLANATION: In diamond, each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four other carbon atoms in a rigid, three-dimensional tetrahedral network. This strong covalent framework gives diamond its extreme hardness, making it ideal for abrasive and cutting tools.

Q: Which petroleum fraction is collected over the boiling range of $40\ ^\circ\text{C}$ to $70\ ^\circ\text{C}$?
A) Petroleum ether
B) Kerosene
C) Gas oil
D) Lubricating oil
ANSWER: A

Q: The industrial process for converting ammonia to trioxonitrate(V) acid is the
A) Sabatier process
B) Ostwald process
C) Contact process
D) Haber process
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: The Ostwald process oxidizes ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) over a platinum-rhodium catalyst at high temperature to form nitric oxide ($\text{NO}$), which is further oxidized to $\text{NO}_2$ and absorbed in water to yield trioxonitrate(V) acid ($\text{HNO}_3$). The Haber process produces ammonia, and the Contact process produces sulphuric acid.

Q: The densities of two gases A and B are $0.25\ \text{g dm}^{-3}$ and $1.0\ \text{g dm}^{-3}$ respectively. What is the rate of diffusion of A relative to B?
A) 1:2
B) 2:1
C) 1:3
D) 3:1
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: By Graham's Law of Diffusion, $R_A / R_B = \sqrt{d_B / d_A} = \sqrt{1.0 / 0.25} = \sqrt{4} = 2$. Thus, gas A diffuses twice as fast as gas B, giving a ratio of 2:1.

Q: Which of the following parameters decreases as steam condenses to water?
A) Number of molecules
B) Temperature of the system
C) Activation energy
D) Entropy of the system
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Steam (gas) has higher molecular disorder than liquid water. Condensation organizes the molecules into a more structured liquid phase, leading to a decrease in the entropy ($\Delta S < 0$) of the system.

Q: If the free energy change ($\Delta G$) of a reaction is $-899\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$ and the entropy change ($\Delta S$) is $+10\ \text{J K}^{-1}\text{mol}^{-1}$ at $25\ ^\circ\text{C}$, calculate the enthalpy change ($\Delta H$).
A) $-2,081\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$
B) $-6,490\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$
C) $+6,490\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$
D) $+2,081\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Temperature $T = 25 + 273.15 = 298.15\ \text{K}$. Using $\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S$:
$$-899 = \Delta H - (298.15 \times 10)$$
$$-899 = \Delta H - 2981.5$$
$$\Delta H = -899 + 2981.5 = +2082.5\ \text{J mol}^{-1} \approx +2081\ \text{J mol}^{-1}$$

Q: Which of the following elements undergoes a net mass increase when heated strongly in open air?
A) Carbon
B) Sulphur
C) Magnesium
D) Ethanol
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: When magnesium is heated in air, it reacts with oxygen to form solid magnesium oxide: $2\text{Mg}(s) + \text{O}_2(g) \rightarrow 2\text{MgO}(s)$. Carbon, sulphur, and ethanol produce gaseous products ($\text{CO}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$) that escape into the atmosphere.

Q: An oxide of formula $\text{XO}_2$ has a vapour density of 32. What is the atomic mass of element X?
A) 32
B) 20
C) 16
D) 14
ANSWER: A
EXPLANATION: Relative molecular mass $= 2 \times \text{vapour density} = 2 \times 32 = 64\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. For $\text{XO}_2$: $X + 2(16) = 64 \implies X + 32 = 64 \implies X = 32$.

Q: When ethene gas mixed with oxygen is passed over a silver catalyst at about $250\ ^\circ\text{C}$, the main product is
A) ethanol
B) ethane-1,2-diol
C) epoxyethane
D) ethanoic acid
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: The catalytic oxidation of ethene with air/oxygen over a silver catalyst at $250\ ^\circ\text{C} - 300\ ^\circ\text{C}$ yields epoxyethane (ethylene oxide): $2\text{C}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{O}_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Ag}} 2\text{C}_2\text{H}_4\text{O}$.

Q: How many unpaired electrons are present in the cation $\text{X}^+$ where the atomic number of X is 16?
A) 1
B) 3
C) 4
D) 2
ANSWER: B
EXPLANATION: Neutral atom $X$ ($Z = 16$, Sulphur) has ground-state electronic configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^4$. Removing one electron to form $\text{X}^+$ gives the configuration $1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^3$. According to Hund's rule, the 3 sub-shell electrons in $3p^3$ occupy separate $p$-orbitals with parallel spins ($3p_x^1 3p_y^1 3p_z^1$), giving 3 unpaired electrons.

Q: Magnesium ($\text{Mg}$) has a smaller atomic radius than Calcium ($\text{Ca}$) because
A) nuclear charge decreases across the period
B) valence electrons in Mg occupy a higher principal energy level
C) Mg has fewer electron shells than Ca
D) metallic bonding is weaker in Ca
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Magnesium ($Z = 12$) has 3 occupied energy shells ($2, 8, 2$), whereas calcium ($Z = 20$) has 4 occupied energy shells ($2, 8, 8, 2$). Atomic radius increases down a group due to the addition of principal electron shells. Thus, $\text{Mg}$ is smaller than $\text{Ca}$.

Q: If $10.8\ \text{g}$ of silver is deposited in a silver coulometer connected in series with an acidulated water coulometer, what volume of oxygen gas (measured at s.t.p.) is liberated at the anode? [$\text{Ag} = 108$; $\text{Molar gas volume at s.t.p.} = 22.4\ \text{dm}^3\ \text{mol}^{-1}$]
A) $5.60\ \text{dm}^3$
B) $2.24\ \text{dm}^3$
C) $1.12\ \text{dm}^3$
D) $0.56\ \text{dm}^3$
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Moles of $\text{Ag}$ deposited $= 10.8 / 108 = 0.10\ \text{mol}$. Cathode half-reaction: $\text{Ag}^+ + e^- \rightarrow \text{Ag}$. Thus, $0.10\ \text{mol}$ of electrons (or $0.10\ \text{F}$) passed through the circuit. Anode half-reaction for water electrolysis: $2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{O}_2 + 4\text{H}^+ + 4e^-$. $4\ \text{moles}$ of electrons liberate $1\ \text{mol}$ of $\text{O}_2$. Moles of $\text{O}_2 = 0.10 / 4 = 0.025\ \text{mol}$. Volume at s.t.p. $= 0.025 \times 22.4 = 0.56\ \text{dm}^3$.

Q: A sample of $6.02\ \text{g}$ of hydrated washing soda, $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot y\text{H}_2\text{O}$, was dissolved in water to make $500\ \text{cm}^3$ of solution. A $25\ \text{cm}^3$ portion of this solution required $32.10\ \text{cm}^3$ of $0.097\ \text{mol dm}^{-3}\ \text{HCl}$ for complete neutralization. Find the value of $y$. [$\text{Na} = 23, \text{H} = 1, \text{C} = 12, \text{O} = 16$]
A) 6
B) 9
C) 5
D) 10
ANSWER: C
EXPLANATION: Equation: $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + 2\text{HCl} \rightarrow 2\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2$.
Moles of $\text{HCl}$ used $= 0.097 \times (32.10 / 1000) = 0.0031137\ \text{mol}$.
Moles of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ in $25\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.0031137 / 2 = 0.0015568\ \text{mol}$.
Moles of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3$ in $500\ \text{cm}^3 = 0.0015568 \times (500 / 25) = 0.031137\ \text{mol}$.
Molar mass of $\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3\cdot y\text{H}_2\text{O} = 6.02 / 0.031137 = 193.34\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$.
Formula mass: $106 + 18y = 193.34 \implies 18y = 87.34 \implies y \approx 4.85 \approx 5$.

Q: What mass of bromine gas is produced at the anode during the electrolysis of molten lead(II) bromide ($\text{PbBr}_2$) when $10.35\ \text{g}$ of lead metal is deposited at the cathode? [$\text{Pb} = 207, \text{Br} = 80$]
A) $4.00\ \text{g}$
B) $7.00\ \text{g}$
C) $4.50\ \text{g}$
D) $8.00\ \text{g}$
ANSWER: D
EXPLANATION: Electrolysis equation: $\text{PbBr}_2(l) \rightarrow \text{Pb}(l) + \text{Br}_2(g)$.
Moles of $\text{Pb} = 10.35 / 207 = 0.05\ \text{mol}$.
From the 1:1 stoichiometry of $\text{Pb}$ and $\text{Br}_2$, moles of $\text{Br}_2$ produced $= 0.05\ \text{mol}$.
Mass of $\text{Br}_2 = 0.05 \times (2 \times 80) = 0.05 \times 160 = 8.00\ \text{g}$.
